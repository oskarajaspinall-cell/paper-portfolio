# Macro overlay: NVR, Inc. (NVR) — 2026-10-09

## Assessment
NVR is a homebuilder with no owned-land leverage, so its thesis runs through one macro variable: long rates. Chain: **10y Treasury yield (mortgage-rate proxy) → mortgage rates/affordability → orders, closings, ASP and margin → revenue; and the same 10y feeds NVR's own DCF discount rate → fair value** [Likely] **(inference)**.

Facts: the 10y Treasury yield (DGS10) is 5.28% (2026-10-07), up +0.50pp in ~1m and +0.72pp in ~3m; the 10y real yield (DFII10) is 2.92%, up +0.49pp/+0.61pp over the same windows; 10y breakeven inflation (T10YIE) is roughly flat at 2.35% (-0.02pp/+0.12pp) (fred.stlouisfed.org, 2026-10-07/08). So almost all of the nominal-yield rise is a *real*-rate move, not an inflation scare [Certain]. Fed funds effective (DFF) rose +0.25pp in 1m to 3.88%, while the 2y yield (4.77%) sits ~0.89pp above it — market pricing (proxy, not fact) implies no near-term cuts, possibly further tightening [Likely] **(inference)**.

This is a genuine surprise versus a housing-bear narrative that had assumed rate relief: real yields are still rising, not falling, which should keep mortgage rates elevated longer than priced when NVR's stock fell 24% over 12 months [Likely] **(inference)**. Yet NVR's multiples remain at the 86th percentile of its own 5y range and above peers — valuation hasn't caught down to the renewed rate pressure.

**Bull**: yields roll over (Fed pivots/term premium fades) → affordability improves → orders/margin stabilize, multiple re-rates up. **Bear**: real yields keep rising → affordability worsens further, margin compression continues, and the DCF discount rate (already using 5.28%) pushes fair value lower.
