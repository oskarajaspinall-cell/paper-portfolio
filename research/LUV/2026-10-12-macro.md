# Macro overlay: Southwest Airlines Co. (LUV) — 2026-10-12
## Assessment
Chain: real yields & credit spreads -> cost of equity/debt -> LUV's own CAPM-based fair value and its ability to keep debt-funding buybacks on negative FCF **(inference)**. LUV's fact-sheet fair value discounts cash flows at the 10y Treasury (5.22%, FRED DGS10, 2026-10-08) plus beta x ERP [Certain, factsheet]. Facts (FRED, 2026-10-08/09): 10y real yield (DFII10) 2.87%, +0.56pp/3m; 10y nominal (DGS10) 5.22%, +0.68pp/3m; HY credit spread (BAMLH0A0HYM2) 3.15%, +0.45pp/3m; breakeven inflation (T10YIE) 2.33%, roughly flat (-0.04pp/1m). Market pricing (proxy, **inference**): 2y yield (4.75%) sitting 0.87pp above fed funds (3.88%, FRED DFF) implies the market still prices a higher-for-longer stance, not imminent cuts; flat breakevens suggest the yield rise is real-rate/term-premium driven, not an inflation surprise.

This hurts, not helps: LUV already shows P/B 119% of its 5y range and P/E 97% above peers, base fair value only +2.1% above the $41.66 close vs -31.7% bear case [Certain, factsheet], FCF margin -1.4% TTM, and net debt/EBITDA rising to 1.3-1.6x funding buybacks [Certain, factsheet]. Rising real yields/spreads mechanically compress the DCF/EV-EBITDA fair value and raise funding cost for the $14.8bn 2026-2031 aircraft order book [Certain, note].

**Bull**: real yields and HY spreads reverse -> lower discount rate, fair value lifts above the base case, cheaper funding for capex. **Bear**: both keep rising -> fair value falls below price, debt-funded buybacks harder to sustain, discretionary demand softens. **Tilt**: toward bear, moderate — macro reinforces an already fragile valuation case.
