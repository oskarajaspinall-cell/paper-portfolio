# Southwest Airlines Co. (LUV) — Core initiation — 2026-10-12

## 1. Business
Southwest is a U.S. low-cost, point-to-point passenger airline flying an all-Boeing-737 fleet, earning revenue from fares plus ancillary and loyalty/co-brand income; it has no long-haul international or cargo operation today [Certain] (source: stockanalysis.com/stocks/luv/). It is weighing a Boeing 787 widebody order to start long-haul international flying [Likely] (source: https://www.reuters.com/business/aerospace-defense/southwest-airlines-weighs-boeing-787-long-haul-international-push-sources-say-2026-10-08/, cited in fact sheet news).

## 2. Quality
Returns are weak: ROIC TTM 8.5% has risen off a low base (-11.1% in FY2021) but ROE is falling (-5.3pp over 5y to 4.8%/11.1% TTM, flattered by buybacks) [Certain] (source: factsheet Quality table, RA). Net margin is thin (2.8% TTM) and FCF margin has been negative every year since FY2022 (-1.4% TTM), with FCF/NI conversion deeply negative (-48.5% TTM, as low as -342% FY2024) [Certain] (source: factsheet, CF/IS). A single-fleet, domestic, low-cost model is easily matched by ULCCs and majors on price; there is no evidence of a durable cost or scale edge versus peers, and net debt/EBITDA has swung from net cash (FY2022-24) back to 1.3-1.6x as buybacks continued despite negative free cash flow [Certain] (source: factsheet, BS/IS). A move into widebody international flying would add capital intensity and execution risk outside its historical model [Likely] (source: Reuters, as above). No visible moat protects returns above cost of capital.

## 3. Valuation
The market is paying up for a cyclical recovery that hasn't reached cash generation. P/E of 26.1x is 97% above the peer median (13.2x; DAL/UAL/AAL/ALK), though near the low end of LUV's own 5y range (distorted by depressed earnings). More tellingly, P/B of 2.9x is above LUV's entire 5y range (119%) and 38% above peers, while FCF yield is negative (-1.99%), so no yield-based support exists [Certain] (source: factsheet Valuation table). EV/EBITDA (10.0x) is roughly in line with peers (+1%) and mid-range of its own history (39%). The fact sheet's own-history fair value (best method: EV/EBITDA; through-cycle DCF unusable given negative/short FCF history) puts base fair value at $42.52 vs a $41.66 close — only +2.1% upside — against a bear case of -31.7% and bull case of +13.0% [Certain] (source: factsheet Fair value table). Risk/reward skews to the downside with no margin of safety in the base case.

## 4. What would prove this wrong
- ROIC (ratios page) stays at or above the current 8.5% TTM for two further consecutive quarters, confirming the improvement is structural rather than a cyclical fuel/demand bounce.
- FCF margin (cash flow statement vs income statement) turns and holds positive for two consecutive quarters, ending the negative-FCF pattern seen every year since FY2022.
- Net debt/EBITDA (ratios page) falls back toward net cash rather than continuing to rise from the -0.40x (FY2024) it held before FY2025's 1.60x, showing buybacks are no longer debt-funded.

## 5. Capital allocation and cash conversion
Southwest repurchased 28m shares for $1.25bn in Q1 2026 and paid $176m of dividends ($0.18/share quarterly) in H1 2026, cutting shares outstanding -13.0% YoY [Certain] (source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/92380/000009238026000077/luv-20260630.htm, 10-Q filed 2026-07-23). But TTM FCF margin is -1.4% and FCF/NI conversion -48.5% [Certain] (source: factsheet, CF/IS), so capital returns are being funded partly by the balance sheet, consistent with net debt/EBITDA rising from net cash to 1.3-1.6x. The firm order book commits roughly $14.8bn through 2031 for 617 aircraft, locking in heavy ongoing capex regardless of the cycle [Certain] (source: 10-Q, as above).

## 6. Unusual items
Southwest terminated its fuel-hedging program in 2025, per the Q2 2026 10-Q, raising its exposure to fuel-price swings with no derivative offset [Certain] (source: 10-Q, as above). The Oct 9, 2026 IT outage was an industry-wide air-traffic/IT backbone failure affecting multiple carriers, not Southwest-specific, and is not treated as material [Likely] (source: https://nypost.com/2026/10/09/business/it-woes-hit-southwest-airlines-causing-long-lines-and-forcing-staff-to-check-passengers-in-old-school/). Short interest is modest (4.56% of float) and falling (-11.9% m/m); not material.
