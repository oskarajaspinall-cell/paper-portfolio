# The Procter & Gamble Company (PG) — Core initiation — 2026-10-09
## 1. Business
P&G sells branded household and personal-care staples (Fabric & Home Care, Baby/Feminine/Family Care, Beauty, Health Care, Grooming) through mass retail and e-commerce worldwide, generating $87.0bn FY2026 net sales (+3%) [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/pg/, https://www.pginvestor.com/). Revenue is driven by repeat purchases of category-leading brands (Tide, Pampers, Gillette, Olay) priced at a premium to private label, monetized via volume, price/mix and productivity rather than large discrete contracts.

## 2. Quality
ROIC 21.5% TTM, ROCE 24.6% (rising +2.7pp over 5y) and ROE 30.3% show durable excess returns on capital [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/pg/financials/ratios/). Gross margin 50.9% and operating margin 24.8% are both up vs FY2022, with FCF conversion of 94.4% of net income [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/pg/financials/income-statement/, https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/pg/financials/cash-flow-statement/). The moat rests on brand equity in categories where performance is trusted, R&D/supply-chain scale and retailer shelf-space power — advantages that have held for two decades [Likely]. Leverage is low (net debt/EBITDA 1.01x) [Certain]. Risk to durability: management flagged ~$1bn after-tax commodity/tariff cost headwinds building (Brent now $102.50 vs the $90 base assumed), and described pricing as now "defensive" rather than a growth lever after losing ground in diapers/oral care [Likely] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/pg/transcripts/745219-barclays-19th-annual-global-consumer-conference/). Organic growth is modest (FY2026 +1%); China is the standout (+3-5% for five straight quarters), offsetting a slower US/Europe [Certain] (source: https://www.pginvestor.com/, transcript above).

## 3. Valuation
At 150.59 vs fair value base of 112.01 (-25.6%) and even the bull case of 122.80 (-18.5%), the market prices in growth the business is not delivering [Certain] (source: factsheet "Fair value" table). The reverse DCF implies 14% year-1 revenue growth fading to terminal — far above FY2026 organic growth of +1% and management's own "defensive pricing" framing [Likely]. Within its own 5-year range PG screens cheap on EV/EBITDA (10th percentile), EV/Sales (12th) and P/FCF (14th), but that range may itself reflect a multi-year staples re-rating, so "cheap vs history" is not cheap in absolute terms [Guessing]. Vs peers (KMB, CL, UL, CHD), PG carries a premium on EV/Sales (+33%) and P/FCF (+28%) despite a lower P/E (-5%), and FCF yield is just 4.33% [Certain] (source: factsheet "Valuation" table). Net: price assumes stronger growth and margin resilience than current trends support.

## 4. What would prove this wrong
- Gross margin (income statement page) falls below 48% (below the FY2022 level), showing commodity/tariff costs are not being offset by productivity.
- Organic/net sales growth (income statement page) stays at or below +1% for two more fiscal years, confirming a structural slowdown rather than a trough.
- ROIC (ratios page) falls below 18% for two consecutive fiscal years, signaling the moat is eroding.
- FCF conversion, FCF/NI (cash-flow and income statement pages), falls below 80% for two consecutive years, signaling deteriorating cash quality.

## 5. Capital allocation and cash conversion
FCF margin of 17.4% TTM and FCF conversion of 94.4% show high-quality earnings converting to cash [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/pg/financials/cash-flow-statement/). Shares outstanding fell 1.3% YoY, indicating continued buybacks [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/pg/statistics/). FY2026 operating cash flow of $19.6bn, as disclosed on P&G's IR site, implies ample coverage of capex, dividends and buybacks [Certain] (source: https://www.pginvestor.com/). Net debt/EBITDA of 1.01x leaves balance-sheet room for continued buybacks without releveraging risk [Likely].

None.
