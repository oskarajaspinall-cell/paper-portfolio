# China Life Insurance Company Limited (HKG:2628) — Core initiation — 2026-10-07

## 1. Business
China Life is China's largest life insurer, state-controlled and Beijing-headquartered [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/quote/hkg/2628/). It underwrites individual and group life, annuity, health and accident policies nationwide through tied agents and bancassurance [Certain] (same source). Premiums collected upfront fund a large investment book (government bonds, equities, deposits); profit is the spread between investment returns and policy liabilities plus an underwriting margin, so earnings are leveraged to China's bond yields and A-share moves [Likely].

## 2. Quality
Scale and state ownership give China Life a distribution and regulatory-relationship moat competitors cannot easily replicate [Likely]. ROE has risen from 11.1% (FY2021) to 27.7% (FY2025) and 41.3% TTM; ROIC from 7.0% to 19.2% (FY2025) and 28.3% TTM (source: https://stockanalysis.com/quote/hkg/2628/financials/ratios/) [Certain]. But this spike is almost certainly driven by investment/mark-to-market gains in a strong equity period rather than a structural improvement in underwriting economics, so the TTM level is not representative of a sustainable run-rate [Likely]. Leverage is low and falling: net debt/EBITDA (site definition) 4.48x→0.38x TTM over the same period (same source) [Certain]. A September 2026 capital injection of roughly 360bn yuan into China's largest state-owned insurers and banks underlines implicit government backing for this class of institution, a quality support factor specific to state insurers [Likely] (source: https://www.scmp.com/business/banking-finance/article/3366559/china-rolls-out-massive-us54b-package-insurers-banks-financial-powerhouse-push, as listed on the fact sheet; article not independently fetched).

## 3. Valuation
The market is pricing China Life well below both its own history and peers on earnings multiples: P/E 2.7x vs a 5-year range of 7.3x–13.9x (currently 71% below the 5-year minimum) and a peer median of 4.8x (-43%); EV/EBITDA 3.4x vs peer median 5.7x (-39%), also below its own 5-year minimum (source: https://stockanalysis.com/quote/hkg/2628/financials/ratios/, peer statistics pages) [Certain]. P/B is 1.4x, at its own 5-year low but 36% above the peer median of 1.0x, while P/FCF (2.2x) is 47% above peers. The combination — cheap on earnings, rich on book value/FCF versus peers — is consistent with the market discounting the quality of the current earnings spike (investment gains) rather than discounting the franchise itself [Likely]. The share is down 5.0%/6.7% versus its 50-/200-day averages despite a +23.9% 12-month gain, suggesting some of that scepticism is already playing out in price [Likely].

## 4. What would prove this wrong
- ROIC (ratios page) falls back below 10%, erasing the FY2021–FY2025 improvement trend.
- ROE (ratios page) falls below its FY2021-FY2023 average (~14%) for two consecutive reporting periods, confirming the TTM spike was not durable and underwriting/investment economics have not actually improved.
- Net debt/EBITDA, site definition (ratios/statistics page) rises back above 2x, reversing the deleveraging trend.
- FCF conversion (FCF/NI, cash flow and income statement pages) turns negative, indicating cash earnings are not tracking reported profit.

## 5. Capital allocation and cash conversion
Shares outstanding are flat (0.00% YoY): no dilution, no buyback distorting per-share metrics (source: https://stockanalysis.com/quote/hkg/2628/statistics/) [Certain]. FCF conversion (FCF/net income) stayed above 100% every year shown, falling from 553% (FY2021) to 177.7% TTM (source: https://stockanalysis.com/quote/hkg/2628/financials/cash-flow-statement/, income-statement) [Certain]. For an insurer this ratio is heavily shaped by float/investment timing, not a clean cash-conversion signal, so read it directionally [Likely]. Net debt/EBITDA (own calculation) has fallen from 5.52x to 0.83x TTM, consistent with a de-risking balance sheet rather than one taking on leverage (same sources) [Certain].

## 6. Unusual items
Beijing injected roughly 360bn yuan ($54bn) of capital into China's largest state-owned insurers and banks in a package announced 2026-09-06, as reported by SCMP [Likely] (source: https://www.scmp.com/business/banking-finance/article/3366559/china-rolls-out-massive-us54b-package-insurers-banks-financial-powerhouse-push, headline as listed on the fact sheet). This is a sector-wide, not company-specific, action; it is not confirmed from the fact sheet whether China Life itself was a direct recipient, but as one of China's largest state-owned life insurers it is a plausible beneficiary or at minimum a signal of policy support for the sector [Guessing]. Institutional ownership is modest at 16.1% and short-interest data is unavailable (source: https://stockanalysis.com/quote/hkg/2628/statistics/).
