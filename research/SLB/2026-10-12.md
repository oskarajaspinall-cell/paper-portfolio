# SLB N.V. (SLB) — Core initiation — 2026-10-12

## 1. Business
SLB is the world's largest oilfield-services company, providing drilling, reservoir, well-construction and production equipment/services to E&P operators globally, with revenue largely tied to customer upstream capex [Certain]. In August 2026 it agreed to acquire thermal-management firm Kelvion for ~$3.4bn cash (~$4.1bn including ~$0.7bn assumed debt, ~11x 2026E EBITDA pre-synergies) to build a data-center cooling business, diversifying beyond oil & gas (source: SEC 8-K exhibit, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/87347/000119312526375358/d106278dex99.htm) [Certain].

## 2. Quality
ROIC rose from 8.3% (FY21) to a peak 17.3% (FY24) during the drilling upcycle, but has since fallen to 13.8% (FY25) and 11.6% TTM; ROCE and ROE show the same rise-then-fade path (fact sheet Quality table) [Certain]. Operating margin peaked at 17.6% (FY24) and eased to 14.2% TTM; FCF margin (12.0% TTM vs 13.2% FY21) and FCF/NI conversion are also drifting lower, though volatile [Certain]. Net debt/EBITDA, which fell to a 0.91x FY2024 low, ticked back up to 1.18x TTM, consistent with debt-funded M&A rather than continued deleveraging [Likely]. Scale, an integrated technology stack and long-cycle contract wins (15-year TotalEnergies DrillPlan deal, Aramco well-construction awards — fact sheet News) support durable share in a concentrated oilfield-services oligopoly [Likely], but returns are clearly cyclical with oil & gas activity, and the new, unrelated data-center-cooling push adds integration risk on top [Guessing].

## 3. Valuation
At $48.95, SLB trades on 23.7x TTM P/E — above the top of its own 5-year range (12.1x–22.3x, 114% of range) and 12% above the peer median 21.3x (HAL, BKR, NOV, FTI) [Certain]. EV/EBITDA (11.0x) and P/B (2.8x) sit mid-range historically but still 13%/5% above peer medians; P/FCF (16.6x) is in line with peers [Certain]. Base fair value of $49.50 (DCF/EV-EBITDA blend) is only 1.1% above last close — priced to fair value with no margin of safety [Certain]; the bear case ($20.84, -57.4%) reflects a cyclical downturn in oilfield spending, the bull case ($65.85, +34.5%) needs sustained activity plus Kelvion delivering [Likely]. The reverse DCF implies ~6.4% year-1 revenue growth fading to terminal — not conservative given FY2025 revenue and margins already rolled over from their FY2024 peak [Likely]. Not cheap on its own history, and no discount to peers on the multiples that matter most [Certain].

## 4. What would prove this wrong
- ROIC (ratios page) stays below 10% for two consecutive fiscal years, confirming the post-2024 returns decline is structural, not cyclical.
- Net debt/EBITDA (ratios/balance sheet pages) rises above 2.0x, showing the Kelvion deal and further M&A are eroding balance-sheet discipline.
- FCF margin (income statement/cash flow pages) stays below 8% for two consecutive fiscal years, showing cash conversion has structurally worsened.
- Kelvion/data-center segment revenue (future filings) fails to reach management's stated run-rate, showing the diversification is destroying value.

## 5. Capital allocation and cash conversion
Shares outstanding rose 7.6% YoY (fact sheet Other table) — net dilution despite any stated buyback, likely from stock-based comp and/or M&A financing rather than disciplined repurchases [Likely]. FCF conversion remains high in absolute terms (141% TTM) but has been volatile (44% in FY2022) and is now funding a debt-financed, non-core acquisition: net debt/EBITDA rose to 1.18x TTM from the 0.91x FY2024 low [Certain]. The ~$3.4bn cash Kelvion purchase (plus ~$0.7bn assumed debt) is being funded primarily with cash/debt, not new equity, per the SEC 8-K [Certain], closing expected H1 2027 [Certain] (source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/87347/000119312526375358/d106278dex99.htm).

## 6. Unusual items
Diversification/execution risk: SLB is paying ~$3.4bn cash (~$4.1bn incl. debt) for Kelvion, a thermal-management company, to enter data-center cooling, an end-market and competitive set unrelated to its oilfield-services core [Certain] (source: SEC 8-K exhibit, 2026-08-31, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/87347/000119312526375358/d106278dex99.htm). This is a capital-allocation and integration risk rather than a near-term catalyst [Likely]. Short interest is modest (4.07% of float) and institutional ownership high (92.1%, both ST); nothing else unusual.
