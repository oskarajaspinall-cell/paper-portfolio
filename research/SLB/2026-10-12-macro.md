# Macro overlay: SLB N.V. (SLB) — 2026-10-12
## Assessment
SLB's earnings are driven primarily by the global upstream capex cycle (oil price, E&P budgets) — not a FRED-tracked series — so that channel sits outside this overlay [Certain]. The macro lever that mechanically touches the thesis is the discount rate in the fact sheet's own DCF: risk-free = 10y Treasury + 0.84 beta × 5% ERP [Certain]. **Facts (FRED, 2026-10-08/09):** 10y real yield (DFII10) 2.87%, +0.44pp/1m, +0.56pp/3m; 10y nominal (DGS10) 5.22%, +0.68pp/3m; 10y breakeven (T10YIE) 2.33%, roughly flat; high-yield spread (BAMLH0A0HYM2) 3.15%, +0.45pp/3m (fred.stlouisfed.org, id=DFII10/DGS10/T10YIE/BAMLH0A0HYM2). Flat breakevens alongside similar real/nominal moves mean the yield rise is a real-rate, not inflation-fear, story [Likely] **(inference)**. **Market pricing:** the 2y yield (4.75%) sitting ~0.9pp above effective fed funds (3.88%, DFF) proxies markets expecting no near-term cuts, possibly further tightening [Likely] **(inference)**. SLB's base fair value ($49.50) is already only +1.1% above last close ($48.95), zero margin of safety: a further ~50bp rate rise would push base fair value below price; a reversal adds headroom [Likely]. Wider HY spreads modestly raise funding costs for leveraged E&P customers, a small drag on capex [Guessing].
**Macro bull:** real yields fall/stabilize, spreads tighten → discount rate eases → fair value band widens. **Macro bear:** real yields keep rising, spreads widen → discount rate up → base fair value falls below price, removing the valuation case. **Tilt:** toward bear, small — the move compresses an already-at-parity valuation; the dominant earnings driver (oil capex) is unaffected.
