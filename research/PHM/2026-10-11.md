# PulteGroup, Inc. (PHM) — Core initiation — 2026-10-11

## 1. Business
PulteGroup is the US's third-largest homebuilder (Pulte, Centex, Del Webb, DiVosta brands), earning ~98% of revenue from home closings — 29,572 homes in FY2025 at ~$566k average price [Certain] (source: 10-K, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/822416/000082241626000007/phm-20251231.htm). It controls land mainly via lot options (133,528 of 234,632 total lots) rather than owning it outright, limiting capital tied up in a downturn [Certain] (same source). Pulte Mortgage (~2% of revenue) originates loans for 64% of closings, then sells them into the secondary market [Certain] (same source).

## 2. Quality
PHM is well run but strongly cyclical, not a durable-moat compounder: ROIC fell from 25.0% (FY2021) to 14.5% (TTM) and ROE from 27.7% to 14.9% as margins cooled with mortgage rates that "have remained elevated" [Certain] (fact sheet; source: 10-K). Its edge is scale, not pricing power: management cites cheaper capital-markets financing, larger land positions, better labor access and diversification versus local builders [Likely] (source: 10-K) — advantages that reduce relative risk but do not insulate PHM from the cycle itself, which caused "significant losses" industry-wide in 2006-2011 (source: 10-K). Piotroski F-score is a weak 4/9, and net margin has fallen to 11.6% TTM from 17.2% in FY2024 [Certain] (fact sheet). The real strength is balance-sheet discipline: net debt/EBITDA of 0.42x and 79.3% FCF/NI conversion TTM give room to keep buying back stock through a deeper downturn [Certain] (fact sheet).

## 3. Valuation
PHM trades on 11.4x TTM P/E and 8.0x EV/EBITDA — 16% and 22% below the peer median (DHI, LEN, NVR, TOL) — but both sit above PHM's own 5-year range (118% of the 5y max each), since trailing earnings have fallen faster than the price [Certain] (fact sheet). P/FCF (13.9x) and P/B (1.6x) are near peer medians and mid-range historically. The stockanalysis.com DCF puts base fair value at $89.03, 20.6% below the $112.12 last close, implying the market is paying up for a cyclical trough not yet fully reflected in earnings [Likely] (fact sheet). The reverse-DCF shows the price implies 6.8% year-1 revenue growth fading to terminal growth — optimistic given -12.2% 12-month momentum and -27.8pp relative strength vs SPY [Certain] (fact sheet). Cheap versus peers, not versus its own cycle or a DCF: no margin of safety for a late-cycle homebuilder [Likely].

## 4. What would prove this wrong
- Net debt/EBITDA (ratios page) rises above 1.0x — the balance-sheet buffer supporting the valuation discount erodes.
- ROIC (ratios page) falls below 10% for two consecutive fiscal years — returns drop below a level consistent with a scale/cost advantage above cost of capital.
- Gross margin (income statement) falls below 24% — incentive-driven margin erosion exceeds what the current price discount already assumes.
- FCF conversion, FCF/NI (cash flow & income statement) falls below 50% — cash generation funding buybacks and the dividend weakens materially.

## 5. Capital allocation and cash conversion
PHM returns cash mainly via buybacks: shares outstanding fell 5.0% YoY [Certain] (fact sheet), funded by a 9.2% TTM FCF margin and 79.3% FCF/NI conversion [Certain] (fact sheet). Leverage is low (net debt/EBITDA 0.42x) [Certain] (fact sheet). The 10-K states capital priorities are to "invest in the business, fund our dividend, and routinely return excess funds to shareholders through share repurchases, while maintaining a modest leverage profile" (source: 10-K). The quarterly dividend was raised to $0.26/share, paid October 2, 2026 (source: https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260902907056/en/PulteGroup-Announces-Quarterly-Cash-Dividend-of-0.26-Per-Share/) [Certain]. 57% of the 234,632-lot pipeline is optioned, reducing capital at risk if demand slows [Certain] (source: 10-K).

## 6. Unusual items
Short interest is a modest 4.75% of float but rose 4.3% month-on-month [Certain] (fact sheet) — not a crowded short, but a signal some investors are positioning for further housing-cycle disappointment ahead of the October 22, 2026 Q3 print. Institutional ownership is very high (97.8%), typical for a large-cap homebuilder and not a governance flag [Certain] (fact sheet).
