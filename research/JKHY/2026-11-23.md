# Jack Henry & Associates (JKHY) — Core initiation — 2026-11-23
## 1. Business
Core banking, payments and digital software for US community banks and credit unions. Revenue is recurring: services and support (cloud hosting, on-premise support, licences and implementation) and processing (card, remittance, faster payments, digital). Contracts are multi-year and switching cores is costly. Customers are US institutions; no material foreign revenue mix identified [Likely] (source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/779152/000077915226000098/jkhy-20260930.htm).

## 2. Quality
High-quality, sticky franchise [Likely]. TTM ROIC is 23.8% and ROCE 24.7%, with operating margin at 25.6% and FCF margin at 27.6% (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/jkhy/financials/ratios/). Net debt/EBITDA is 0.13x. The moat is switching costs and an entrenched installed base [Likely].

Direction change: ROIC fell over five fiscal years (-1.3pp) but is rising over the last 12 months, from 21.8% to 23.8% (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/jkhy/financials/ratios/?p=trailing). I read this as recovery from the FY2024 trough of 19.1%, not a new decline [Likely].

The Q1 FY27 10-Q shows processing revenue growth of 10.7% [company-reported] (source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/779152/000077915226000098/jkhy-20260930.htm). Part of services growth is lumpy deconversion revenue. R&D is up 16.6% [company-reported] (same URL), and management cites cyber, AI and infrastructure spend as a margin headwind [Likely] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/jkhy/transcripts/736394-q1-2027/).

## 3. Valuation
The price is 171.32 USD, 11.3% above its 200-day average. P/E is 23.5x against a 5y median of 31.7x (24th percentile of its range). EV/EBITDA is 16.5x (23rd percentile) and P/FCF 16.6x (12th percentile). FCF yield is 6.01%. Multiples sit about 80% above the peer median (FISV, FIS, GPN, PAYX), a weak comparison given those peers' lower quality [Likely].

The mechanical fair value says expensive: base 125.12 (-27.0% vs the close), bull 137.26, so the price is above even the bull case. The reverse DCF implies 12.4% year-1 revenue growth, above recent reported growth [Likely]. The P/E method (230.66) conflicts with the DCF. The DCF may be harsh on a high-margin compounder [Guessing]. Cheap on its own history, expensive on cash flows; the price assumes strong execution continues.

## 4. What would prove this wrong
- ROIC (ratios page, TTM) falls below 19%.
- Operating margin (income statement, TTM) falls below 23%.
- FCF margin (cash flow statement, TTM) falls below 22%.
- Organic revenue growth (10-Q MD&A, ex-deconversion) turns negative, or core-contract losses are disclosed.

## 5. Capital allocation and cash conversion
FCF conversion is 138.2% of net income, so earnings are cash-backed. The share count is down 2.22% YoY, so buybacks are net-shrinking the base. Leverage is minimal. Acquisitions are small (the 10-Q mentions a company acquired in the prior fiscal year) [company-reported] (https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/779152/000077915226000098/jkhy-20260930.htm). Disciplined [Likely].
