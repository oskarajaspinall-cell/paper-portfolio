# Dropbox (DBX) — Core initiation — 2026-11-16
## 1. Business
Subscription file-sync, collaboration and AI search (Dash) sold to individuals, families and teams. Revenue is overwhelmingly self-serve: "more than 90%" through self-serve channels, with 18.20 million paying users [company-reported] (source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1467623/000146762326000061/dbx-20260930.htm). Country revenue split: [data unavailable] in the sources read; no single foreign country is confirmed at ~10%+. [Certain]

## 2. Quality
Highly cash generative: operating margin 26.6% and FCF margin 36.1% TTM, gross margin 79.9% (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/dbx/financials/income-statement/). [Certain] ROIC is 33.7% TTM, but fell from 64.9% a year earlier (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/dbx/financials/ratios/?p=trailing). The fall is mostly the denominator: the lower-leverage base was replaced by debt-funded buybacks (shares down 18.02% YoY). [Likely] Margins are flat over the year, so the business itself is stable, not eroding. [Certain]
The moat is habit and switching cost in a mature base, not growth. The 10-Q adds risk language that growth "has declined" and that paying users may fluctuate or decline, and the Q3 call headline points to flat preliminary FY2027 revenue (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/dbx/). [Likely] The moat protects returns, not growth. Altman Z 0.53 and Piotroski 5 are weak. [Certain]

## 3. Valuation
P/E 18.6x (35% of own 5y range), EV/EBITDA 12.4x (14%), P/FCF 8.1x (11%), FCF yield 12.31%. Peer medians are far higher (P/E 55.9x), but peers are faster-growing, so the discount is partly deserved. [Likely] The reverse DCF implies -6.1% year-1 revenue growth, fading to terminal growth: the price assumes steady shrinkage. [Certain] Mechanical base fair value is 47.53 vs the 33.53 close (+41.7%), bear 22.81; it relies on DCF holding margins, so is one estimate only. [Likely] The stock is 4.4% below its 50-day and 13.9% above its 200-day average, 12.9% above a year ago.

## 4. What would prove this wrong
- ROIC (ratios page, trailing) stays below 20% for two consecutive quarters.
- FCF margin (cash-flow page, TTM) falls below 30%.
- Net debt / EBITDA (ratios page) rises above 4.5x.
- Paying users (10-Q) fall below 17 million, or operating margin (income statement, TTM) falls below 22%.

## 5. Capital allocation and cash conversion
FCF conversion of net income is 210.3% TTM. Cash goes to buybacks and debt service, not growth: the 10-Q shows 31.6 million shares repurchased in nine months [company-reported] (source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1467623/000146762326000061/dbx-20260930.htm). Net debt/EBITDA is 3.41x, up from 2.09x in FY2024. A $1.1bn convertible notes offering was priced on 2026-11-10 (source: https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20261109623758/en/Dropbox-Inc.-Announces-Upsize-and-Pricing-of-1.1-Billion-Convertible-Senior-Notes-Offering/). [Certain] Use of proceeds not verified; buyback or refinancing is plausible. [Guessing] The 10-Q says FCF fell in the nine months on higher term-loan interest. [Certain]

## 6. Unusual items
- Why the price fell: it peaked at 38.22 on 2026-10-26, then declined after the Q3 results (an EPS and revenue beat with raised FY2026 guidance, but flat FY2027 preview) and the convertible offering, which adds potential dilution and hedging flows. [Likely] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/dbx/)
- Workforce cut of about 9% announced 2026-10-28 [company-reported] (source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1467623/000146762326000061/dbx-20260930.htm); new "DBX Agent Cloud" investment is unproven. [Likely]
- Short interest 19.01% of float, up 5.7% on the month; insiders hold 40.24%. [Certain]
- The 10-Q discloses ongoing litigation from the 2024 Dropbox Sign breach. [Certain]
