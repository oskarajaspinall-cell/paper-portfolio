# Cathay Pacific Airways (HKG:0293) — Core initiation — 2026-11-08
## 1. Business
Hong Kong flag-carrier airline group: passenger and cargo revenue, plus the HK Express low-cost unit. Earnings are driven by yields, load factors, fuel and Hong Kong's hub traffic. ## 2. Quality
Returns have recovered from the pandemic: ROIC 12.6% TTM vs -0.4% in FY2021, ROE 24.5% [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/quote/hkg/0293/financials/ratios/). Operating margin 11.3% TTM is down 0.8pp from a year ago and gross margin has fallen from 40.0% (FY2022) to 25.8% TTM, so unit economics are fading from the peak [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/quote/hkg/0293/financials/income-statement/). FCF margin is falling over five fiscal years (-1.1pp) but rising over the last 12 months (12.5% to 14.1%); the latest year is the better read of current cash generation [Likely] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/quote/hkg/0293/financials/ratios/?p=trailing). Net debt/EBITDA is 2.07x vs 11.07x in FY2021; Piotroski F is 7 [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/quote/hkg/0293/statistics/). Moat is a hub position with limited slot and traffic-right supply, but airlines are cyclical and fuel- and fare-sensitive; returns are not proven durable through a downturn [Guessing].

## 3. Valuation
P/E is 7.3x, 78% up its 5y range (5.8x-7.7x), against a peer median of 14.7x (n=2). EV/EBITDA is 5.1x, below its 5y minimum of 5.2x and 8% under peers. P/B is 1.6x, above its 5y maximum of 1.4x. FCF yield is 20.16% and P/FCF 5.0x vs peers at 29.3x [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/quote/hkg/0293/statistics/). Multiples are low for earnings that look near peak; the reverse DCF implies revenue falling 21.7% in year 1, a very pessimistic assumption [Likely]. Mechanical base fair value is 76.34 HKD vs a 15.06 close, a gap too large to trust; the model capitalises recent airline cash flows with a through-cycle method, and an airline cash cycle can reverse quickly [Guessing]. The price assumes the cycle turns, not that returns hold.

## 4. What would prove this wrong

## 5. Capital allocation and cash conversion
FCF/NI is 137.5% TTM and has exceeded 100% since FY2023, reflecting depreciation and working capital inflows on a heavy fleet; capex needs sustain this only if fleet spending stays contained [Likely]. Share count is down 7.58% year on year, so buybacks or lower share count are adding to per-share value [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/quote/hkg/0293/statistics/).
