# Macro overlay: Globe Life Inc. (GL) — 2026-10-09
## Assessment
Chain: long-term rates → reinvestment yield on GL's fixed-income portfolio backing long-duration reserves (net investment income) → and long-term rates → discount rate in the fact sheet's own P/B+ROE fair value (risk-free = 10y Treasury). These run opposite ways for a life insurer; GL's low beta (0.44 [Certain], https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/gl/statistics/) shows the market already treats it as low macro-sensitivity.

Facts (FRED, 2026-10-07/08): 10y Treasury (DGS10) 5.28%, +0.50pp ~1m, +0.72pp ~3m; 10y real yield (DFII10) 2.92%, +0.49pp/+0.61pp; 10y breakeven (T10YIE) 2.35%, roughly flat (+0.12pp 3m); fed funds (DFF) 3.88%, +0.26pp 3m; HY credit spread 3.09%, +0.39pp 3m but still tight; VIX 15.1, calm [Certain]. **(inference)** Because the nominal-yield rise is almost entirely real yield (breakevens flat), the market is pricing a higher real-rate/term-premium path, not an inflation shock — a tailwind to new-money reinvestment yields, not a cost-push risk to claims.

Bull: real yields stay elevated/rise further → investment income growth outpaces the modest lift to the cost-of-equity input, fair value holds or edges up despite the higher discount rate [Likely].
Bear: real yields reverse sharply (growth-scare cuts) and HY spreads widen further → reinvestment yields compress and risk appetite fades, pressuring the multiple even as the discount-rate input falls [Likely].
Tilt: small, toward neutral-to-bull — the earnings tailwind and discount-rate headwind largely offset, and low beta limits the risk-premium channel; the thesis still turns mainly on company-specific cash-conversion and governance watch items, not macro.
