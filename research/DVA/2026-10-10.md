# DaVita Inc. (DVA) — Core initiation — 2026-10-10
## 1. Business
DaVita operates 2,657 US outpatient dialysis centers serving ~200,500 patients with chronic kidney failure, plus clinics in 14 other countries; US dialysis is ~86% of consolidated revenue [Certain] (source: SEC 10-K, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/927066/000092706626000012/dva-20251231.htm). It is paid per treatment: government programs (Medicare/Medicaid) are 68% of US dialysis revenue from 89% of patients, while commercial payers are only ~11% of patients but ~26% of revenue [Certain] (same source) — commercial rates are much higher than government rates.

## 2. Quality
ROIC and ROCE have risen over five years (12.3%/14.2% TTM, +1.7pp/+2.2pp) [Certain] (source: fact sheet, ratios page). FCF conversion is strong and rising (190% TTM) [Certain]. But ROE of 88.5% TTM and P/B of 10.6x mainly reflect a shrunken book-equity base from years of debt-funded buybacks (shares down -14.73% YoY), not an extraordinary structural return [Likely]. Net debt/EBITDA is elevated and flat at 4.5x [Certain], limiting error margin. The moat is scale (2,657 centers, switching costs, regulatory barriers to new dialysis capacity) [Likely], but pricing power is capped by CMS rate-setting given 89% patient dependency on government coverage, and the small commercial segment is disproportionately important to revenue. Q2 2026 results showed lower revenue per treatment from declining commercial enrollment, which management attributed to patients dropping ACA marketplace plans after subsidy changes, while reaffirming full-year guidance [Likely] (source headline: https://www.reuters.com/legal/litigation/davita-flags-lower-revenue-per-treatment-reaffirms-annual-forecast-2026-08-04/).

## 3. Valuation
P/E (15.3x) is above its own 5y range (146%, vs 5y max 13.8x) and +20% vs peer median (12.7x) [Certain]. EV/EBITDA is in line with peers (8.5x vs 8.4x median) and low in its own range (30th percentile) [Certain]. P/FCF is cheap at 7.1x, -50% vs peer median (14.3x) [Certain] — the expensive P/E vs cheap P/FCF split suggests buyback-driven leverage and D&A/interest are distorting GAAP earnings relative to cash generation [Likely]. P/B (10.6x, +661% vs peers) is not a useful comparison given the shrunken equity base [Likely]. The fair-value model's base case (428 vs 179.25 close, +139%) implies the price assumes -11.7% revenue decline in year 1 fading to terminal growth — more pessimistic than current volume trends — so treat it as directional support for undervaluation on a cash basis, not a target [Likely] (source: fact sheet fair-value section).

## 4. What would prove this wrong
- US revenue per treatment / commercial mix (income statement, ratios page) declining for 2+ more consecutive quarters, signalling a structural shift rather than a one-off ACA effect.
- Net debt/EBITDA (ratios page) rising above 5.5x, showing buybacks are outrunning cash generation.
- FCF margin (cash flow/income statement) falling below 9% (near its 5y low of 8.3%), showing cash conversion deteriorating.
- ROIC (ratios page) falling back below 10%, reversing the five-year improving trend.

## 5. Capital allocation and cash conversion
DaVita funds heavy buybacks (shares down -14.73% YoY) with debt, holding net debt/EBITDA near 4.5x [Certain] (source: fact sheet). FCF conversion is strong (190% TTM, FCF margin 11.5%), giving headroom to keep repurchasing [Certain]. No dividend. 2025 buyback dollar figures were not disclosed in the 10-K sections reviewed, so pace versus FCF generation can't be fully quantified here [data unavailable] (source: SEC 10-K).

## 6. Unusual items
Short interest is high and rising: 12.67% of float, +7.4% month-over-month [Certain] (source: fact sheet, statistics page) — a crowded short likely tied to leverage/reimbursement risk, which can add volatility in either direction. Altman Z-score (1.64) sits in the distress-risk zone, though Piotroski F-score (6) shows no fundamental deterioration [Certain] (source: fact sheet, statistics page). Reimbursement dependency (89% of patients on government programs) is a structural macro/policy exposure [Certain] (source: SEC 10-K).
