# Centene Corporation (CNC) — Core initiation — 2026-10-12
## 1. Business
Centene is the largest U.S. Medicaid managed-care organization by membership, and also runs Medicare Advantage/Part D (Wellcare) and ACA Marketplace (Ambetter) plans. It collects fixed per-member premiums from state Medicaid agencies, CMS and ACA exchanges, and earns the spread between those premiums and members' medical costs (the health benefit ratio, HBR), plus smaller fee income [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/cnc/; https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1071739/000107173926000154/a20260728ex991pressrelease.htm).

## 2. Quality
FY2025 was a loss year: ROE -28.7%, FCF conversion -64.7%, driven by ACA Marketplace mispricing and Medicaid rate inadequacy [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/cnc/financials/ratios/). TTM figures (ROE -20.4%, FCF conversion -175.5%) still carry that drag even as 2026 has turned: Q2 2026 GAAP EPS was $2.19, HBR improved to 89.6% from 93.0%, and management raised FY2026 GAAP EPS guidance to above $3.11 [Certain] (source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1071739/000107173926000154/a20260728ex991pressrelease.htm). ROIC has been low and volatile (3.1%-8.8%, falling 5y trend) [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/cnc/financials/ratios/).

The moat is thin: Centene's edge is scale and incumbency in multi-year state Medicaid contracts, sticky between re-bids but exposed to rate-adequacy and contract-loss risk, since margins are a government-set spread, not priced by the company [Likely]. Molina, Elevance, UnitedHealth and Humana compete for the same state RFPs and ACA risk pools, so excess returns have not proven durable [Likely]. Net debt/EBITDA is unresolved between two definitions (3.50x vs -1.75x TTM) [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/cnc/financials/ratios/ — conflict flagged, not resolved).

## 3. Valuation
At $65.36, CNC trades on EV/EBITDA 5.3x, below its own 5-year range (5.5x-10.6x) and 66% below the peer (MOH, ELV, HUM, UNH) median of 15.5x; P/FCF is 3.6x vs peer 20.5x; P/B is 1.4x vs peer median 2.6x [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/cnc/financials/ratios/; peer statistics pages). A model fair value is not computable: TTM revenue/share and EPS inputs are missing or negative, so neither DCF nor P/E resolves [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/cnc/statistics/). The multiples imply the market is still pricing in 2025-style distress and regulatory risk (ACA subsidy uncertainty, Medicaid redetermination, rate inadequacy) despite the 2026 guidance raise and HBR improvement [Likely]. The stock is up 70.0% over 12 months and sits only 0.7% above its 50-day MA after a 3-month pullback of -3.3%, so some re-rating has occurred, but the discount to peers (-44% to -82%) leaves room for further re-rating if the 2026 recovery holds [Likely] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/cnc/history/).

## 4. What would prove this wrong
- Medicaid HBR (income statement / earnings release) rises materially above the current TTM-adjacent 93.9% and stays elevated for two or more quarters, signalling rate inadequacy has returned.
- ROE (ratios page) fails to stay positive through FY2026, repeating the FY2025 pattern (-28.7%).
- FCF conversion (cash-flow and income-statement pages) remains negative for two further consecutive quarters, indicating earnings quality has not normalized.
- Marketplace/Medicaid membership (10-Q/press releases) declines further beyond the already-disclosed Marketplace drop (5.9m to 3.5m), beyond the deliberate ACA de-risking management has described.

## 5. Capital allocation and cash conversion
Shares outstanding fell 2.48% year over year, consistent with net buybacks [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/cnc/statistics/). However TTM FCF conversion is deeply negative (-175.5%) and the two net-debt/EBITDA readings conflict (3.50x vs -1.75x) [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/cnc/financials/ratios/), so recurring free cash flow cannot currently be shown to fund that buyback; it has more likely been funded by working-capital timing or debt than by clean operating cash [Likely]. There is no dividend. Capital-return durability is unproven until FCF conversion turns consistently positive, checkable in the next 10-Q [Likely].
