## Assessment
WFC is a scale bank: net interest margin and its own cost-of-equity assumption (used in the fair-value model) are both rate-sensitive, so this is **secondary**, not background noise [Likely] **(inference)**.

**Facts (FRED, official):** 10y Treasury (DGS10) 5.22% on 2026-10-08, +0.42pp/1m, +0.68pp/3m; 2y (DGS2) 4.75%, +0.36pp/1m, +0.59pp/3m; effective fed funds (DFF) 3.88%, +0.25pp/1m, +0.26pp/3m; 10y real yield (DFII10) 2.87%, +0.56pp/3m; HY credit spread (BAMLH0A0HYM2) 3.15%, +0.45pp/3m; breakevens (T10YIE) 2.33%, roughly flat [Certain] (fred.stlouisfed.org, 2026-10-08/09).

**Market pricing (proxy, not fact):** 2y yield sits ~0.9pp above effective fed funds, implying the market prices further tightening or "higher for longer," not the cuts many expected earlier this year [Likely] **(inference)**. The 10y-2y curve is positively sloped (+0.47pp) and steepened slightly over 3 months — a NIM tailwind — while flat breakevens mean the yield rise is mostly real-rate/term-premium, not inflation fear [Likely] **(inference)**. This matches the market narrative in WFC's own news flow (WSJ, 2026-10-05, "a new rate-hiking cycle" — commentary, not data).

**Bull:** yields plateau, curve stays steep → NIM holds, cost of equity stops rising, multiple drifts toward the 71.61 base fair value. **Bear:** Fed resumes hiking, 10y pushes toward/above 5.5% and HY spreads keep widening → cost of equity rises further (lowering an already-below-market fair value) and provisioning risk rises faster than NIM gains.

**Tilt:** toward bear, moderate — price (83.55) is already above even the bull fair value (77.76), and the rate/spread move is still unresolved, not yet priced as a reversal [Likely] **(inference)**.
