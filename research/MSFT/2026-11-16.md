# Microsoft Corporation (MSFT) — Core initiation — 2026-11-16
## 1. Business
Software and cloud platform: Azure and server products, Microsoft 365 and Dynamics subscriptions, Windows licences, LinkedIn, gaming and devices. Revenue is mostly recurring enterprise subscription and consumption, increasingly AI-linked (Copilot, Azure AI). Country split: [data unavailable] on the pages read; no single foreign country confirmed at ~10%+ [Guessing]. The company recasts segments to Agents and Infra and Devices and Consumer from fiscal 2027 [Certain] (source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/789019/000119312526436133/msft-20260930.htm).

## 2. Quality
Profitability is elite but the return on capital is deteriorating. [Certain] Operating margin is 47.1% TTM, up from 42.1% in FY2022, and gross margin is 67.7%, flat (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/msft/financials/income-statement/). Net debt/EBITDA is 0.34x, so the balance sheet is not a constraint (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/msft/financials/balance-sheet/). Against that, ROIC fell from 61.9% (FY2022) to 25.3% TTM, 4.7pp in the last 12 months alone, and FCF margin fell from 26.6% to 17.3% (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/msft/financials/ratios/?p=trailing). [Likely] The cause is AI datacentre capex building the asset base ahead of revenue, not weaker pricing: margins are rising while returns on the larger base dilute. The moat (Office/Windows installed base, enterprise distribution, Azure scale) is durable [Likely], but the new 10-Q adds risk language that AI offerings face competition from hyperscalers, open-source and frontier model providers, some of them partners and Azure customers, and that AI pricing could fall through commoditisation [Certain] (source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/789019/000119312526436133/msft-20260930.htm).

## 3. Valuation
[Certain] P/E is 27.4x against a 5y range of 20.7x–37.7x (39th percentile), and EV/EBITDA is 19.3x (42%). The multiples sit below the stock's own median but above the peer median (P/E +61%). P/FCF is 64.9x, above its 5y maximum of 51.6x, and FCF yield is only 1.54%, because capex absorbs cash (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/msft/statistics/). Price is 18.6% above the 200-day average and 6-month momentum is +23.7%, but the 12-month change is +3.0%. The mechanical fair value (base 248.42, against a close of 523.88) is flagged unreliable: DCF and EV/Revenue disagree 5.3x. The reverse DCF implies 51.1% year-1 revenue growth, which is not credible, so the DCF likely understates the business by treating the capex surge as permanent [Guessing]. [Likely] The price assumes the AI investment earns a return that restores cash conversion; earnings multiples are undemanding, cash multiples are not.

## 4. What would prove this wrong
- ROIC (ratios page, TTM) falls below 20%.
- FCF margin (cash flow and income statement pages, TTM) falls below 12%.
- Operating margin (income statement page, TTM) falls below 42%, which would show AI price competition or cost inflation reaching profit.
- Net debt/EBITDA (balance sheet page) rises above 1.0x as capex is debt-funded.

## 5. Capital allocation and cash conversion
[Certain] FCF/net income has fallen from 84.0% (FY2024) to 42.0% TTM, and net debt/EBITDA is up from -0.31x to 0.34x. Quarterly additions to property and equipment were 39,367 million USD [company-reported] (source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/789019/000119312526436133/msft-20260930.htm). The same filing shows the dividend still being paid and a smaller buyback. Share count is down 0.21% a year, so buybacks barely offset dilution. [Likely] Reinvestment now takes priority over returns to shareholders; payback on the datacentre build is the decisive variable.
