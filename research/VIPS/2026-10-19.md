# Vipshop Holdings (VIPS) — Core initiation — 2026-10-19

## 1. Business
Vipshop is an online discount/flash-sale retailer in China, selling branded apparel, cosmetics and lifestyle goods bought wholesale and re-sold to members at markdown prices [Certain]. Revenue is almost entirely 1P product sales, supplemented by a SVIP loyalty tier and physical Shan Shan outlet stores (source: fact sheet OV).

## 2. Quality
Historically a strong cash generator: ROIC has averaged >30% over five years and net cash sits at -2.22x EBITDA (TTM), but the trend is deteriorating — ROIC fell from a 55.4% FY2023 peak to 22.8% TTM and FCF conversion fell from 150.9% (FY2023) to 75.5% (FY2025) (source: ratios/cash-flow pages) [Certain]. Q2 2026 revenue fell 4.3% YoY to RMB24.7bn and active customers fell to 42.3mn from 43.5mn YoY (-2.8%) [company-reported] (source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1529192/000110465926100484/tm2623972d1_ex99-1.htm), evidence that the customer base is shrinking, not just growth slowing. TTM net margin of 9.8% is inflated by a one-off RMB5.79bn REIT listing gain in June 2026 [company-reported, same source]; underlying profitability is lower than headline TTM figures suggest. The moat is scale in off-price sourcing and a loyal SVIP cohort, but China's discount channel is now contested by PDD/Temu and Taobao's markdown features, so durability is uncertain [Likely].

## 3. Valuation
At 4.2x P/E and 2.8x EV/EBITDA, VIPS trades below its own 5-year range (6.5x/3.3x minimums) and far below peer medians (18.6x P/E, 7.8x EV/EBITDA for PDD/JD/BABA) (source: fact sheet valuation table) [Certain]. The reverse DCF implies the market is pricing in -18.6% revenue growth in year 1 fading to terminal growth — i.e. a continuation, not a stabilization, of the customer and revenue declines just reported. Base fair value of $20.31 is 58% above the last close of $12.86; even the bear case ($13.06) sits only 1.5% above the price, so the market has already priced close to a worst-case scenario [Likely]. P/FCF (8.7x) and P/B (1.1x) are less extreme — P/FCF is actually above the peer median (+12%), reflecting the recent fall in FCF margin rather than cheapness.

## 4. What would prove this wrong
- Active customers (company filings, SEC 6-K) decline accelerates beyond -5% YoY for two consecutive quarters, signalling structural share loss rather than cyclical softness.
- Gross margin (income statement page) falls below 22% (vs. 23.4% TTM), indicating the off-price sourcing edge is being competed away.
- ROIC (ratios page) falls below 20% on a sustained (two-quarter) basis, below even the depressed TTM level.
- Net debt/EBITDA (balance sheet/ratios page) turns positive (loses net cash), indicating the buyback is being funded beyond free cash flow generation.

## 5. Capital allocation and cash conversion
Shares outstanding fell 4.25% YoY (source: statistics page), funded by buybacks: a $1.0bn program (effective through February 2027, $216.9mn remaining) plus a new $1.0bn program approved for the following 24 months [company-reported] (source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1529192/000110465926100484/tm2623972d1_ex99-1.htm). FCF margin has fallen from a 10.9% FY2023 peak to 5.2% in FY2025 (source: cash-flow/income-statement pages), so the buyback pace relies on a cash generation trend that is declining, not a one-off. Net cash of 2.22x EBITDA gives balance-sheet headroom to sustain it regardless [Likely].

## 6. Unusual items
Q2 2026 net income rose 189% YoY almost entirely because of a one-off RMB5.79bn investment gain from listing the Vipshop Commercial REIT on the Shanghai Stock Exchange in June 2026 [company-reported] (source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1529192/000110465926100484/tm2623972d1_ex99-1.htm); TTM profitability ratios in the fact sheet are flattered by this and should not be read as a run-rate. Short interest rose 8.3% month-over-month (source: statistics page) [Certain], consistent with bearish positioning into the slowing top line, though institutional ownership remains substantial at 48.6%.
