# Leidos Holdings (LDOS) — Core initiation — 2026-10-19
## 1. Business
Leidos provides IT systems engineering, digital/cyber and technology services mostly to the US government, under long-dated, often multi-year contracts [Certain]. From FY2026 it reports four segments: Intelligence & Digital, Health, Homeland and Defense (https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1336920/000133692026000246/ldos-20260703.htm) [company-reported]. US government customers were 83-85% of revenue in H1 2026, down from 87% a year earlier (https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1336920/000133692026000246/ldos-20260703.htm) [company-reported].

## 2. Quality
ROIC rose from 9.9% (FY21) to 14.2% TTM (+7.7pp over 5y); ROCE rose from 11.6% to 16.8% (+8.1pp); operating margin expanded from 8.5% to 11.8%; FCF margin rose to 12.3% TTM — all rising trends [Certain]. The moat is incumbency: security-cleared staff, past-performance requirements and multi-year programs make it costly for the government to switch providers or for new entrants to bid cleared work [Likely]. The flip side is customer concentration on one buyer's budget cycle (see section 1); the 10-Q flags a likely FY2027 continuing resolution and shutdown risk at the September 2026 deadline (https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1336920/000133692026000246/ldos-20260703.htm) [company-reported]. Net debt/EBITDA improved from 3.45x (FY21) to 1.77x (FY25) but rose back to 2.45x TTM after new acquisition-funded debt — the deleveraging trend has paused, not reversed [Likely].

## 3. Valuation
LDOS trades on 11.7x P/E, 9.0x EV/EBITDA, 7.2x P/FCF — each below its own 5-year minimum and 16-38% below the SAIC/BAH/CACI/GD peer median. Base fair value is $279.78 vs a $124.85 close (+124%); the reverse DCF implies the price assumes -13.7% revenue in year 1 fading to terminal growth. A gap this large should be treated cautiously: it likely reflects a sector-wide re-rating of US government-services contractors on budget-cut and shutdown risk rather than company-specific distress, given rising ROIC/margins and a stable net-debt trend [Likely]. The stock is down 33% over 12 months but up 15% over 3 months, so some of that de-rating may already be stabilizing [Likely].

## 4. What would prove this wrong
- US government revenue share (10-Q MD&A, [company-reported]) falls below 75% of revenue on program losses rather than deliberate commercial diversification
- Operating margin (income statement page) falls back below 10%, reversing the 5-year expansion
- ROIC (ratios page) falls back below 10%, erasing the improvement since FY2021
- Net debt/EBITDA (ratios page) rises above 3.0x without a visible deleveraging path from the Entrust/Analogic-related debt

## 5. Capital allocation and cash conversion
FCF conversion is strong (157% of net income TTM) and FCF margin is rising (12.3% TTM) [Certain]. The company issued $600m and $800m of senior notes and terminated a bridge facility to fund the Entrust acquisition and the Analogic/Altaris security-screening JV combination, lifting net debt/EBITDA from 1.77x to 2.45x and raising non-operating expense ($142m H1 2026 vs $105m H1 2025), partly a $23m UK pension buyout settlement loss (https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1336920/000133692026000246/ldos-20260703.htm) [company-reported]. Shares outstanding fell 3.0% YoY, consistent with continued buybacks alongside the M&A spend [Certain].

## 6. Unusual items
FY2026 segment realignment (four segments replacing the prior structure) limits like-for-like comparability this year (https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1336920/000133692026000246/ldos-20260703.htm) [company-reported]. Short interest rose 32% month-on-month but remains small in absolute terms (3.84% of float) [Certain]. No risk-factor changes of substance in the latest 10-Q (https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1336920/000133692026000246/ldos-20260703.htm) [Certain].
