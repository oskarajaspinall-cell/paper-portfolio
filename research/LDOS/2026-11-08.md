# Leidos Holdings (LDOS) — Core initiation — 2026-11-08

## 1. Business
Leidos is an IT and mission-services contractor. It sells engineering, cyber, intelligence, health and defense solutions mainly to US federal agencies, under multi-year contracts. [Certain] About 83% of third-quarter revenue came from US government contracts [company-reported] (source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1336920/000133692026000293/ldos-20261002.htm). Segments were realigned this year into Intelligence & Digital, Health, Homeland and Defense. No single foreign country is shown as ~10%+ of revenue. [Likely]

## 2. Quality
A good, not exceptional, services business. Margins stepped up structurally: operating margin 8.5% (FY2021) to 11.8% TTM, FCF margin 6.8% to 11.0% TTM (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/ldos/financials/income-statement/). [Certain] The moat is incumbency: security clearances, embedded program knowledge and recompete switching costs. It is real but not pricing power. [Likely]

**Direction change addressed:** ROIC is 15.2% TTM versus 16.6% a year earlier and a 17.5% peak; ROCE is 17.1% versus 19.3% (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/ldos/financials/ratios/?p=trailing). [Certain] Operating margin is flat (+0.1pp) and FCF margin rose 3.0pp, so the decline is a denominator effect: the Entrust acquisition added capital and debt (net debt/EBITDA 2.46x vs 1.77x at FY2025). The latest 10-Q attributes higher operating income to program wins and mix, offset by completed higher-margin contracts [company-reported] (source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1336920/000133692026000293/ldos-20261002.htm). [Likely] Returns remain well above FY2021-23 levels, so the 5-year "rising" trend is still intact but has stalled.

## 3. Valuation
The price assumes decline. P/E 11.9x is below its 5y minimum of 15.7x and 44% below peers (21.1x); EV/EBITDA 9.2x is below a 5y minimum of 11.2x; P/FCF 8.3x versus a 13.4x minimum; FCF yield 12.10% (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/ldos/statistics/). [Certain] The reverse DCF implies -11.6% revenue growth in year 1 (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/ldos/). Shares are down 32.0% over 12 months and the 50-day is below the 200-day. [Certain]

Model base fair value is 270.01 against a 132.68 close, but the DCF leg (315.60) is far above the EV/EBITDA leg (178.85); I treat the lower leg as the more credible anchor and the DCF as optimistic. [Likely] Even the bear case (145.50) is above the price. [Certain] The market is pricing a sharp federal-spending or margin reset; the Q3 results (raised EPS guidance, higher dividend) do not show one yet. [Likely] Revenue guidance was cut, partly from the SES divestiture, which closed October 5 (source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1336920/000133692026000293/ldos-20261002.htm). [Likely]

## 4. What would prove this wrong
- Operating margin (TTM, income statement page) falls below 10.5%.
- ROIC (TTM, ratios trailing page) falls below 13%.
- FCF margin (TTM, cash flow and income pages) falls below 8%.
- Net debt/EBITDA (ratios page) rises above 3.0x.

## 5. Capital allocation and cash conversion
FCF conversion is 138.1% of net income TTM, and the share count fell 3.42% YoY (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/ldos/statistics/). [Certain] The dividend was raised 4.7% to $0.45 a quarter (source: https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/leidos-holdings-inc-increases-quarterly-cash-dividend-by-4-7-302924879.html). [Certain] Debt rose to $6.0 billion for the Entrust deal (acquired March 2026) [company-reported] (source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1336920/000133692026000293/ldos-20261002.htm). Deleveraging is the priority; buybacks have slowed accordingly. [Likely]

## 6. Unusual items
