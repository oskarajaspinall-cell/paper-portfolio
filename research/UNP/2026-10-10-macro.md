# Macro overlay: Union Pacific Corporation (UNP) — 2026-10-10
## Assessment
Chain: rising long-end rates -> higher DCF discount rate -> lower fair value, and -> higher financing cost on the pending $85bn Norfolk Southern debt-funded deal -> leverage/EPS-accretion risk [Likely] **(inference)**.

Facts: US 10y Treasury (DGS10) 5.22% on 2026-10-08, +68bp over 3 months; 10y real yield (DFII10) 2.87%, +56bp over 3 months; 10y breakeven (T10YIE) 2.33%, roughly flat (-4bp 1m, +9bp 3m) (FRED, fredgraph.csv, 2026-10-08/09). So the back-up is real-rate/term-premium driven, not inflation-expectations driven [Certain]. 2y (DGS2) 4.75% vs effective fed funds 3.88% implies the market still prices some easing ahead, but less than three months ago (2y +59bp vs funds +26bp) [Likely] **(inference)**, a market-pricing proxy, not fact. US HY spread (BAMLH0A0HYM2) 3.15%, +45bp in 3 months — funding conditions tightening modestly [Certain].

The fact sheet's own DCF already uses the current 5.22% risk-free rate, so this rate level is already in the cited base fair value (178.57) and the -36% gap to the 278.34 close; this doesn't change the bull/bear case, only its magnitude if rates move further. **Bull**: yields roll over (DGS10 back toward ~4.8%) -> discount rate eases, fair value edges up, deal financing cheaper, narrowing (not closing) the valuation gap. **Bear**: real yields keep rising -> fair value falls further below price, NS-deal leverage/financing cost rises, reinforcing the already-identified overvaluation and merger-execution risk. **Tilt**: toward bear, small — rates amplify an existing valuation/merger thesis rather than drive it; UNP's revenue (domestic freight volumes) is not materially rate-sensitive.
