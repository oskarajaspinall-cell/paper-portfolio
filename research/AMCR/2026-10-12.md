# Amcor plc (AMCR) — Core initiation — 2026-10-12

## 1. Business
Amcor designs and manufactures flexible and rigid packaging (films, bottles, closures, containers) for food, beverage, healthcare, home/personal care and other customers, selling mainly under multi-year supply contracts that pass through resin/input costs [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/amcr/). Scale increased materially after the 2025 all-stock merger with Berry Global [Certain] (source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1748790/000174879026000022/amcr-20260630.htm).

## 2. Quality
Returns have fallen since the Berry Global merger: ROIC 10.3%→6.9% TTM, ROCE 12.2%→7.1%, ROE 18.2%→9.4% over five years [Certain] (fact sheet, ratios page). Net debt/EBITDA jumped to 6.98x in FY2025 and sits at 3.97x TTM, a 5y rise of +0.91x [Certain] (fact sheet, ratios/balance sheet/income statement). Margins have held up better: gross margin flat near 20%, operating margin down only 1.4pp to 9.0%, consistent with contractual pass-through pricing and customer switching costs that peers also enjoy [Likely]. FCF conversion remains strong at 111% TTM [Certain] (fact sheet, cash flow/income statement). Altman Z-score of 1.37 sits in the distress zone (<1.8), a genuine leverage flag despite a healthy Piotroski F of 7/9 [Certain] (fact sheet, statistics page). Management states Berry integration synergies are "ahead of schedule," supporting double-digit EPS growth guidance [Likely] (source: Jefferies Global Industrials Conference transcript, https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/amcr/transcripts/745831-jefferies-global-industrials-conference-2026/). The contractual/scale moat looks intact but is currently funding deleveraging, not lifting returns.

## 3. Valuation
At $41.72, AMCR trades at 17.5x P/E (+18% vs. peer median 14.8x), 9.4x EV/EBITDA (+6% vs. peers but 15% below its own 5y range minimum), 15.7x P/FCF (+17% vs. peers) and a 6.4% FCF yield [Certain] (fact sheet, ratios/statistics). P/B of 1.6x is 61% below the peer median of 4.2x, but reflects the enlarged, goodwill-heavy post-merger balance sheet and 45.6% YoY share dilution, not cheapness [Certain] (fact sheet). Base-case fair value (DCF 67% / EV-EBITDA 33% blend, inform-only) is $58.05 (+39%), bear $35.88 (-14%), bull $108.88 (+161%) [Certain] (fact sheet). The reverse DCF implies the market is pricing only 0.2% year-1 revenue growth fading to terminal growth, a low bar versus management's double-digit EPS guidance [Certain] (fact sheet), but the P/E and P/FCF premiums to peers show this is not a statistically cheap entry; upside depends on synergies and deleveraging converting into re-rated returns.

## 4. What would prove this wrong
- Net debt/EBITDA (ratios page) rises above 4.5x TTM (vs. 3.97x now), showing deleveraging has stalled.
- ROIC (ratios page) falls below 6% TTM (vs. 6.9% now), showing post-merger returns are still eroding rather than recovering.
- FCF margin (income statement/cash flow statement) falls below 4.5% (vs. 5.2% TTM), showing integration is not improving cash conversion.
- Altman Z-score (statistics page) falls further below its current 1.37, showing balance-sheet distress risk is worsening rather than easing.

## 5. Capital allocation and cash conversion
Management's stated priority order is dividends, organic growth, disciplined/targeted M&A and portfolio pruning, then buybacks, while protecting the investment-grade rating [Certain] (source: FY2026 10-K, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1748790/000174879026000022/amcr-20260630.htm). FCF conversion of 111% TTM is strong [Certain] (fact sheet, cash flow/income statement). Shares outstanding rose 45.6% YoY from the all-stock Berry merger, diluting per-share metrics and swamping any buyback effect for now [Certain] (fact sheet, statistics page). No quantified debt-reduction target or timeline, nor a dollar synergy figure, appears in the FY2026 10-K [Certain] (source: SEC filing above); deleveraging is described only as a priority once "full synergies" are achieved.
