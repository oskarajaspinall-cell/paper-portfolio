# EOG Resources, Inc. (EOG) — Core initiation — 2026-10-08

## 1. Business
EOG explores for, develops, produces and markets crude oil, natural gas liquids and natural gas, including gathering, processing and marketing of those volumes [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/eog/). The asset base spans multiple U.S. basins (Permian/Delaware, Eagle Ford, Utica) plus international positions in the UAE and Trinidad [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/eog/). Revenue is commodity-price-driven: realized oil, NGL and gas prices times produced volumes, so earnings swing with WTI/Henry Hub even where EOG controls its own well costs [Likely].

## 2. Quality
ROIC, ROCE and ROE all compressed FY2021-FY2025 (ROIC 20.7%→16.0%; ROCE 17.9%→13.8%; ROE 22.0%→16.8%), though TTM readings (19.8%/18.0%/22.5%) remain well above a typical cost of capital [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/eog/financials/ratios/). Margins also narrowed (gross -3.9pp, operating -2.3pp, net -1.6pp over the period) and FCF conversion (FCF/NI) fell from 105.9% to 69.3% by FY2025 before recovering to 96.2% TTM [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/eog/financials/cash-flow-statement/). The moat is cost position, not pricing power: EOG is a price-taker on oil/gas, so its durable edge is a low per-well breakeven and a multi-basin portfolio that lets capital rotate to the highest-return play each year [Likely] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/eog/transcripts/661414-q2-2026/). That edge compresses in strong years as peers' returns rise with the cycle too, so the five-year deterioration looks mostly cyclical rather than competitive [Likely], but the trend needs to stop, not just slow.

## 3. Valuation
At 144.21 USD, EOG trades on 11.3x TTM P/E, near the top of its own 5y range (9.3x-11.4x, 92nd percentile) but 30% below the peer median of 16.1x (FANG, DVN, CTRA, OXY) [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/eog/financials/ratios/). Cash-flow multiples look cheaper: EV/EBITDA 5.5x is roughly in line with peers (5.6x) and near the top of its own range (75th pct); EV/Sales 3.0x is 19% below peers; P/FCF 11.4x is 11% below peers and near the bottom of its own 5y range (15th pct) [Certain]. The outlier is P/B at 2.4x, a 59% premium to the 1.5x peer median, reflecting EOG's higher returns on capital rather than asset inflation [Likely]. FCF yield is 8.74% [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/eog/statistics/). Net, the price assumes margins and FCF conversion stabilise near current, already-reduced levels rather than reverting further down [Likely].

## 4. What would prove this wrong
- ROIC (ratios page) falls below 12% trailing-twelve-months (TTM 19.8%; FY2025 16.0%)
- Net debt/EBITDA (ratios/balance sheet page) rises above 1.0x (TTM 0.23x)
- FCF conversion, FCF/NI (cash-flow and income-statement pages) stays below 50% for two consecutive fiscal years (FY2025 was 69.3%)
- Piotroski F-Score (statistics page) falls below 5 (currently 7)

## 5. Capital allocation and cash conversion
Shares outstanding fell 3.33% YoY, consistent with net buybacks funded from free cash flow [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/eog/statistics/). Net debt/EBITDA is 0.23x TTM versus negative/near-zero in FY2022-24 — still low in absolute terms, but the sharpest one-year move in the five-year series [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/eog/financials/ratios/). FCF conversion is volatile (67.9%-105.9% across FY21-25, 96.2% TTM), consistent with working-capital and commodity-price swings rather than a structural cash-conversion problem [Likely]. Management has said it intends to direct the large majority of 2026 free cash flow to dividends and buybacks [Likely] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/eog/transcripts/661414-q2-2026/), consistent with the falling share count already observed.

## 6. Unusual items
CFO transition announced 2026-09-24: Ann Janssen is retiring and Jeff Hibbard is named incoming CFO [Certain] (source: https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/eog-resources-announces-ann-janssens-retirement-and-jeff-hibbard-as-next-cfo-302888526.html). This reads as a planned, orderly transition at a company with Piotroski F-Score 7 and Altman Z 3.69 [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/eog/statistics/), not a governance red flag on its own, but the new CFO's first full quarter (Q3 2026, reported around 2026-11-05/06) is worth watching for any change in capital-allocation tone [Likely].
