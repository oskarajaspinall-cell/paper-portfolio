# Shanghai Rural Commercial Bank (SHA:601825) — Core initiation — 2026-11-09
## 1. Business
Regional bank serving Shanghai and its rural and suburban areas; earns net interest income on loans, plus fees. Revenue is China-domestic, reported in CNY, so no foreign-country exposure above 10% is expected [Likely]; the segment split is not stated in the fact sheet. Net margin is 52.8% TTM, typical of a deposit-funded lender (source: https://stockanalysis.com/quote/sha/601825/financials/income-statement/).

## 2. Quality
ROE is 9.5% TTM, down from 11.3% in FY2021, falling (-1.6pp) over five years [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/quote/sha/601825/financials/ratios/). Net margin has risen +2.3pp over the same period, so profit has held up while returns on equity fell, which suggests the equity base is growing faster than earnings [Likely]. ROIC, ROCE, operating margin and leverage metrics are [data unavailable], as is usual for banks, so quality rests on ROE alone. Piotroski F is 3, which is weak (source: https://stockanalysis.com/quote/sha/601825/statistics/). Beta is -0.05 and shares change is 0.21% YoY, so there is no dilution. I see no evidence of a durable moat beyond a local deposit franchise and a regulated market [Guessing]. Cash-flow metrics for banks are not meaningful: FCF margin swings from -52.3% to 207.9%.

## 3. Valuation
P/E is 7.1x against a 5y median of 6.7x and a 5y maximum of 7.3x, so it sits at the 92nd percentile of its range and is +7% above the peer median of 6.6x [Certain]. P/B is 0.6x against a 5y median of 0.6x (80th percentile) and is 5% below peers at 0.7x. P/FCF is not meaningful for a bank. The mechanical fair value is flagged unreliable because the methods disagree 3.0x. The best method, P/B plus ROE, gives a base of 12.29 CNY, against a close of 9.04 CNY. The dividend discount method gives 4.53 CNY (source: fact sheet, https://stockanalysis.com/quote/sha/601825/statistics/). The price implies -7.5% year-1 growth in the reverse DCF [Likely]. It is cheap on book value but not cheap against its own earnings history. The 3.5% country risk premium and the falling ROE argue against trusting the P/B upside [Likely].
