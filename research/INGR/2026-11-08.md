# Ingredion (INGR) — Core initiation — 2026-11-08
## 1. Business
Ingredion converts corn, tapioca and other crops into starches, sweeteners and texturisers sold to food and beverage makers. Segments: Texture & Healthful Solutions (T&HS, higher margin, specialty), Food & Industrial Ingredients LATAM and US/Canada, plus All Other (source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1046257/000162828026071518/ingr-20260930.htm) [Certain]. Mexico and Brazil are material within LATAM; country share of revenue [data unavailable] (not in the filing text read). Pending all-cash purchase of Tate & Lyle (UK) [Certain].

## 2. Quality
Fiscal-year returns improved: ROIC rose from 7.0% (FY2021) to 15.6% (FY2025), operating margin from 9.9% to 14.4% (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/ingr/financials/ratios/) [Certain]. The last year contradicts that: TTM ROIC 12.0% vs 15.0% a year earlier, operating margin 12.0% vs 14.6%, FCF margin 4.8% vs 7.6% (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/ingr/financials/ratios/?p=trailing) [Certain]. Causes in the latest filing: a Q2 thermal event at the Argo plant, higher manufacturing and raw-material costs, Mexican peso effects and an earthquake in Colombia (source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1046257/000162828026071518/ingr-20260930.htm) [Certain]. Gross margin fell to 23.0% TTM from 25.3% in FY2025 [Certain]. Q3 missed and 2026 guidance was cut, with soft US/Canada demand and tapioca inflation (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/ingr/) [Likely]. Moat is modest: scale, plant network and customer qualification in commodity-linked ingredients; T&HS share gains are the durable part [Likely]. Whether Argo is a one-off or a cost problem is [Guessing] unresolved.

## 3. Valuation
Cheap against history and peers. P/E 10.4x (5y range 9.6x–55.0x, median 13.0x) vs peer median 15.2x. EV/EBITDA 6.0x is below its 5y minimum of 6.3x, vs peers 11.3x. P/B 1.3x is below its 5y minimum. FCF yield is 6.07% and P/FCF 16.5x vs 11.6x median, so cash conversion is the weak spot (FCF/NI 61.6% TTM). Mechanical base fair value 108.91 vs close 91.72 (+18.7%); the DCF bear case is negative and the bull is wide, so the estimate is unreliable [Likely]. The reverse DCF implies revenue shrinking 2.2% in year 1. The price discounts depressed profits persisting; it does not discount a clean recovery. Trend is weak: below both moving averages, 12-month change -14.8% (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/ingr/statistics/) [Certain].

## 4. What would prove this wrong
- TTM operating margin (income statement, trailing page) stays below 11.0% after Argo is back in full production.
- TTM ROIC (ratios, trailing page) falls below 10.0%.
- Net debt/EBITDA (statistics page) above 3.0x pro forma after the Tate & Lyle financing, or the deal's financing is drawn on worse terms.
- T&HS net sales volume (10-Q segment note) turns negative year on year.

## 5. Capital allocation and cash conversion
Share count fell 2.81% YoY (buybacks) and the dividend was raised (source: https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/09/17/3364424/0/en/ingredion-incorporated-increases-quarterly-dividend-to-0-83-per-share.html) [Certain]. Net debt/EBITDA is 0.72x today. The Tate & Lyle deal changes this: up to about $4.2 billion of new debt, with consolidated debt of up to about $6.0 billion post-closing vs $1.8 billion now [company-reported] (source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1046257/000162828026071518/ingr-20260930.htm). Management cites cost synergies and EPS accretion (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/ingr/transcripts/736615-barclays-19th-annual-global-consumer-conference/); delivery is [Guessing]. FCF conversion is below 100% in two of the last three years.
