# Micron Technology (MU) — Core initiation — 2026-11-16
## 1. Business
Micron makes DRAM and NAND memory, selling to data centre, PC/mobile, auto and industrial customers. Revenue is driven by bit shipments times average selling price (ASP), and ASP is set by the industry supply/demand balance. In fiscal 2026 over half of revenue came from the top ten customers and about 60% from data centre [company-reported] (source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/723125/000072312526000023/mu-20260903.htm) [Certain]. Revenue by country: [data unavailable] (not found in the 10-K text read; no foreign-country share confirmed).

## 2. Quality
This is a cyclical commodity producer at peak-cycle returns, not a proven compounder. TTM ROIC is 93.4% against 15.1% a year earlier, and operating margin is 74.6% against 26.1% (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/mu/financials/ratios/?p=trailing) [Certain]. The 5y history shows ROIC of -7.4% in FY2023 and 1.7% in FY2024 (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/mu/financials/ratios/) [Certain]. Net cash and an Altman Z of 9.72 mean balance-sheet risk is low (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/mu/statistics/) [Certain].

The possible durable change is the new take-or-pay "strategic customer agreements", with multi-year volumes and mostly price bands. Management says even floor-price margins would exceed past-cycle peaks [company-reported] (source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/723125/000072312526000023/mu-20260903.htm). This is a claim, and its first test is the next downturn [Likely]. Supply is tight, but the filing warns that rival capacity additions could reduce ASPs [Certain].

## 3. Valuation
At 778.31 USD, P/E is 9.9x, at the bottom (2%) of its 5y range of 7.3x-136.2x. EV/EBITDA is 7.5x (25% of range) and P/FCF is 14.9x, below its 5y minimum. FCF yield is 6.70% (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/mu/financials/ratios/) [Certain]. Those low multiples sit on peak earnings, and the 5y median P/E is 14.4x [Certain]. The price assumes margins largely hold. MU trades 56% above the peer median P/E (n=4) and 76% above on EV/EBITDA [Certain].

The mechanical fair value is flagged unreliable: base 213.56 against bull 1,073.44, and the methods disagree by 2.1x. I give it little weight [Certain]. The shares are 1.7% above the 200-day average. Three-month momentum is -18.1%, and they trail SPY by 15.0pp over 3 months (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/mu/history/) [Certain]. The recent drop is tied to a chip sell-off around Nvidia and AI-spending doubts, which headlines describe (source: https://invezz.com/news/2026/11/16/why-micron-and-sandisk-stocks-are-falling-on-monday/) [Likely]. Beta is 2.40 [Certain].

## 4. What would prove this wrong
- Gross margin (income statement page, TTM) falls below 55%, which would mean the pricing step-up is reversing.
- FCF margin (cash flow statement page, TTM) falls below 25% while capex keeps rising.
- ROIC (ratios page, trailing) falls below 40%.
- Management discloses cancelled, renegotiated or price-reduced strategic customer agreements (10-K/10-Q, company releases).

## 5. Capital allocation and cash conversion
FCF conversion is 69.4% of net income and FCF margin is 44.3%. Capex was 30.7bn USD in fiscal 2026 [company-reported] (source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/723125/000072312526000023/mu-20260903.htm), so heavy fab spending is absorbing much of the cash. The company repurchased 650 million USD of stock and paid 610 million USD of dividends, which is small against earnings [company-reported] (same filing). Customer deposits on strategic agreements were 12.75bn USD [company-reported] (source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/723125/000072312526000023/mu-20260903.htm), so part of the FCF is pre-funded by customers. Shares outstanding rose 1.60% YoY, so there is no net buyback benefit (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/mu/statistics/) [Certain].

## 6. Unusual items
- Added 10-K risks: ASP swings (annual DRAM and NAND ASP changes of +200% to -50% over five years), AI demand that is hard to forecast, rival capacity additions, government-incentive dependence, labour disputes and antitrust (source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/723125/000072312526000023/mu-20260903.htm) [Certain].
- Supply allocation decisions may hurt some customers and end markets [Certain].
- Pillar Two (15% minimum tax) largely offsets the Singapore incentive, which raises the tax rate [Certain].
- Short interest is 2.07% of float, low. Institutions own 79.6% (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/mu/statistics/) [Certain].
- Next earnings: Dec 16, 2026 [Certain].
