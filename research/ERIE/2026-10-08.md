# Erie Indemnity Company (ERIE) — Core initiation — 2026-10-08

## 1. Business
Erie Indemnity is the attorney-in-fact for Erie Insurance Exchange, a reciprocal insurer with no employees of its own. Indemnity performs policy issuance, underwriting, claims handling and administration for the Exchange and in return earns a management fee capped at 25% of the Exchange's direct and affiliated written premiums [Certain] (source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/922621/000162828026010666/erie-20251231.htm). It carries none of the Exchange's underwriting risk.

## 2. Quality
Indemnity is a capital-light fee business, not an underwriter: TTM ROIC 27.4% and ROCE 29.2%, up from the low-20s in FY2021, with net debt/EBITDA consistently negative (-0.29x TTM), i.e. no leverage [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/erie/financials/ratios/). Operating margin rose from 11.9% (FY2021) to 18.4% (TTM) and FCF conversion is 95.9% TTM [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/erie/financials/cash-flow-statement/). The moat is structural: the 10-K states Indemnity has "no direct competition in providing these services to the Exchange" [Certain] (source: SEC 10-K above). The risk is that the Exchange is Indemnity's only customer and the fee rate is reset at least annually by Indemnity's own Board, weighing the Exchange's capital needs against Indemnity's results — a governance tension since the fee is a direct cost to the Exchange's policyholder-owners [Certain] (source: SEC 10-K above). The rate already sits at the 25% ceiling, so expansion must come from premium growth, not a higher take rate.

## 3. Valuation
On its own multiples the stock looks historically cheap: TTM P/E 19.8x sits 42% below its 5-year low of 26.8x, EV/EBITDA 13.3x is 37% below its 5-year low of 17.9x, and P/B 4.6x is 45% below its 5-year low of 6.6x [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/erie/financials/ratios/). This follows a 31.1% 12-month share-price decline and 46.9pp underperformance vs SPY over 12 months [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/erie/history/). But against P&C peers (PGR, TRV, ALL, CINF) ERIE still trades at a large premium — P/E +129%, EV/EBITDA +109%, P/B +127% vs peer medians [Certain] (source: fact sheet peer statistics pages). The premium reflects the fee model's structurally higher ROE/FCF conversion than underwriters that carry claims risk, but the price still assumes that premium persists; it is a bargain only against its own rich history, not peers.

## 4. What would prove this wrong
- Net margin (income statement page) falls below 10% (TTM: 14.0%), signalling fee-rate pressure or cost inflation.
- ROE (ratios page) falls below 15% (TTM: 24.8%), signalling the fee economics are deteriorating.
- FCF conversion, FCF/NI (cash-flow and income statement pages) falls below 60% (TTM: 95.9%), signalling working-capital or investment-income strain.
- Gross margin (income statement page) falls below 10% (TTM: 17.9%), signalling the Board has cut the management fee rate materially below the 25% ceiling.

## 5. Capital allocation and cash conversion
Shares outstanding are essentially flat (-0.01% YoY), consistent with a payout-focused rather than buyback-driven model [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/erie/statistics/). FCF margin rose from 9.6% (FY2021) to 14.0% (TTM) and FCF conversion from 85.3% to 95.9%, with no net debt to service, so free cash flow is available almost entirely for distribution [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/erie/financials/cash-flow-statement/).

## 6. Unusual items
Short interest is elevated for an insurer at 9.5% of float, though it fell 8.9% month-on-month [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/erie/statistics/). Q2 2026 results showed improved underwriting and higher net income with moderating premium growth, consistent with the quality trend above, not a new risk [Likely] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/erie/transcripts/654449-q2-2026/).
