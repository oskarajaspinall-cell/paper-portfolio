# Illinois Tool Works Inc. (ITW) — Core initiation — 2026-10-09

## 1. Business
ITW makes engineered, branded industrial components and equipment across seven segments (Automotive OEM, Food Equipment, Test & Measurement/Electronics, Welding, Polymers & Fluids, Construction Products, Specialty Products), selling direct plus recurring consumables/aftermarket [Certain] (source: https://www.itw.com). Each of ~80 autonomous divisions runs the proprietary "ITW Business Model" — 80/20 front-to-back simplification and customer-back innovation [Certain] (source: https://www.itw.com).

## 2. Quality
ROIC has held ~29-30% every year since FY2021 (29.3% TTM) while ROCE rose from 27.8% to 41.8% TTM and gross/operating margins expanded +2.8pp/+2.3pp over five years; FCF conversion is 91.5% TTM [Certain] (source: fact sheet, RA/IS/CF). The moat is organizational: ~80 small, decentralized divisions each apply 80/20 simplification and close customer engineering to defend pricing power in niche/proprietary components, which is hard for larger, more centralized peers (DOV, ETN, EMR, PH) to replicate division-by-division [Likely]. But the reinvestment runway looks thin — ROIC has not compounded higher despite the margin gains (flat vs FY2021) — and net debt/EBITDA has risen from 1.52x (FY2024) to 1.84x (TTM) [Certain] (source: fact sheet, RA/BS/IS), consistent with a mature, buyback-funded compounder rather than a share-gain growth story.

## 3. Valuation
Current multiples sit in the cheaper third of ITW's own five-year range (P/E 34th percentile, EV/EBITDA 11th, P/FCF 3rd) and 8-30% below peer (DOV/ETN/EMR/PH) medians on P/E, EV/EBITDA, EV/Sales and P/FCF [Certain] (source: fact sheet). But the stockanalysis.com DCF/EV-EBITDA blend puts base fair value at $167.26 vs the $264.71 close (-36.8%), and the reverse DCF shows the price embeds ~18.7% year-one revenue growth fading to terminal — well above the 4-5% FY2026 revenue growth management itself guided to after Q2 (raised from 2-4%) [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/itw/ news, 2026-07-28; fact sheet). P/B at +330% vs peers reflects an equity base shrunk by years of buybacks against >100% ROE, not a standalone signal [Likely]. Net: relatively cheap on multiples, but the DCF gap means a new buyer pays for growth beyond what guidance or five-year history supports.

## 4. What would prove this wrong
- Organic revenue growth (income statement / quarterly release) stays below ~3% for two consecutive quarters, undercutting the premium-multiple thesis.
- Gross margin (income statement) falls back below 42% (the FY2023 level), signalling 80/20 pricing/mix discipline is breaking down.
- Net debt/EBITDA (ratios page) rises above 2.0x, constraining the buyback-funded capital return that currently drives EPS growth.
- ROIC (ratios page) falls below 27%, below the five-year range, evidence that returns are being competed away.

## 5. Capital allocation and cash conversion
In August 2026 the board raised the dividend 7% to $6.88/share and authorized a new $6bn buyback [Certain] (source: https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/08/07/3341312/31446/en/itw-announces-7-dividend-increase-and-6-billion-share-repurchase-program.html). FCF conversion of 91.5% TTM funds both without added leverage strain beyond the 1.84x net debt/EBITDA noted above [Certain] (source: fact sheet, CF/IS). Shares outstanding fell 1.86% YoY [Certain] (source: fact sheet, ST), consistent with buybacks as the main growth lever given mid-single-digit organic growth — capital discipline is high but organic growth optionality is limited.

## 6. Unusual items
Short interest is 3.4% of float, flat month-on-month; institutional ownership 86.4%; Piotroski F-score 7/9 and Altman Z 7.87 indicate no balance-sheet distress [Certain] (source: fact sheet, ST). None of this is material enough to move the decision beyond the valuation gap noted in Section 3.
