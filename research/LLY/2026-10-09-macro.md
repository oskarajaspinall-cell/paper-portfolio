# Macro overlay: Eli Lilly and Company (LLY) — 2026-10-09
## Assessment
LLY's thesis is fundamental (GLP-1 franchise, patent cliffs, pricing) [Certain], but its valuation is priced like a long-duration growth asset — FCF yield 1.75%, reverse DCF implies 37.6% year-1 revenue growth, and the fact sheet's DCF uses the 10y Treasury (DGS10, 5.28%, 2026-10-07, FRED) as risk-free rate [Certain] — so real yields are the macro variable moving its discount rate and fair-value range **(inference)**.

**Facts**: 10y real yield (DFII10) 2.92% on 2026-10-07, +0.49pp/~1m, +0.61pp/~3m (fred.stlouisfed.org, id=DFII10). 2y Treasury (DGS2) 4.77% vs effective fed funds (DFF) 3.88% (2026-10-07). **Market pricing (proxy)**: 2y ~89bp above fed funds implies the market isn't pricing near-term cuts — a proxy, not a guarantee [Likely]. **Inference**: base fair value ($508, 56.5% below the $1,169.60 close) already uses today's elevated 5.28% risk-free rate; a further real-yield rise would push fair value lower, deepening the existing overvaluation signal; a decline would narrow the gap without flipping it. Realized beta is low (0.45, 5y, stockanalysis.com), suggesting LLY has historically traded less rate-sensitively than its DCF profile implies — a tension flagged **(inference)**.

**Bull**: real yields fall (soft inflation / dovish pivot) → discount rate falls → fair-value range rises, narrowing the gap to price. **Bear**: real yields keep rising → discount rate rises → fair value falls further below the close, reinforcing the overvaluation case. **Tilt**: toward bear, small — yields are rising on the facts, but low historical beta argues this is a secondary pressure, not the driver.
