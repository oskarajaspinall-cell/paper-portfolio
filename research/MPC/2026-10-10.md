# Marathon Petroleum Corporation (MPC) — Core initiation — 2026-10-10

## 1. Business
Marathon refines crude into fuels (Refining & Marketing), plus Midstream (logistics, largely via MPLX) and a small Renewable Diesel unit. Revenue is the crack spread — crude input cost vs refined-product output price — times volumes run through its Gulf Coast, Mid-Continent and West Coast refineries (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/mpc/). [Certain]

## 2. Quality
Refining is commodity processing with no pricing power: margins track the market crack spread. TTM ROIC/ROCE of 18.1% and ROE of 42.1% look excellent, but ROIC swung from 6.1% (FY21) to 30.6% (FY22) to 9.8-9.9% (FY24-25) before this TTM spike (factsheet RA) — extreme cyclicality. Net margin *fell* over 5 years (8.1%→3.0% FY25, 5.5% TTM) even as ROE rose, so buybacks (shares down 6.6% YoY, ST), not margin expansion, are inflating per-share returns [Likely]. Net debt/EBITDA rose to 3.24x (FY25) from 0.70x (FY22) — leverage building into an extended cycle [Likely]. News ties the margin surge to war/disruption supply shocks (Iran/Hormuz, Ukraine strikes on Russian refining, possible diesel export curbs) (https://www.wsj.com/business/energy-oil/american-oil-refiners-are-printing-money-as-wars-shrink-global-energy-supplies-c9240231; https://www.barrons.com/articles/diesel-prices-valero-marathon-refining-stocks-4487b345), flagged by that commentary as reversal-prone [Likely]. No moat beyond scale; a cyclical business at a cycle peak.

## 3. Valuation
At 455.03 USD, MPC trades at 130% of its own 5y P/E max, 262% of its 5y EV/Sales max, and 651% of its 5y P/B max (RA/ST). EV/EBITDA (10.0x) and P/FCF (9.9x) sit modestly below peer medians (10.8x, 12.9x), but P/E (+15%), EV/Sales (+28%) and P/B (+140%) are above peer medians (n=4). The through-cycle DCF base fair value (398.76, best method here) is 12.4% *below* the last close; bear case (99.54) implies 78.1% downside. The reverse DCF needs only modest growth to justify the price, but applies to TTM earnings at a cyclical peak (FCF conversion 150.8% TTM vs 29.7-126.3% in FY21-24) — the earnings base, not the growth rate, is the risk. Price already sits above base-case fair value; the stock is up 141.1% in 12 months vs SPY's +15.7% (HI/ST), RSI 73.5 — valuation and momentum both say the cycle's persistence is priced in. [Likely]

## 4. What would prove this wrong
- Net debt/EBITDA (ratios page) falls back below 2.0x, showing deleveraging rather than peak-cycle borrowing.
- ROIC (ratios page) holds above 15% for two further fiscal years, evidencing a structurally higher mid-cycle return rather than a one-off margin spike.
- Net margin (income statement) recovers above its FY21-23 average (~7.6%), showing margin gains are not just buyback-driven ROE inflation.
- FCF conversion (cash flow/income statement) normalizes toward its FY21-23 range (30-126%) without a collapse in absolute FCF, confirming earnings quality through the cycle.

## 5. Capital allocation and cash conversion
TTM FCF margin is 8.4% with FCF/NI conversion of 150.8%, and shares outstanding fell 6.6% YoY (factsheet RA/ST), confirming an active buyback. Reuters reported refiners "stepped up investor rewards" in Q2 (source: https://www.reuters.com/business/energy/top-us-refiners-see-profits-soar-step-up-investor-rewards-2026-08-12/ — headline only, article not fetchable) [Likely]. Net debt/EBITDA has risen to 3.24x (FY25)/1.72x (TTM) even while returning cash, meaning capital return is being funded partly by increased leverage into a cycle peak rather than purely organic FCF [Likely].

## 6. Unusual items
Short interest rose 18.3% month-on-month to 2.76% of float (factsheet News & sentiment) — a modest but rising bet against the stock at the top of its momentum move [Likely]. RSI 73.5 signals an overbought condition, reinforcing that little margin of safety remains against the stretched valuation in section 3.
