# The Cooper Companies, Inc. (COO) — Core initiation — 2026-10-12
## 1. Business
CooperVision sells disposable, toric and multifocal contact lenses to eye-care practitioners and distributors worldwide, earning recurring replacement revenue; it is the larger of two segments [Certain] (source: https://www.coopercos.com, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/711404/000162828026061155/cooperq32026pressrelease.htm). CooperSurgical sells fertility (IVF) and women's-health surgical products, the smaller but currently faster-growing segment [Certain] (same sources). Market cap $10.3bn (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/coo/).

## 2. Quality
Returns on capital are mediocre and falling: ROIC 8.0% (FY21) to 5.0% TTM; ROCE 6.5% to 5.1% TTM, both below a reasonable cost of capital [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/coo/financials/ratios/). Operating margin compressed from 19.7% to 12.9% TTM even as gross margin held near 66% [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/coo/financials/income-statement/). Management cut full-year guidance in September, attributing it to a proactive U.S. channel-inventory reduction in CooperVision rather than a one-off; this is the second cut this year, which makes the "proactive, temporary" framing hard to verify yet [Likely] (source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/711404/000162828026061155/cooperq32026pressrelease.htm). Net debt/EBITDA has risen from 1.9x to 2.8x over five years [Certain] (same ratios source). FCF conversion is strong (119% TTM) [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/coo/financials/cash-flow-statement/). Patents and practitioner relationships give CooperVision some moat in lenses, but five straight years of falling ROIC against larger eye-care peers suggest that moat is eroding rather than durable [Likely].

## 3. Valuation
At $54.37, COO trades on 18.7x TTM P/E, the bottom quartile of its own 5-year range (7.0x-53.1x) and a 61% discount to the peer median (48.1x), though the peer set (ALC, BLCO, ZBH, EW) mixes eye care with unrelated med-tech, which widens that gap mechanically [Likely] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/coo/financials/ratios/). On EV/EBITDA (13.9x) and EV/Sales (3.1x) it sits roughly in line with peers; on P/FCF (15.2x vs 26.0x peer) and P/B (1.2x, below its own 5-year minimum) it looks cheap even against them [Certain] (same source). The fact sheet's base fair value is $71.49 (+31.5% vs last close), bear $13.65 (-74.9%), bull $96.80 (+78.0%) [Likely] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/coo/). The reverse DCF implies the price assumes only 5.6% revenue growth next year fading to terminal, close to the low end of recent organic growth — the market is pricing continued weakness, not a reversal [Likely].

## 4. What would prove this wrong
- CooperVision Americas or Asia-Pacific organic revenue growth stays negative for two more consecutive quarters, confirming structural share loss rather than a channel one-off (income statement / stockanalysis.com)
- ROIC (ratios page) falls below 4% TTM, extending the five-year decline
- Net debt/EBITDA (ratios page) rises above 3.5x, showing leverage becoming a constraint on buybacks
- Piotroski F-Score (statistics page) falls below 5, signalling broader fundamental deterioration

## 5. Capital allocation and cash conversion
FCF margin is 16.0% TTM with FCF conversion of 119% of net income, well above the five-year average, showing working-capital discipline even through the guidance cuts [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/coo/financials/cash-flow-statement/). Shares outstanding fell 2.3% year-on-year, indicating net buybacks [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/coo/statistics/). Net debt/EBITDA of 2.8x (up from 1.9x in FY21) leaves less room to keep buying back stock aggressively if earnings stay under pressure [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/coo/financials/ratios/). Management completed a strategic review in September and chose not to divest CooperSurgical, instead reaffirming the existing capital-allocation plan [Certain] (source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/711404/000162828026061155/cooperq32026pressrelease.htm).

## 6. Unusual items
Activist investor Jana Partners publicly urged the board (18 Sept 2026) to run an external CEO search, appoint a new chair and explore asset sales, which the company rejected days later in its strategic-review outcome — an unresolved governance overhang [Likely] (source: https://www.reuters.com/legal/transactional/jana-urges-cooper-replace-ceo-explore-asset-sales-after-performance-setbacks-2026-09-18/). A securities-law firm (SueWallSt, 14 Sept 2026) announced an investigation into disclosures following the revenue miss and guidance cut; no claim has been filed yet, so this is an early-stage legal risk, not a confirmed liability [Guessing] (source: https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/coo-shareholder-investigation-suewallst-notifies-investors-of-potential-securities-claims-involving-cooper-companies-302877234.html). Short interest is 5.30% of float and rose 14.0% month-on-month, consistent with rising bearish positioning into the news flow [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/coo/statistics/).
