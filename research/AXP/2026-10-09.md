# American Express Company (AXP) — Core initiation — 2026-10-09

## 1. Business
AXP runs a closed-loop payments network and issues its own cards, so it earns discount revenue (merchant swipe fees) on network spend, net interest income on card loans, and annual card fees — unlike Visa/Mastercard, which take network fees only and carry no credit risk (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/axp/). [Certain]

## 2. Quality
TTM ROIC is 11.9%, flat over five years (+0.3pp), and TTM ROE is 34.4%, though trending down (-1.7pp) from 2021 (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/axp/financials/ratios/). [Certain] The moat is the closed-loop model itself: AXP owns the merchant relationship and the cardholder data in one network, letting it target a premium, high-spend customer base and fund rich co-brand rewards (e.g., Delta, Marriott) that bank issuers on Visa/Mastercard rails cannot easily replicate — it is also deepening this with lounge capacity and a new Amex Corporate platform (sources: https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260930479998/en/American-Express-Delivers-the-Next-Generation-of-Amex-Corporate%E2%84%A2/, https://www.fastcompany.com/91620290/american-express-just-revealed-its-biggest-airport-lounge-expansion-ever). [Likely] Risks to the moat: fintech/neobank entrants are explicitly targeting Amex's premium and commercial-card customers (Revolut's CEO has stated intent to compete with Amex and JPMorgan in the US) — a stated intention, not yet evidence of share loss [Guessing], and a regulatory compliance order (see §6) adds near-term distraction risk. [Likely]

## 3. Valuation
At 18.7x P/E, AXP sits mid-range of its own 5-year history (43rd percentile of 14.9x–23.8x) and 16% below the peer median of 22.2x (V, MA, COF, SYF) (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/axp/financials/ratios/). [Certain] On P/FCF (13.8x vs. peer median 30.1x) and P/B (6.1x vs. peer median 10.7x) AXP looks far cheaper, but the peer set mixes pure network tolls (V, MA) with lenders (COF, SYF), so these gaps partly reflect business-model differences, not just mispricing. [Likely] The fact sheet's own-history fair value (P/E method; FCFE/DCF unavailable — no TTM revenue or share count data) puts base fair value at $272.52, 11.5% below the last close of $308.10, with bear/bull at $245.75/$344.57 (source: fact sheet fair value table). [Certain] The current price therefore assumes AXP sustains above its own 5-year-average earnings multiple despite a decelerating ROE trend — a reasonable but not cheap entry point. [Likely]

## 4. What would prove this wrong
- ROE (ratios page) falls below 25% for two consecutive fiscal years, signalling the premium-spend model has lost its return advantage.
- ROIC (ratios page) falls below 8%, closing the gap to cost of capital.
- FCF conversion, FCF/NI (cash flow + income statement pages), falls below 80% on a trailing-twelve-month basis, sustained for two quarters, indicating cash-quality deterioration.
- A future bank-regulatory order (Fed/OCC or other) imposes an asset or receivables growth cap on American Express National Bank, directly constraining loan-book growth (next filings via SEC EDGAR).

## 5. Capital allocation and cash conversion
TTM FCF conversion is 131.5% of net income — still healthy but down from 161%–256% in 2021–22 (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/axp/financials/cash-flow-statement/, https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/axp/financials/income-statement/). [Certain] Shares outstanding fell 2.49% year-on-year, indicating continued net buybacks funding part of the high ROE (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/axp/statistics/). [Certain] TTM FCF yield is 7.23% (source: statistics page), comfortably covering buybacks and the (not separately disclosed) dividend. [Likely]

## 6. Unusual items
On 2026-10-08, American Express National Bank consented to Federal Reserve and OCC orders over its financial-crimes/AML compliance program and agreed to pay a $350m OCC civil penalty, partly previously reserved; the filing states the orders impose no asset cap and are not expected to affect 2027 guidance (source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/4962/000000496226000376/axp-20261008.htm). [Certain] This is a one-off compliance cost/governance flag, not (on the company's own disclosure) a growth constraint — worth monitoring via future filings but not thesis-changing at this size (~0.2% of market cap). Short interest is low (2.06% of float, falling) and institutional ownership (64.8%) is unremarkable (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/axp/statistics/). [Certain]
