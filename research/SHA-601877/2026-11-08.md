## 2. Quality
Mediocre, and the cash-flow picture does not reconcile. Returns are low: ROIC 6.6% TTM, down from 9.5% in FY2021, and ROCE 8.6% (source: https://stockanalysis.com/quote/sha/601877/financials/ratios/). [Certain] Operating margin of 12.9% TTM is 1.5pp below a year earlier and net margin has slipped to 6.8% (source: https://stockanalysis.com/quote/sha/601877/financials/income-statement/?p=trailing). [Certain]

The headline FCF margin of 36.1% TTM, against net margin of 6.8%, gives FCF/NI of 534%. FCF was negative in FY2021-FY2022. Net debt/EBITDA rose from 2.50x to 4.25x while FCF supposedly surged, and Altman Z is 1.57 (source: https://stockanalysis.com/quote/sha/601877/statistics/). That pattern points to working-capital or financing-related inflows rather than durable surplus cash. [Likely] FCF margin is also 5.1pp below its 2-year high. No evidence of a durable moat beyond brand and distribution scale. [Guessing]

## 3. Valuation
The price (24.73 CNY) is optically cheap: P/E 11.6x is below the 5y minimum of 12.4x, P/B 0.9x is below its 5y range, and EV/EBITDA 9.6x sits at 15% of its range. Versus peers (P/E median 25.7x; EV/EBITDA 18.2x) the discount is 55% and 47%. [Certain] The 46% FCF yield and P/FCF of 2.2x are not credible as an earnings-power signal given the conversion issue above. The model fair value (base 164.55 CNY, DCF-led) is an artefact of that FCF and is not relied on; the bear case is negative. [Likely] The stock is down 16.4% over 12 months and 25.4% over six months, lagging SPY. The market is pricing falling returns plus leverage; the peer set (grid-capex and automation names) deserves a premium for growth that Chint's margins are not showing. [Likely]
