# Automatic Data Processing, Inc. (ADP) — Core initiation — 2026-10-09

## 1. Business
ADP sells cloud-based Human Capital Management software: payroll, tax compliance, benefits administration and HR/workforce management, billed as recurring per-employee fees (Employer Services), plus PEO co-employment (ADP TotalSource) for small/mid-sized clients [Certain] (source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/8670/000000867026000030/adp-20260630.htm). It also earns interest income on client payroll-tax funds held briefly before remittance to tax authorities [Certain] (same source).

## 2. Quality
A genuinely good business: TTM ROIC 60.5% and ROE 72.2%, both up over five years; operating margin 26.5% (+3.1pp 5y) and FCF margin 23.9% (+6.2pp 5y), with FCF/NI conversion of 118.8% [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/adp/financials/ratios/, https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/adp/financials/income-statement/). The moat is scale and switching costs: 1.1m clients and 42m workers give the "industry's largest workforce dataset," with payroll embedded in tax-authority and bank integrations; the 10-K cites ~13-year average client life in Employer Services and ~6 years in PEO [Certain] (source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/8670/000000867026000030/adp-20260630.htm). One structural risk: a slice of earnings is client-fund interest income, which the 10-K flags as sensitive to lower rates or changed remittance rules [Likely] (same source). ROCE has fallen 4.7pp over five years to 44.2% even as ROIC rose — a widening gap worth monitoring but not yet a quality problem (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/adp/financials/ratios/).

## 3. Valuation
At 24.7x P/E ADP trades mid-range of its own 5-year band (20.3x–30.7x, 43rd percentile) and in line with peers (24.6x median: PAYX, PAYC, WDAY, NSP) [Certain]. But on EV/EBITDA it is cheaper than peers (16.9x vs 21.8x, -23%) while on P/FCF (+22%) and P/B (+30%) it is more expensive — a mixed multiple picture (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/adp/statistics/). More telling is the fact sheet's own-history fair value: base case $228.49 and even the bull case $249.39 sit 16% and 8% below the $270.73 last close; only the reverse-DCF implied growth (11.6% revenue growth in year 1, fading to terminal) rationalizes the price, well above ADP's recent organic trajectory [Likely] (source: fact sheet fair-value section). The market is pricing a durable acceleration in growth that the bull case doesn't support.

## 4. What would prove this wrong
- ROCE (ratios page) falls below 40%, extending the five-year decline from 48.9% to 44.2%.
- FCF margin (cash flow/income statement) falls below 20%, reversing the rise from 17.7% to 23.9%.
- Net debt/EBITDA (ratios page) rises above 0.5x, signalling weaker cash discipline or leveraged M&A.

## 5. Capital allocation and cash conversion
Balance sheet is conservative: net debt/EBITDA 0.20x (own definition), down from 0.48x five years ago [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/adp/financials/ratios/). Cash conversion is strong and improving (FCF/NI 118.8%, +19.6pp over 5y), and shares outstanding fell 1.32% YoY, consistent with net buybacks [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/adp/statistics/). Dividend per share and payout ratio are not shown on the fact sheet [data unavailable].

## 6. Unusual items
None material: short interest is low (2.66% of float, down 2.5% month-on-month) and institutional ownership is high (91.2%) — both unremarkable for a mega-cap [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/adp/statistics/). Two routine board nominations (Kenny, McKinstry) were announced 2026-09-24; not thesis-relevant [Certain] (source: https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/adp-nominates-david-w-kenny-and-nancy-mckinstry-to-board-of-directors-302888361.html).
