# Fair Isaac Corporation (FICO) — Core initiation — 2026-10-07

## 1. Business
FICO sells (1) Scores — the FICO Score, the standard credit-risk measure used by US lenders, distributed through and alongside Experian, TransUnion and Equifax, which together generated 51% of FY2025 company revenue [Certain] (source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/814547/000081454725000030/fico-20250930.htm); and (2) Software — decision-management/analytics SaaS (fraud, originations, collections) sold directly to banks and insurers. Mortgage Scores use is effectively mandated by Fannie Mae/Freddie Mac/FHFA rules [Certain] (same source).

## 2. Quality
Returns on capital are exceptional and still rising: ROIC 65.0% TTM (up from 32.3% in FY2021), ROCE 96.5% TTM, operating margin 52.1% TTM, FCF margin 41.6%, FCF conversion 122% (source: stockanalysis.com/stocks/fico/financials/ratios/). The moat is regulatory: 12 CFR Part 1254 requires FHFA validation/approval before any alternative model can replace FICO Score for GSE mortgages, and lender models are calibrated on its scale, creating switching costs [Certain] (SEC 10-K, above URL). The threat: FICO's distribution partners (the three bureaus) jointly own VantageScore, a direct competing score the 10-K flags as a risk [Certain] (same source). On 2026-10-01 the market reacted to an FHFA move to simplify mortgage pricing with an intraday drop of >20% (source: stockanalysis.com news feed headline, https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/fico-shareholder-investigation-suewallst-notifies-investors-of-potential-securities-claims-involving-fico-302895621.html) — a genuine risk to the mortgage-Scores toll-booth, not purely sentiment [Likely]. Leverage has risen with buybacks: net debt/EBITDA 4.23x TTM vs 2.60x FY2021 (ratios page), narrowing the safety margin.

## 3. Valuation
At 695.46 USD (2026-10-06 close), FICO trades at P/E 19.6x, EV/EBITDA 16.1x, EV/Sales 8.5x and P/FCF 15.1x (source: stockanalysis.com/stocks/fico/financials/ratios/ and statistics/). All are below FICO's own 5-year range: P/E 13% below its 5-year low of 27.9x, EV/EBITDA 13% below its low of 21.8x, P/FCF 10% below its low of 20.7x. Versus peers (MCO, SPGI, VRSK, EFX), FICO is 23% cheaper on P/E and 21% cheaper on P/FCF, roughly in line on EV/EBITDA (-1%) and a 6% premium on EV/Sales. FCF yield is 6.63%. The price no longer assumes a premium "best-in-class analytics" multiple; it assumes either a durable step-down in the regulatory moat, below FICO's own historical trough, or prolonged uncertainty. With ROIC/margins still rising in the trailing data and no confirmed fundamental deterioration yet, the de-rating looks more severe than the financials published to date [Likely].

## 4. What would prove this wrong
- ROIC (ratios page) falls below 35% (its FY2021 level), signaling the moat is actually eroding, not just re-rated on fear.
- Gross margin (income statement) falls below 75% (below its 5-year range), indicating loss of pricing power in Scores.
- Combined revenue concentration from the three credit bureaus (10-K/10-Q disclosure) declines materially as FHFA/GSEs formally adopt VantageScore alongside or instead of FICO Score for conforming mortgages.
- Net debt/EBITDA (balance sheet/ratios page) rises further past 5x while FCF margin compresses, showing leverage outrunning cash generation.

## 5. Capital allocation and cash conversion
FICO is cash-generative (FCF margin 41.6% TTM, FCF conversion 122% of net income) and has shrunk its share count 4.5% YoY through buybacks (source: stockanalysis.com/stocks/fico/statistics/). That return of capital has been funded partly with debt: net debt/EBITDA has risen from 2.60x (FY2021) to 4.23x (TTM) (source: ratios page). No dividend figure is shown on the fact sheet [data unavailable]. Continued buybacks at this leverage trajectory assume the Scores cash flows prove durable through the current regulatory uncertainty.

## 6. Unusual items
FHFA mortgage-pricing-simplification action (2026-10-01) triggered a >20% single-day drop and is a direct, not yet fully resolved, threat to the Scores moat [Likely] (source: stockanalysis.com news feed headline, https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/fico-shareholder-investigation-suewallst-notifies-investors-of-potential-securities-claims-involving-fico-302895621.html). FICO announced a 15% workforce reduction tied to AI-driven restructuring on 2026-10-06 [Certain] (source: stockanalysis.com news feed headline, https://www.reuters.com/business/world-at-work/fico-cuts-workforce-by-15-ai-driven-restructuring-2026-10-06/). A law firm (SueWallSt) is soliciting investors over a potential securities claim following the price drop — a routine post-decline solicitation, not a confirmed filed suit [Guessing] (same PRNewswire headline). Short interest is 9.28% of float and rose 8.0% month-on-month (source: stockanalysis.com/stocks/fico/statistics/), showing material bearish positioning alongside the regulatory risk above.
