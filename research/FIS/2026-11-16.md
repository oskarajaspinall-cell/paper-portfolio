# Fidelity National Information Services (FIS) — Core initiation — 2026-11-16
## 1. Business
FIS sells core processing, payments and capital-markets software and outsourcing to banks. Revenue is mostly recurring, multi-year contracts [Certain]. Q3 2026: Banking Solutions was the largest segment and recurring revenue about 82% [company-reported] (source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1136893/000113689326000063/fis-20260930.htm). Non-North-America revenue was about 22% [company-reported], spread across several countries, none identified at ~10%+ (same source). Issuer Solutions (bought from Global Payments, closed January 2026) is now inside Banking [Certain].

## 2. Quality
Returns are low for a "quality" label: TTM ROIC 6.9%, ROCE 8.1%, operating margin 21.5%, FCF margin 21.5% (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/fis/financials/ratios/?p=trailing). [Certain]
The moat is switching cost: core-system migrations are slow and risky for banks, and the contracts are recurring [Likely].
Direction change, addressed: ROCE (9.2% to 8.1%), operating margin (22.6% to 21.5%) and FCF margin (24.7% to 21.5%) all fell over the last 12 months, while ROIC rose (3.2% to 6.9%). ROIC is inflated by the Worldpay gain flowing through TTM net margin (26.8%), so I treat ROCE and operating margin as the cleaner reads: margins are eroding, not improving [Likely].
The November 12 conference transcript points to client-directed implementation delays and higher cyber spend weighing on 2027 margins (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/fis/transcripts/796331-kbw-fintech-ecosystem-conference-2026/) [Likely]. Piotroski F is 5 and Altman Z 0.31 (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/fis/statistics/).

## 3. Valuation
At 29.54 the stock trades below its 5y minimum on every multiple: EV/EBITDA 9.5x (5y range 14.9x–22.0x), P/FCF 5.5x, P/B 0.9x; FCF yield 18.26%. EV/EBITDA is 16% below the peer median of 11.3x (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/fis/statistics/). [Certain]
Reverse DCF: the price implies -24.9% revenue growth in year 1 [Certain]. The mechanical fair value (base 97.84) is flagged unreliable (+231%), so I give it no weight. P/E of 4.4x is distorted by the Worldpay gain.
The market is pricing leverage, guidance-cut and execution risk, not a recurring-revenue collapse [Likely]. Cheap on cash flow, but net debt/EBITDA of 5.43x leaves little room if EBITDA slips [Likely].

## 4. What would prove this wrong
- Operating margin (income-statement TTM page) falls below 19%.
- FCF margin (cash-flow TTM page) falls below 15%.
- Net debt / EBITDA (ratios page) rises above 6.5x.
- ROCE (ratios TTM page) falls below 7%.

## 5. Capital allocation and cash conversion
Debt rose to about 20.8bn from about 13.1bn at year-end, and goodwill to about 25.0bn, after the Issuer Solutions deal, funded with about 7.7bn of new debt [company-reported] (source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1136893/000113689326000063/fis-20260930.htm). Shares are down 1.75% YoY, so buybacks continue, but deleveraging should take priority [Likely]. FCF conversion of 80.3% TTM is flattered by the Worldpay gain in net income; FCF margin is falling [Likely].
