# Macro overlay: Fidelity National Information Services (FIS) — 2026-10-09
## Assessment
FIS is USD-reporting and not a rate-sensitive lender, but it just funded the Issuer Solutions deal with ~$7.7bn of new debt, taking net debt/EBITDA to 5.82x TTM with an Altman Z of 0.35 (distress zone) [Certain] (source: factsheet). That makes refinancing cost, not FX or demand, the one macro channel that can move this thesis: `real yields + credit spreads -> refinancing cost & DCF discount rate -> deleveraging speed & equity risk premium -> multiple`.

**Facts**: 10y real yield (DFII10) 2.92% (2026-10-07), +0.61pp/3m; 10y Treasury (DGS10) 5.28%, +0.72pp/3m — mostly a real-yield move, not inflation expectations (breakeven T10YIE +0.12pp to 2.35%) [Certain] (fred.stlouisfed.org, 2026-10-07/08). HY credit spread (BAMLH0A0HYM2) 3.09%, +0.39pp/3m [Certain] (fred.stlouisfed.org, 2026-10-07).

**Market pricing (proxy)**: the 2y Treasury (4.77%) above effective fed funds (3.88%) implies the market isn't pricing near-term cuts [Likely] **(inference)**.

FIS's fair value already uses the 5.28% 10y as risk-free rate, so today's level is priced; a further rise is the surprise. **Bull**: yields/spreads ease, refinancing cheapens, deleveraging from 5.82x proceeds, distress discount narrows toward the 22.3x peer P/E. **Bear**: yields/spreads keep widening, refinancing the new debt costs more, deleveraging stalls, the P/E 5.3x-vs-peers-22.3x discount persists regardless of operating progress. **Tilt**: moderate toward bear — the 3-month move works against the balance sheet's one vulnerability (fresh debt, Altman Z 0.35), though much of the discount is likely already priced given the multiple gap.
