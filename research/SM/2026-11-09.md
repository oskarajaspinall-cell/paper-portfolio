# SM Energy (SM) — Core initiation — 2026-11-09
## 1. Business
US oil-weighted, liquids-rich exploration and production company. It sells crude, NGLs and gas from the Permian (Midland and Delaware), the DJ Basin, the Maverick Basin and the Uinta Basin (source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/893538/000089353826000147/sm-20260930.htm). The January 2026 Civitas merger roughly doubled the share count and added DJ and Permian acreage [Certain]. The South Texas assets were sold in April 2026 [Certain]. All revenue is US; no foreign country is material.

## 2. Quality
Cyclical price-taker with no structural moat; returns follow commodity prices and cost position [Likely]. Gross margin is high (85.4% TTM) and ROE is 15.0%, but the last 12 months are weakening: ROIC fell from 11.9% to 8.7% and operating margin from 34.9% to 26.9% (2-year high 47.0%), on the fact sheet's Direction-change lines. FCF margin recovered to 16.8% from -48.7% a year earlier, when merger-year spend distorted it. The merger lifted scale and synergies, but per-unit returns have not yet improved [Likely]. Piotroski F is 6, and Altman Z is weak at 1.12 [Certain]. Uinta takeaway constraints may shift drilling away from Utah in early 2027, which limits growth in that basin (source: https://www.reuters.com/business/energy/sm-energy-may-shift-drilling-early-2027-amid-uinta-basin-constraints-2026-11-04/) [Likely].

## 3. Valuation
The price is 29.92 USD. P/E is 6.2x, at the 3rd percentile of its 5y range and 29% below peers (8.7x). EV/EBITDA is 3.8x, mid-range (48%) and in line with peers (3.7x). P/FCF is 6.6x, 29% above peers, and FCF yield is 15.05%. P/B is 0.9x. The price therefore assumes earnings near today's depressed-margin level with no recovery in returns [Likely]. The cheap P/E partly reflects trough-ish margins and merger-inflated share count, not a mispricing [Guessing]. The mechanical fair value is negative and flagged unreliable, so it is ignored. Peer set note: CIVI was acquired by SM, so its data may be stale and the peer median is soft [Likely]. Shares trade 18.4% below the 50-day average and 2.7% below the 200-day, with RSI at 26.8.

## 4. What would prove this wrong
- Net debt / EBITDA (fact sheet quality table) rises above 2.5x from 1.84x TTM.
- Operating margin (TTM, income-statement trailing page) falls below 20% from 26.9%.
- ROIC (TTM, ratios trailing page) falls below 6% from 8.7%.
- FCF margin (TTM, cash-flow trailing page) turns negative.

## 5. Capital allocation and cash conversion
Management is using operating cash flow and divestiture proceeds to repay debt: it redeemed the 2026 and 2027 notes, repurchased higher-coupon 2028 notes and has no senior maturities until mid-2028, with no revolver borrowings (source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/893538/000089353826000147/sm-20260930.htm) [Certain]. Buybacks and a dividend resumed alongside deleveraging (source: https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/sm-energy-reports-third-quarter-2026-results-302928401.html) [Likely]. FCF conversion is 111.6% TTM. The merger issued 124 million shares, 52 percent of the post-close total [company-reported] (https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/893538/000089353826000147/sm-20260930.htm); shares are up 71.68% YoY, so per-share gains depend on synergies [Certain].

## 6. Unusual items
Merger integration risk language was removed from the latest 10-Q, since the deal closed on 30 January 2026 [Certain]. Short interest is 6.57% of float, up 7.1% on the prior month. Institutions own 95.9%. Beta is 0.79.
