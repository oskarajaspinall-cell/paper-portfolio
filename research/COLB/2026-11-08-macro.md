## Assessment
**Weight: secondary.** COLB is a US regional bank with no material foreign exposure [Certain]. Its multiple depends on credit and risk appetite more than on the rate level.

**Chain:** credit spreads / risk appetite -> bank equity risk premium and expected credit losses -> P/B multiple. Second link: curve slope -> NIM (4.11% last quarter, per the research note).

**Facts (FRED, 2026-11-05):**
- HY spread 3.48%, +0.45pp in 1m (https://fred.stlouisfed.org/graph/fredgraph.csv?id=BAMLH0A0HYM2).
- VIX 28.18, +13.2 in 1m (https://fred.stlouisfed.org/graph/fredgraph.csv?id=VIXCLS).
- 10y 4.84% (-0.43pp 1m), 2y 4.46% (-0.33pp 1m), so the curve is +0.38pp (https://fred.stlouisfed.org/graph/fredgraph.csv?id=DGS10, https://fred.stlouisfed.org/graph/fredgraph.csv?id=DGS2).
- Fed funds 3.88%, unchanged in 1m (https://fred.stlouisfed.org/graph/fredgraph.csv?id=DFF).

**Market pricing (proxy):** the 2y sits well above fed funds, so no near-term cuts look priced; that is easing as the 2y fell. **(inference)**

**Inference:** the VIX jump and spread widening are the change. A 3.48% spread is still moderate: a risk-off shock, not credit stress. The positive curve supports NIM. The exposure is western-US commercial and CRE credit at 1.2x book. Beta is 0.68.

**Surprise direction:** wider spreads or a flatter curve hurt; spreads back near 3.0% help.

**Tilt:** toward bear, small. The fundamentals (NIM recovery, synergies) are unchanged, but the risk-premium shock raises the odds of a lower multiple.
