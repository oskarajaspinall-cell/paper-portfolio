# Copart, Inc. (CPRT) — Core initiation — 2026-10-08

## 1. Business
Copart runs an online auction marketplace for salvage and used vehicles, sold mainly on consignment for insurance companies (~79% of vehicles processed in FY2026) [Certain] (source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/900075/000119312526405731/cprt-20260731.htm). It earns seller/buyer fees (selling, transportation, title, storage, bidding) rather than owning inventory, except limited principal purchases in the UK, Germany and Spain [Certain] (same source). It operates in ~11 countries via its VB3 virtual-bidding platform [Certain] (source: https://www.copart.com/).

## 2. Quality
Returns are high but clearly declining: TTM ROIC is 28.4%, down from 35.2% in FY2022; ROCE 17.7%, down from 28.2%; ROE 16.2%, down from 26.7% [Certain] (source: fact sheet, ratios page). Gross margin is flat near 45%, but operating margin has slipped from 39.3% to 35.4% over the same period [Certain]. FCF margin and conversion are improving (27.2% and 85.4% TTM) and the balance sheet is net cash, net debt/EBITDA -2.36x [Certain]. The moat looks real: long-dated insurance-company contracts, a 100+ inspection-station yard network and the VB3 bidding platform are costly to replicate [Likely] (source: SEC 10-K above). But the 10-K itself flags that a limited number of large sellers have "collectively accounted for a substantial portion of revenues," so the margin decline could already reflect insurer pricing pressure or fee concessions rather than mix alone [Guessing].

## 3. Valuation
At 17.2x P/E and 10.8x EV/EBITDA, CPRT trades below its own 5-year minimum on every multiple tracked (P/E, EV/EBITDA, EV/Sales, P/FCF, P/B are all 5-10% under their 5-year floor) [Certain] (source: fact sheet). Versus peers KAR, RBA and KMX it is cheaper on P/E (-45%) and EV/EBITDA (-28%) but more expensive on EV/Sales (+35%) and P/FCF (+57%), which reflects its superior margins rather than a genuine discount [Certain]. The fair-value estimate (base $28.64, blended 67% DCF/33% EV/EBITDA) implies only +7.6% upside to the $26.62 close, with bear/bull of -24.3%/+21.4% — a roughly symmetric payoff, not a deep-value re-rating case [Certain]. The reverse DCF embeds 13.2% year-1 revenue growth fading to terminal growth, a reasonable but not undemanding bar given decelerating ROIC [Likely].

## 4. What would prove this wrong
- ROIC (ratios page) falls below 25% (vs. 28.4% TTM), confirming the 5-year return decline is structural rather than cyclical mix.
- Operating margin (income statement) falls below 34% (vs. 35.4% TTM), pointing to competitive or insurer pricing pressure on fees.
- Net debt/EBITDA (ratios page) turns positive after the ACV Auctions cash acquisition closes, signalling the balance-sheet buffer is gone.
- Insurance-company vehicle mix or a major-seller loss disclosed in a future 10-K "significant customers" section, evidencing share shift away from Copart.

## 5. Capital allocation and cash conversion
FCF conversion is improving (85.4% TTM vs 77.0% in FY2022) and the balance sheet remains net cash (-2.36x net debt/EBITDA) [Certain] (source: fact sheet). Capital is being redeployed into the pending ~$1.9bn all-cash tender offer for ACV Auctions at $10.50/share, intended to extend Copart beyond insurance salvage into the broader used/whole-car market [Certain] (source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/900075/000119312526405731/cprt-20260731.htm; https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20261008309482/en/Copart-Announces-Extension-of-Tender-Offer-to-Acquire-ACV/). The offer has been extended repeatedly since September 17 without closing, a soft signal on tender acceptance or regulatory timing worth watching before the deal's guided year-end-2026 close is assumed [Likely] (source: Business Wire link above).

## 6. Unusual items
Short interest is modest and falling (4.02% of float, -10.2% month-on-month) and institutional ownership is high (87.1%), so this is not a crowded-short or ownership-risk situation [Certain] (source: fact sheet). The ACV tender offer's repeated extensions and the attendant "shareholder notice" investigation press releases concern ACV Auctions, not Copart, and read as deal-process noise rather than a Copart-specific legal issue [Likely] (source: fact sheet news section).
