# Indus Towers (NSE:INDUSTOWER) — Core initiation — 2026-11-09
## 1. Business
Indian telecom tower lessor: charges mobile operators rent per tenancy on towers it owns, with fees contracted to carriers. [Likely] Revenue is overwhelmingly India; a recent push into Africa is at an early stage (source: https://stockanalysis.com/quote/nse/INDUSTOWER/transcripts/716786-q2-26-27/). Customer concentration (Bharti Airtel, Vodafone Idea) could not be verified: company website fetch was blocked [data unavailable]. Home-market revenue; no foreign country is yet ~10% of revenue [Guessing].

## 2. Quality
Gross margin is stable (56.0% TTM vs 55.3% FY2022), consistent with contracted, infrastructure-like economics [Likely]. Moat: towers are costly to replicate and tenancies are sticky, and sharing lowers carriers' cost [Likely]. But returns are volatile and falling: ROIC 14.1% TTM vs 22.2% FY2025, and 19.9% a year earlier; operating margin 32.1% vs 43.2% a year earlier; FCF margin 23.6% vs 36.8% (source: https://stockanalysis.com/quote/nse/INDUSTOWER/financials/ratios/?p=trailing). FY2025 looks inflated by one-offs (a prior-period write-back is mentioned in the Q3 FY26 call; https://stockanalysis.com/quote/nse/INDUSTOWER/transcripts/403726-q3-25-26/) [Likely], so the normalised level is nearer 15% ROIC [Guessing]. Net debt/EBITDA 1.17x is comfortable; Piotroski F 6 (https://stockanalysis.com/quote/nse/INDUSTOWER/statistics/).

## 3. Valuation
P/E 14.2x (53% of 5y range, median 13.0x); EV/EBITDA 6.7x (29% of range); P/FCF 12.9x (26%); FCF yield 7.76%. Versus peers (Bharti, Tata Comm, AMT, CCI) it trades 40-67% cheaper, but US tower REITs carry different growth, leverage and rate regimes, so the discount overstates the gap [Likely]. Mechanical fair value base 397.09 INR vs close 381.90 (+4.0%); bear 160.22, bull 659.45: a wide, unreliable range. The reverse DCF implies 1.6% year-1 revenue growth, which is undemanding if tenancy growth continues [Likely]. Overall: fairly priced to modestly cheap, with no clear margin of safety. (source: https://stockanalysis.com/quote/nse/INDUSTOWER/statistics/)

## 4. What would prove this wrong
- ROIC (ratios page, TTM) stays below 12% for two consecutive quarters.
- Operating margin (income statement, TTM) falls below 28%.
- Net debt/EBITDA (ratios page) rises above 2.0x.
- FCF conversion (FCF/NI, cash-flow statement) falls below 80% on a TTM basis.

## 5. Capital allocation and cash conversion
FCF conversion was 110.3% TTM, with FCF/NI at 98.8% in FY2026, so earnings are cash-backed. Share count is flat (+0.10% YoY): no dilution. A dividend was recommended for FY2026 (source: https://stockanalysis.com/quote/nse/INDUSTOWER/transcripts/544183-q4-25-26/) [Certain]. Management says Africa rollouts are starting, and the Q1 call said they would not affect the India dividend (source: https://stockanalysis.com/quote/nse/INDUSTOWER/transcripts/658940-q1-26-27/) [Likely]. Capex and returns on the new market are unproven.

## 6. Unusual items
- Africa expansion is a new, riskier, lower-visibility capital deployment; the Q2 call noted rollout timing and costs remain variable (source: https://stockanalysis.com/quote/nse/INDUSTOWER/transcripts/716786-q2-26-27/) [Likely].
- Customer concentration and counterparty credit among Indian carriers is the key risk; unverified [Guessing].
- India country risk (INR, RBI policy, regulation) for the macro overlay; INR reporter, 2.0% country premium in fair value.
- Short interest [data unavailable]; beta 0.18.
