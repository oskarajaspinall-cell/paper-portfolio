# Macro overlay: Everest Group, Ltd. (EG) — 2026-10-09
## Assessment
Everest reinvests a large investment float into mostly high-grade bonds, so Treasury yields plausibly move both future net investment income (NII) and the cost of equity in the fact sheet's own CAPM fair value (discounted at the 10y Treasury, 5.28%) [Certain] (fred.stlouisfed.org/graph/fredgraph.csv?id=DGS10, 2026-10-07).

**Facts**: 10y real yield (DFII10) 2.92%, +0.61pp/3m; 10y nominal (DGS10) 5.28%, +0.72pp/3m; 10y breakeven (T10YIE) 2.35%, roughly flat (+0.12pp/3m); high-yield spread (BAMLH0A0HYM2) 3.09%, +0.39pp/3m, still well below the 5.0% stress threshold (reports/macro/2026-10-09.md, FRED, 2026-10-07/08).
**Pricing proxy (inference)**: flat breakevens with rising nominal/real yields point to a growth/policy-driven move, not an inflation surprise; 2y (4.77%) over fed funds (3.88%) implies the market still prices policy firmness, not imminent cuts **(inference)**.
**Chain**: rising real yields → higher reinvestment yield on EG's bond float (NII tailwind) and → higher discount rate (valuation headwind) — these largely offset for a reinsurer, unlike a long-duration growth name.

**Bull**: yields stay elevated, credit spreads contained → NII keeps rising, helping ROE hold near TTM 12.6% rather than reverting to the FY2022 trough, supporting the base/bull re-rating case [Likely] **(inference)**.
**Bear**: a growth scare cuts yields sharply alongside HY-spread widening → NII tailwind fades and ERP rises together, pressuring the already-depressed 0.9x P/B further [Likely] **(inference)**.
**Tilt**: the underwriting/reserve-adequacy cycle (not macro) still dominates this thesis; macro only shifts the edges of the valuation range.
