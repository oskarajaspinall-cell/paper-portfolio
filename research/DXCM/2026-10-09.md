# DexCom, Inc. (DXCM) — Core initiation — 2026-10-09

## 1. Business
DexCom designs, manufactures and sells continuous glucose monitoring (CGM) systems (G6/G7 sensors, Dexcom ONE+, and the OTC Stelo) for people with diabetes [Certain]. Revenue is overwhelmingly recurring: once a transmitter/app is set up, sensors are replaced every 10-15 days, billed mostly to insurers/health systems and increasingly direct-to-consumer via Stelo [Certain]. Growth comes from new patient starts (US and international), expanded payer coverage, and new product launches [Likely].

## 2. Quality
TTM ROIC is 44.5% and ROCE 27.8%, both up sharply from 18.5%/6.3% in FY2021 (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/dxcm/financials/ratios/) [Certain]. FCF margin is 28.3% with FCF conversion of 140.5% of net income TTM, on a net-cash balance sheet (net debt/EBITDA -0.39x) (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/dxcm/financials/cash-flow-statement/, https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/dxcm/financials/balance-sheet/) [Certain]. The US CGM market is effectively a duopoly with Abbott's Libre; DXCM's moat rests on FDA-cleared sensor accuracy, integration with insulin pumps/apps, and payer/physician-embedded prescribing habits that create real switching costs [Likely]. Gross margin has slipped from 68.6% (FY2021) to 62.5% TTM, -8.5pp over five years (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/dxcm/financials/income-statement/) [Certain] — a sign that competitive and mix pressure (Libre, lower-margin Stelo) is real, even as operating leverage still lifts operating margin to 22.9% TTM [Certain]. DexCom's Q2 2026 10-Q discloses pending securities class-action, derivative-action and G6/G7 product class-action litigation for which it "cannot reasonably estimate the ultimate outcome" (source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1093557/000109355726000143/dxcm-20260630.htm, filed 2026-07-30) [Certain] — an unquantified tail risk.

## 3. Valuation
DXCM's current multiples sit near the bottom of their own five-year range (P/E 33.3x vs a 31.0x/88.5x/239.9x five-year min/median/max; EV/EBITDA 22.3x barely above its 21.6x five-year min; P/FCF 22.7x is actually below its 24.0x five-year min) (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/dxcm/financials/ratios/, https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/dxcm/statistics/) [Certain] — this reflects hypergrowth-era multiples compressing as growth matured, not a bargain versus history. Against peers (ABT, PODD, MDT, TNDM) DXCM trades at a premium on every multiple: P/E +31%, EV/EBITDA +41%, EV/Sales +88%, P/B +146% [Certain]. The fact sheet's own-history fair value is $64.89 base (-23.1% vs the $84.41 close), with bear/bull of $11.69/$145.97 (source: fact sheet fair-value section) [Certain]. The reverse DCF shows the price implies roughly 28.7% year-1 revenue growth fading to terminal [Certain] — well above the growth DexCom actually delivered in its most recent reported quarter per its 10-Q (source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1093557/000109355726000143/dxcm-20260630.htm) [Likely]. The gap between priced-in and delivered growth is the central valuation problem.

## 4. What would prove this wrong
- Gross margin (income statement page) falls below 58%, an acceleration of the five-year decline confirming the Libre/Stelo mix pressure is structural rather than transitory.
- ROIC (ratios page) falls below 30% TTM, reversing the five-year expansion and showing returns compressing as competition intensifies.
- Net debt/EBITDA (ratios page) rises above 1.0x, showing buybacks are being funded by debt rather than free cash flow.
- The pending G6/G7 product class-action or securities class-action litigation (SEC filings) results in a quantified, disclosed liability, crystallizing a currently unquantified tail risk.

## 5. Capital allocation and cash conversion
FCF conversion is 140.5% of net income TTM (FCF margin 28.3% vs net margin 20.1%) (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/dxcm/financials/cash-flow-statement/, https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/dxcm/financials/income-statement/) [Certain], funding a $600m buyback of 8.6m shares in H1 2026 under a new $1.0bn authorization through June 2027, after terminating a prior $750m program with $250m unused (source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1093557/000109355726000143/dxcm-20260630.htm) [Certain]. Shares outstanding fell 2.61% y/y (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/dxcm/statistics/) [Certain]. The balance sheet is net cash (net debt/EBITDA -0.39x TTM) [Certain], leaving room to keep funding buybacks and R&D (next-generation sensors, Stelo expansion) without added leverage risk [Likely].

## 6. Unusual items
DexCom's 10-Q discloses pending securities class-action, derivative and G6/G7 product class-action litigation with an outcome it says it cannot reasonably estimate (source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1093557/000109355726000143/dxcm-20260630.htm, filed 2026-07-30) [Certain]. Short interest is 4.59% of float, down 2.1% versus the prior month (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/dxcm/statistics/) [Certain] — not elevated. Recent governance/management changes: Jereme Sylvain promoted to COO (2026-09-14) and Glenn Boehnlein appointed to the board (2026-09-10), both without disclosed strategic implications (source: https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260914303235/en/Dexcom-Promotes-Jereme-Sylvain-to-Chief-Operating-Officer/, https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260910382308/en/Dexcom-Appoints-Glenn-Boehnlein-to-Board-of-Directors/) [Certain].
