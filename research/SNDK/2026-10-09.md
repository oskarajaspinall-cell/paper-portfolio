# Sandisk Corporation (SNDK) — Core initiation — 2026-10-09

## 1. Business
Sandisk makes NAND flash storage: enterprise/datacenter SSDs for AI and cloud workloads, client SSDs and embedded storage for PCs/automotive, and retail cards/USB drives [Certain] (source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2023554/000162828026057406/sndk-20260703.htm). No customer is >10% of revenue and 82% of sales are international [Certain] (same source). It is vertically integrated through Flash Ventures, a 49.9%-owned joint venture with Kioxia operating eight Japanese fabs that supply roughly half its NAND wafers at cost-plus pricing [Certain] (same source).

## 2. Quality
NAND is a commodity, capital-intensive, cyclical market; rivals (Samsung, SK Hynix, Micron, Kioxia, YMTC) are similarly vertically integrated, so competition is on scale/node, not brand or switching costs [Certain] (source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2023554/000162828026057406/sndk-20260703.htm). SNDK's own 5-year record shows the lack of pricing power: operating margin went +12.5% (FY22) → -21.3% (FY23) → +61.6% (TTM), and ROE -17.6% → +91.6% over the same window (source: fact sheet, RA/IS) — a swing driven by the NAND cycle, not a moat [Certain]. TTM figures (ROIC 102%, gross margin 71.5%) sit far above the 5y range, a likely cycle peak [Likely]. Sandisk must fund ~half of Flash Ventures' capex/fixed costs regardless of output taken, plus $1.2bn of fixed Kioxia payments through 2029 [Certain] (same 10-K source) — commitments that do not flex down if pricing reverses. A multi-day memory sell-off (Oct 6-8) followed Samsung's disappointing preliminary results, framed by press as the AI-memory trade "cooling" [Likely] (source: https://www.investopedia.com/market-update-the-memory-trade-cools-after-samsung-preliminary-results-disappoint-12164457).

## 3. Valuation
At 1,609.46, SNDK trades at EV/EBITDA 18.2x (77th pct of its 5y range 11.2x-20.2x) and P/B 15.2x (92nd pct of 0.7x-16.4x) — near the top of its own cycle history [Certain] (source: fact sheet). P/E 21.8x is 35% above the 3-peer median 16.2x (MU, WDC, STX); EV/EBITDA is 38% below peer median (29.4x) and EV/Sales roughly in line (11.3x vs 11.4x); peers are in the same up-cycle, so this dispersion likely reflects where each sits in its own margin cycle, not a clean discount [Guessing]. The fact sheet's DCF/EV-EBITDA blend puts base fair value at 1,415.29 (-12.1% vs last close) and bull at 2,126.61 (+32.1%); bear is deeply negative, a methodology artifact of a loss-making 5y FCF base, not a literal estimate [Certain]. The embedded reverse DCF requires 14.9% year-1 revenue growth fading to terminal — the market is pricing continued peak-cycle growth/margins, not mean reversion [Likely].

## 4. What would prove this wrong
- Gross margin (income statement) falls below 50% — still above the pre-cycle 5y average but a clear sign the up-cycle is reversing
- ROIC (ratios page) falls below 30% — still far above the 5y average but signals normalization has begun
- FCF margin (cash flow/income statement) turns negative, as it was in FY2023-FY2025
- Net debt/EBITDA (balance sheet/income statement) rises back above 1.0x, driven by Flash Ventures capex calls

## 5. Capital allocation and cash conversion
FCF conversion is strong on a TTM basis (FCF/NI 100.5%, FCF margin 56.8%) and the balance sheet shows net cash (net debt/EBITDA -0.36x to -0.50x) [Certain] (source: fact sheet). Shares outstanding rose 6.9% YoY [Certain] (source: fact sheet), a dilution headwind. The Flash Ventures structure obligates Sandisk to fund roughly half of JV capex and fixed costs regardless of demand, plus $1.2bn of further fixed payments to Kioxia through 2029 [Certain] (source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2023554/000162828026057406/sndk-20260703.htm) — capital commitments that do not shrink if NAND pricing reverses.

## 6. Unusual items
Short interest is 3.89% of float, down 6.3% month-over-month, with 80.5% institutional ownership [Certain] (source: fact sheet). The FY2023-FY2025 net losses mean the fair-value model's bear case produces a negative DCF output — a methodology artifact of a loss-making 5y base, not a literal valuation [Certain] (source: fact sheet).
