# ADT Inc. (ADT) — Core initiation — 2026-10-18
## 1. Business
ADT is the largest U.S. residential/small-business security-monitoring company, installing alarm and smart-home hardware and billing a monthly recurring monitoring fee [Certain]. It served approximately 6.1 million subscribers as of June 30, 2026 [company-reported] (source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1703056/000170305626000100/adt-20260630.htm). Roughly 40% of new subscribers now take outright equipment ownership (ADT+/ADT Blu DIY) rather than the traditional company-owned-equipment model [company-reported] (source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1703056/000170305626000100/adt-20260630.htm), with monitoring (RMR) remaining the profit engine [Certain].

## 2. Quality
Recurring-revenue economics are strong and improving: TTM FCF margin is 31.2%, up from 18.7% in FY2021; operating margin is 25.4%; ROIC/ROCE have risen to 8.3%/9.0% from near zero [Certain]. The ADT brand, an installed hardware base and multi-year contracts create real switching costs — a durable but not unassailable moat [Likely]: DIY/self-monitoring entrants and telecom bundlers are pressuring the category, prompting ADT's own push into DIY via ADT Blu on Walmart.com (source: https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/09/14/3361156/0/en/adt-brings-adt-blu-to-walmart-com-expanding-availability-through-one-of-the-nation-s-largest-online-retailers.html) [Likely]. Gross customer attrition rose to 13.1% from 12.8% a year earlier, driven by non-payment disconnects — a macro-sensitive base [company-reported] (source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1703056/000170305626000100/adt-20260630.htm). Margin and ROIC gains partly reflect the FY2025 multifamily divestiture and buybacks (shares outstanding -7.45% y/y) rather than organic growth [Likely]. Net debt/EBITDA has fallen from 5.05x (FY2021) to 2.84x (FY2025) but remains high for the sector [Certain].

## 3. Valuation
At 9.5x P/E, 4.7x EV/EBITDA, 3.1x P/FCF and 1.4x P/B, ADT trades below its own 5-year minimum on every multiple and 31-84% below the REZI/ALLE/JCI/CMCSA peer median; FCF yield is 32% [Certain]. The reverse DCF implies the market is pricing in a perpetual revenue decline of roughly 24% a year [Certain], far more pessimistic than the modest recent organic growth and raised full-year guidance the company has reported [Likely]. The model's base fair value of $36.72 (+434.6% vs. the $6.87 close) is not a credible near-term target: it reflects how punitively the market discounts this cash flow given $7.4 billion of total long-term debt [company-reported] (source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1703056/000170305626000100/adt-20260630.htm) and an Altman Z-score of 0.65 (distress zone) [Certain] — risk a plain DCF does not price. The discount looks overdone against peers but is not free money; it compensates for real balance-sheet risk [Likely].

## 4. What would prove this wrong
- Net debt/EBITDA (ratios page) rises above 3.2x, reversing the deleveraging trend from FY2025's 2.84x.
- Gross customer attrition (10-Q MD&A) rises above 14.5%, signalling accelerating subscriber losses beyond Q2 2026's 13.1%.
- TTM FCF margin (cash flow + income statement) falls below 20%, down from the current 31.2%, signalling the ADT+ outright-sales mix shift is compressing cash conversion.
- Altman Z-score (statistics page) falls further below 0.65, signalling rising distress risk despite management's stated covenant compliance.

## 5. Capital allocation and cash conversion
Cash conversion is excellent: TTM FCF/NI conversion is 263.4% [Certain]. Management is returning capital via buybacks and dividends while continuing to delever, consistent with shares outstanding down 7.45% y/y [Certain]. Current maturities of long-term debt are $240.5 million against total long-term debt of $7.4 billion, and management states compliance with all financial covenants [company-reported] (source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1703056/000170305626000100/adt-20260630.htm). Buybacks are funded from free cash flow rather than fresh borrowing, consistent with net debt/EBITDA continuing to fall [Likely].

## 6. Unusual items
Altman Z-score of 0.65 sits in the distress zone, typical of this post-LBO, asset-heavy, highly-leveraged sector but still a genuine risk flag; it is partly offset by falling leverage and stated covenant compliance [Likely]. Short interest is modest at 4.52% of float and fell 7.4% month-over-month [Certain]. Institutional ownership is high at 80.8% [Certain]. No governance, legal or regulatory items appear in the SEC filing delta or recent news flow beyond the items above.
