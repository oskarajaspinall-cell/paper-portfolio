# Gree Electric Appliances, Inc. of Zhuhai (SHE:000651) — Core initiation — 2026-10-19
## 1. Business
Gree makes and sells home air-conditioners (its core category, with other appliances — refrigerators, washers — and smart/industrial equipment) [Certain], mainly in China with export volume, through a large dealer/distribution network it has historically controlled tightly (source: stockanalysis.com overview, https://stockanalysis.com/quote/she/000651/) [Certain].

## 2. Quality
Gree is one of China's two dominant residential AC makers (with Midea) [Likely], giving it scale and distribution advantages competitors struggle to replicate. TTM ROCE 18.3% and ROE 19.0% are both roughly flat over five years (ratios page), and gross/operating/net margins have all risen (+5.7pp/+5.8pp/+4.7pp FY2021→TTM) — evidence the moat is holding, not eroding [Likely] (source: factsheet quality table, income statement). The one caution: TTM ROIC of 41.2% is down sharply from 210.2% in FY2021 (-163.4pp), a much bigger fall than ROCE/ROE show, implying the invested-capital base has grown faster than returns even as margins improved [Likely] — worth monitoring but not yet a margin problem. FCF conversion is strong (127% of NI TTM) and the balance sheet carries net cash (net debt/EBITDA -3.27x), reducing the risk that a competitor can out-invest Gree [Certain].

## 3. Valuation
At 7.9x P/E, Gree sits near the top of its own 5-year range (5.8x–8.7x, 74th percentile) but 25% below the 3-peer median (10.5x) [Certain]. EV/EBITDA (3.3x vs 5y range 2.4x–5.9x, peer median 9.5x, -65%) and P/FCF (6.2x vs peer median 13.8x, -55%) show a wider peer discount than P/E; FCF yield is 16.05% [Certain]. The reverse DCF implies the price assumes revenue shrinks -28.7% in year 1 before fading to terminal growth — more pessimistic than the flat-to-rising margins in section 2 suggest [Likely]. Base fair value (72.98 CNY, DCF 67%/EV-EBITDA 33%) is +84.8% above the 39.49 CNY close; bear case is -56.6%, reflecting real uncertainty (source: factsheet fair-value table). The peer set is thin (n=2-3, including Whirlpool, a lower-margin Western peer), so peer-discount figures should be weighted less than the own-history position [Guessing].

## 4. What would prove this wrong
- Gross margin (income statement page) falls back below 27%, reversing the five-year rising trend and signalling the AC duopoly is breaking down.
- ROCE (ratios page) drops below 15%, showing the ROIC decline (section 2) has spread into capital efficiency on the measure that has so far stayed flat.
- Net debt/EBITDA (balance sheet/ratios page) turns positive (net cash position reverses), indicating capital allocation or demand stress.
- Revenue (income statement page) declines for two or more consecutive quarters beyond normal seasonal patterns.

## 5. Capital allocation and cash conversion
FCF conversion has averaged well above 100% of net income over FY2022-FY2025 (92%-176%) and is 127% TTM, so earnings are being realised as cash, not just booked [Certain]. The balance sheet is net cash (net debt/EBITDA -3.27x TTM), giving flexibility without needing external funding [Certain]. Shares outstanding rose 1.16% YoY — mild dilution, not buybacks [Certain]. The fact sheet does not carry a dividend or payout figure, so capital return to shareholders is [data unavailable].

## 6. Unusual items
Altman Z-score is 1.75, inside the conventional "distress" zone (<1.81), despite the net-cash balance sheet shown above — likely a function of the model's equity/market-cap term for a low-multiple stock rather than real distress risk, but it should be watched alongside the ROIC decline in section 2 [Guessing]. Piotroski F-score of 7 (of 9) supports fundamental quality. Institutional ownership is low (15.7%) and insider ownership minimal (2.31%), typical for a China A-share but a reminder of thinner informed-holder support than a US/European peer (source: factsheet statistics page). No company-specific catalyst in the listed headlines changes the thesis; the most recent (filings only, plus a general SCMP piece on Chinese exporters) carries no decision-relevant, company-specific information [Likely].
