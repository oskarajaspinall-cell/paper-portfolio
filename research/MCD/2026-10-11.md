# McDonald's Corporation (MCD) — Core initiation — 2026-10-11

## 1. Business
McDonald's is a 95%-franchised quick-service restaurant system [Certain] (source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/63908/000006390826000073/mcd-20260630.htm). It earns rent and royalties from franchisees (62% of Q2-26 revenue, $4.4bn) plus sales at company-operated units (36%, $2.5bn) (same source). McDonald's owns much of the underlying real estate, giving it a landlord-style, high-margin royalty stream on top of the restaurant business [Certain].

## 2. Quality
Returns are high and durable: TTM ROIC 17.8% and ROCE 22.7%, both roughly flat to rising over five years, with operating margin up to 45.7% and gross margin to 57.4% [Certain] (source: fact sheet, RA/IS). The moat is scale (nearly 14,000 US units) plus owned real estate and a franchise royalty base that competitors cannot replicate quickly [Likely]. The weaker spot is cash conversion: FCF margin has fallen from 30.6% (FY21) to 28.0% TTM and FCF/NI from 94% to 88% [Certain] (source: fact sheet, CF/IS), consistent with capex rising to $1.5bn in H1-26 from $1.3bn a year earlier (source: 10-Q above). Comparable sales growth in Q2-26 was positive but modest: +0.8% US, +1.5% International Operated, +1.9% Developmental Licensed (source: 10-Q above) [Certain] — a slowing, not collapsing, top line. Net debt/EBITDA of 3.6x is structurally high for the sector but stable over five years, funded by the predictable franchise royalty stream [Likely].

## 3. Valuation
Current multiples look cheap versus MCD's own five-year history: P/E 19.1x is below the 5y range (25.3x-31.2x), EV/EBITDA 14.8x below (18.6x-20.4x), P/FCF 21.4x below (28.2x-35.2x) (source: fact sheet, RA). Versus peers (YUM, SBUX, QSR, DPZ), MCD is roughly in line to slightly cheap on EV/EBITDA (-11%) and P/FCF (-2%), close to peer P/E (+6%), but far above on EV/Sales (+99%, reflecting its higher-margin, asset-heavy model, not mispricing) [Likely]. However, the fact sheet's DCF-based fair value (base $218.53; bear $172.20; bull $244.18) sits below the last close of $235.23 — base implies -7.1%, bull only +3.8% [Certain] (source: fact sheet). This de-rating reflects a genuine growth slowdown (reverse DCF implies 9.4% year-1 revenue growth needed, above recent comps), not a cheap entry point [Likely].

## 4. What would prove this wrong
- US comparable sales growth (10-Q, Item 2 MD&A) turns negative for two consecutive quarters.
- ROIC (stockanalysis.com ratios page) falls below 15% for a full fiscal year.
- FCF conversion, FCF/NI (cash-flow and income-statement pages) falls below 75% for a full fiscal year, confirming the current decline is structural rather than capex-cycle noise.
- Net debt/EBITDA (ratios page) rises above 4.5x, signalling the franchise royalty base can no longer comfortably support the balance sheet.

## 5. Capital allocation and cash conversion
H1-26 capital returns were substantial: dividends of $3.72/share ($2.6bn) and buybacks of 4.2m shares ($1.3bn) (source: 10-Q above) [Certain], funded by a FCF margin still above 25% despite the recent decline. Capex stepped up to $1.5bn in H1-26 from $1.3bn, partly reinvestment in the franchise system (e.g. a hand-breaded chicken test at 200 US sites, source: fact sheet news) [Likely]. Shares outstanding fell 0.73% YoY (source: fact sheet, ST), consistent with buybacks modestly offsetting dilution. The combination of high payout and rising capex is squeezing free cash flow headroom, worth monitoring but not yet a red flag [Likely].

## 6. Unusual items
A federal antitrust lawsuit filed the week of 2026-10-05 alleges McDonald's AI-based pricing tool lets franchisees exchange nonpublic pricing data to set menu prices; McDonald's has publicly dismissed the claim (source: fact sheet news, https://nypost.com/2026/10/01/business/mcdonalds-brushes-off-claims-its-using-ai-to-set-prices-as-big-nothingburger/) [Likely]. This is a reputational and regulatory risk — potential for franchisee friction or a consent decree — but no quantified financial exposure is yet disclosed [Guessing]. Short interest is low (1.73% of float) though it rose 10.3% month-on-month [Certain] (source: fact sheet, ST) — not yet material.
