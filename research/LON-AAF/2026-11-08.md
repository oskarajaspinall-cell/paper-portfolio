# Airtel Africa (LON:AAF) — Core initiation — 2026-11-08
## 1. Business
Pan-African mobile operator (voice, data) plus Airtel Money mobile-money, reporting in USD with prices quoted in GBX. Revenue is earned in local currencies across Nigeria, East Africa and Francophone Africa. Nigeria is the largest single country and clearly above ~10% of revenue [Likely] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/quote/lon/AAF/transcripts/707595-q2-2027/), but no primary-source share was retrievable (company site not on allowlist): share [data unavailable]. Bharti Airtel is the controlling shareholder [Likely] (source: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/05/22/airtel-india-uk-africa-investment.html).

## 2. Quality
Good returns, not yet proven through a currency shock. TTM ROCE 26.1% and ROIC 14.8% (source: https://stockanalysis.com/quote/lon/AAF/financials/ratios/?p=trailing), operating margin 33.6% rising from 30.9% a year earlier, gross margin 67.4%. [Certain] Moat: spectrum licences, network scale and distribution in markets with few rational competitors; the H1 call described Nigerian competition as intense but rational [Likely] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/quote/lon/AAF/transcripts/707595-q2-2027/). Weak spots: ROIC was 10.5% in FY2025 after 25.0% in FY2024, and net margin fell to -3.3% in FY2024, consistent with local-currency devaluation hits [Likely]. Reported H1 growth was helped by currency tailwinds that management said fade [Likely]. Piotroski F 8, Altman Z 2.26 (source: https://stockanalysis.com/quote/lon/AAF/statistics/).

## 3. Valuation
Price 277.60 GBX, down 24.2% over 6 months and 17.5% below the 200-day average (source: https://stockanalysis.com/quote/lon/AAF/history/). P/E 18.0x sits at 37% of its 5y range (median 17.7x), EV/EBITDA 5.5x (median 4.7x), P/FCF 5.3x (median 5.1x): in line with own history. Versus peers (MTN, Vodacom, Vodafone, Bharti): P/E -19%, EV/EBITDA -10%, P/FCF -39%, but P/B +35% and EV/Sales +18% (source: https://stockanalysis.com/quote/lon/AAF/statistics/). FCF yield 18.92%. The model fair value (base 1,025.37 GBX) rests on a DCF of 1,444.61 while the EV/EBITDA method gives 186.89, below the price; this spread means fair value is unreliable [Guessing]. FCF margin is falling over 12 months (36.7% to 34.8%) and capex is stepping up [Likely]. The reverse DCF implies a deep revenue decline, which looks like a Nigeria/FX risk discount rather than operating weakness [Likely].

## 4. What would prove this wrong
- Operating margin (income statement, TTM) falls below 30%.
- ROCE (ratios page, TTM) falls below 20%.
- FCF margin (cash flow statement, TTM) falls below 28%.
- Net debt / EBITDA (ratios page) rises above 2.5x.

## 5. Capital allocation and cash conversion
FCF conversion of 338.7% of net income, FCF margin 34.8%, shares down 0.66% YoY: buybacks plus a growing dividend [Likely] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/quote/lon/AAF/transcripts/707595-q2-2027/). Net debt/EBITDA 1.84x TTM, down from 2.69x in FY2025. Management flagged heavy capex including spectrum and licence renewals, which pressures FCF [Likely]. Airtel Money was listed in London in October 2026 with the parent keeping control [Likely] (source: https://www.premiumtimesng.com/business/business-news/915675-airtel-money-commences-conditional-trading-on-london-stock-exchange.html).

## 6. Unusual items
- Controlling shareholder: Bharti Airtel intends to raise its stake substantially (source: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/05/22/airtel-india-uk-africa-investment.html) [Likely]; minority and free-float liquidity risk.
- Currency and cash repatriation from Nigeria is the central macro exposure; the macro overlay should check naira, Nigerian policy rate and regulation [Likely]. A Uganda coverage-licence dispute was noted on the call [Likely].
- Fuel cost inflation squeezes margins [Likely]. Institutions own 15.5%; short interest [data unavailable].
