# Macro overlay: UDR, Inc. (UDR) — 2026-10-12
## Assessment
UDR is a leveraged, long-duration residential REIT (net debt/EBITDA 5.76x TTM [Certain], fact sheet RA) whose valuation and financing cost are mechanically tied to real yields: **real yields -> REIT discount rate/cap rate -> NAV & P/FFO multiple**, and **policy rate -> refinancing cost -> FFO growth**.

Facts: the 10y real yield (DFII10) is 2.87% (2026-10-08), up +0.44pp in ~1m and +0.56pp in ~3m; the 10y nominal yield (DGS10) is 5.22%, up +0.68pp in 3m, while 10y breakevens (T10YIE) are flat (+0.09pp) (fred.stlouisfed.org, 2026-10-08/09). The FOMC hiked 25bp to 3.75-4.00% on 2026-09-16, citing inflation "remains elevated" (federalreserve.gov, 2026-09-16); effective fed funds (DFF) is 3.88%. The 2y yield (4.75%) above fed funds is a proxy for no near-term cuts priced — "higher for longer," not a dovish pivot [Likely] **(inference)**.

Since the rate rise is almost entirely real, not inflation-expectation driven, it hits the discount-rate/cap-rate channel directly rather than being offset by higher nominal rent growth. Much of this looks already priced: UDR is down -14% over 3m and -17.6pp vs SPY, and now trades 37% below peer P/E and below its own 5y multiple range [Certain] (fact sheet). A further real-yield rise or another hike would still pressure the multiple and refinancing cost (bear); a pause/cut (e.g. at the Oct 28-29 FOMC) would likely re-rate this beaten-down, low-beta (0.71) name toward peers (bull). Fundamentals (rising ROIC/ROE, raised guidance) are solid but macro currently caps the re-rating.
