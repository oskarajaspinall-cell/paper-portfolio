## MD&A
- **Added**: APA is an independent energy company that owns subsidiaries that explore for, develop, and produce crude oil, natural gas, and natural gas liquids (NGLs). The Company’s business has oil and gas exploration, development, appraisal, and/or ongoing operations primarily in four geographic areas: the U.S., Egypt, offshore the U.K. in the North Sea (North Sea), and offshore Suriname. APA also has exploration interests in Uruguay, Alaska, and other international locations that may, over time, result in reportable discoveries and development opportunities. As a holding company, APA Corporation’s primary assets are its ownership interests in its consolidated subsidiaries.
- **Added**: Uncertainties in the global supply chain and financial markets impact oil supply and demand and contribute to commodity price volatility. These uncertainties include the impacts and duration of armed conflicts involving the U.S., Iran, Russia, Ukraine, Israel, and other parties in the Middle East, inflation, current and potential tariffs or other trade barriers, global trade policies, and disputes, and actions taken by foreign oil and gas producing nations, including OPEC+. Despite these uncertainties, the Company is focused on its longer-term objectives: (1) to remain committed to providing affordable, reliable, and responsibly produced energy; (2) to deliver top operational performance across safety, environmental responsibility, execution, and risk management measures; (3) to maintain financial discipline by managing costs, protecting the balance sheet ...
- **Added**: Since the end of 2024, the Company has set increasing targets for cost reduction efforts to drive sustainable cost savings for the long-term. This includes reducing overhead costs, improving the capital cost structure for its drilling, completions, and facility investments, and driving efficiencies of day-to-day field operating practices. The Company anticipates reaching annualized savings of $500 million by the end of 2026 as a result of these efforts.
- **Added**: •As of the date of this filing, the Company has reduced its outstanding total debt balance by $4.2 billion since year-end 2021.
- **Added**: The Company generated $3.6 billion of cash from operating activities and incurred $1.9 billion of oil and gas exploration, development, and property acquisition costs during the first nine months of 2026. The Company paid $264 million in dividends to APA common stockholders and repurchased approximately $550 million of Company common stock during the first nine months of 2026. The Company also repaid $1.2 billion of debt principal during the first nine months of 2026.
- **Added**: •Daily boe production from the Company’s U.S. assets, which increased 5 percent from the third quarter of 2025, accounted for 65 percent of the Company’s worldwide production during the third quarter of 2026. The Company averaged four drilling rigs in the Permian Basin, including three rigs in the Southern Midland Basin and one rig in the Delaware Basin in the third quarter of 2026. The Company brought online 46 operated wells during the quarter. The Company’s core Permian Basin development program continues to represent a key area for the U.S. assets.
- **Added**: •The Company expects to close its acquisition of Savant Alaska, LLC during the fourth quarter of 2026, for approximately $70 million in upfront consideration, plus contingent payments tied to future development of the Company’s Alaska position. The to-be-acquired infrastructure is expected to support operations beginning with 2026-2027 exploration and appraisal activities, while enhancing future development flexibility. Upon closing, the transaction is expected to add approximately 104,000 gross acres and approximately 1,500 b/d of oil production.
- **Added**: •In Egypt, the Company averaged 12 drilling rigs and drilled nine new productive wells during the third quarter of 2026. The Company also averaged 18 workover rigs as it continues optimizing drilling and workover activity for capital efficiency. Third-quarter 2026 gross production from the Company’s Egypt assets decreased 2 percent and net production decreased 12 percent from the third quarter of 2025. Third-quarter 2026 net production was negatively impacted by higher price realizations and lower cost recovery volumes under the production sharing contract.
- **Added**: •In Egypt, the Company expects approximately one-half of its rig activities to continue to be gas-focused and anticipates continued strong performance for the remainder of the year, with realized gas prices increasing through the period.
- **Added**: The Company’s worldwide oil production decreased 13.4 Mb/d to 220.9 Mb/d during the third quarter of 2026 from the comparative prior-year period, primarily a result of natural production decline in the North Sea and net production in Egypt which was impacted by higher price realizations and lower cost recovery volumes under the merged concession agreement. These decreases were partially offset by increased drilling efficiency in the Permian Basin and the timing of sales liftings in the North Sea.
- **Added**: The Company’s worldwide natural gas production decreased 34.4 MMcf/d to 897.8 MMcf/d during the third quarter of 2026 from the comparative prior-year period, primarily a result of natural production decline in the North Sea. This decrease was partially offset by lower volume curtailments at Alpine High compared with the 2025 period, as production resumed from wells previously shut in due to uneconomic or negative pricing associated with extreme Waha basis differentials.
- **Added**: The Company’s worldwide NGL production increased 5.5 Mb/d to 79.8 Mb/d during the third quarter of 2026 from the comparative prior-year period, primarily a result of lower volume curtailments at Alpine High compared with the 2025 period, as production resumed from wells previously shut in due to extreme negative price basis differentials in the Permian Basin. The increase in NGL production was partially offset by natural production decline in the North Sea.
- **Added**: The Company’s worldwide oil production decreased 14.0 Mb/d to 221.3 Mb/d in the first nine months of 2026 compared to the same prior-year period, primarily a result of natural production decline in the North Sea and the sale of non-core assets in the U.S. Net production in Egypt was negatively impacted by higher price realizations and lower cost recovery volumes under the merged concession agreement. These decreases were partially offset by increased drilling efficiency in the Permian Basin.
- **Added**: Exploration expenses increased $29 million and $40 million from the third quarter and the first nine months of 2025, respectively, primarily the result of higher dry hole expense in Egypt compared to the same prior-year periods.
