# CSPC Pharmaceutical Group Limited (HKG:1093) — Core initiation — 2026-10-19

## 1. Business
CSPC makes and sells branded generic and innovative drugs in China — oncology, cardiovascular, anti-infective, nutrition/CNS — plus a vitamin C/caffeine bulk-API business [Certain] (source: stockanalysis.com fact sheet). It is increasingly out-licensing its innovative pipeline to multinationals: AstraZeneca paid for rights to CSPC obesity/diabetes peptides (Jan 2026) and kidney-disease candidates (Jul 2026) [Likely] (sources: https://www.wsj.com/health/pharma/astrazeneca-strikes-multibillion-dollar-obesity-deal-with-chinas-cspc-81830497, https://www.reuters.com/legal/litigation/astrazeneca-signs-cspc-china-deal-experimental-kidney-disease-drugs-2026-07-02/ — headlines only, deal figures not used as data).

## 2. Quality
Returns are solid in absolute terms (TTM ROIC 31.7%, ROE 20.9%) but every profitability line has fallen steadily over five years: ROIC -22.2pp, ROCE -11.9pp, ROE -11.5pp, gross margin -10.2pp [Certain] (source: stockanalysis.com ratios/income statement). This matches China's volume-based procurement pricing pressure on legacy generics — a policy mechanism all domestic peers face, compressing the whole segment's returns, not just CSPC's [Likely]. The moat is partial: generics have little pricing power, but the innovative pipeline's optionality — two 2026 AstraZeneca licensing deals — is a scale advantage smaller peers lack [Likely]. The balance sheet is a genuine strength: net cash every year for five years (net debt/EBITDA -1.80x TTM) and rising cash conversion (FCF margin TTM 38.3%, FCF/NI 161.7%) [Certain] (source: stockanalysis.com ratios/cash flow). Some of this TTM strength likely reflects AstraZeneca upfront payments, not recurring operations [Guessing].

## 3. Valuation
The market is pricing continued margin erosion, not the AstraZeneca optionality. P/E (11.8x) sits below CSPC's own 5y range (12.0-22.3x) and at 60% below the peer median (29.5x); EV/EBITDA (7.2x) is 45% below peers; P/FCF (7.3x) is 60% below CSPC's own 5y minimum (16.0x), while P/B (2.2x) is roughly in line with its own history and peers [Certain] (source: stockanalysis.com ratios/statistics, peer medians n=4). The P/FCF gap is partly a cash-conversion artefact (see section 2) rather than pure re-rating room [Guessing]. The reverse DCF implies the price already assumes a -11.7% revenue decline in year 1 fading to terminal growth — a harsher outcome than the realised margin trend suggests [Likely] (source: fact sheet fair-value table). Base fair value is HKD12.12 (+36.5% vs the 8.88 close); bear HKD5.38 (-39.4%) (source: stockanalysis.com-derived fair value, inform-only).

## 4. What would prove this wrong
- Gross margin (income statement page) falls below 60%, extending the 5y slide past FY2025's 65.6%.
- ROIC (ratios page) falls below 10%, continuing past FY2025's 12.8% trough.
- FCF margin (cash flow/income statement) drops below 10% once licensing upfronts roll off, confirming TTM's 38.3%/161.7% conversion was non-recurring.
- Net debt/EBITDA (balance sheet) turns net-debt-positive, ending the five-year net-cash run (-1.34x to -1.90x).

## 5. Capital allocation and cash conversion
Net cash on the balance sheet every year for five years and no sign of leverage (net debt/EBITDA -1.80x TTM) [Certain]. FCF conversion exceeds net income (161.7% TTM, up from 57.6% in FY2021), and the FCF yield is 13.68% [Certain] (source: stockanalysis.com statistics/cash flow). Shares outstanding fell 1.02% YoY, consistent with modest buybacks rather than dilution [Certain] (source: stockanalysis.com statistics).

## 6. Unusual items
Reports in CNY but lists in HKD (Hong Kong); as a CNY/HKD reporter it counts toward the portfolio's China/HK country-group cap [Certain]. Insider ownership is high at 26.5%, alongside 36.2% institutional ownership, which aligns management with minority holders [Certain] (source: stockanalysis.com statistics). Short-interest level and its change are both [data unavailable] on the fact sheet.
