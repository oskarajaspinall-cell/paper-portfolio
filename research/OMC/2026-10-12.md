# Omnicom Group Inc. (OMC) — Core initiation — 2026-10-12

## 1. Business
Omnicom plans, buys and creates advertising, media, PR and precision-marketing campaigns for clients, earning agency fees/commissions largely tied to client ad spend. It closed its all-stock merger with Interpublic Group (IPG) on 2026-11-26 [Certain] (source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/29989/000002998926000019/omc-20260630.htm), creating the largest global ad holding company by billings.

## 2. Quality
Scale and client embeddedness (long-run media/data contracts, the "Omni" platform) give some switching-cost moat, but the ad-agency industry is low-differentiation among four comparable holdcos and faces recurring fee pressure [Likely]. Pre-merger returns were strong (ROIC 22.8%, ROE 32.1% in FY2024) [Certain] (source: factsheet RA), but FY2025 shows ROIC collapsing to 3.1%, ROE to 0.5% and net margin to -0.3% [Certain] (source: factsheet IS/RA) — driven by one-off integration costs ($99.5m YTD-2026 in SG&A) [Certain] (source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/29989/000002998926000019/omc-20260630.htm), not operating deterioration: TTM is already recovering (ROIC 9.9%, net margin 1.7%, FCF margin 10.7%) [Likely]. Q2-2026 organic growth was 6.1%, adjusted EBITDA growth 20.4%, and guidance was raised [Likely] (source: factsheet news, transcript). Leverage is contained: net debt/EBITDA 2.20x TTM, and the credit-facility ratio was 2.4x vs a 3.5x covenant, in compliance [Certain] (same SEC source). No synergy target is disclosed [Certain] (same source), so integration upside is unproven.

## 3. Valuation
P/E of 49.5x looks extreme vs its own 5y range (11.1x–12.6x) and peers (15.9x median), but this is an artifact of merger-depressed TTM earnings, not a re-rating [Likely]. On cash-based multiples the stock is cheap: EV/EBITDA 7.9x sits near the bottom of its 5y range (7.8x–10.7x) and below the peer median of 8.5x; P/FCF 8.8x is below both its 5y range (9.1x–19.6x) and peers (9.8x); FCF yield is 11.4% [Certain] (source: factsheet). Base fair value of $147.22 (+92.5% vs the $76.48 close) implies a reverse-DCF revenue decline of -12.2% in year one fading to terminal growth — far more pessimistic than the 4.5-5% organic growth guidance [Likely] (source: factsheet, transcript), so the gap looks overstated; even the bear case is only -16.9% below the close, giving downside protection. The market is pricing OMC on depressed GAAP earnings while cash generation and EBITDA already show integration tracking [Likely].

## 4. What would prove this wrong
- Net debt/EBITDA (ratios page, site definition) rises above the 3.5x covenant threshold or trends toward it without a stated deleveraging plan.
- TTM ROIC (ratios page) fails to continue recovering off the 3.1% FY2025 trough over the next two quarters, signalling integration costs/dis-synergies are structural, not one-off.
- Organic revenue growth guidance (earnings releases/transcripts) is cut below the 4.5-5% range given at the Q2-2026 call, indicating client attrition from the merger.
- FCF margin (cash-flow/income-statement pages) falls back toward its FY2022 low (5.9%) rather than holding the FY2025/TTM improvement (16.1%/10.7%).

## 5. Capital allocation and cash conversion
FCF margin improved to 16.1% in FY2025 and 10.7% TTM despite the merger noise, and FCF yield is 11.4% [Certain] (source: factsheet CF/ST). Shares outstanding rose 27.3% YoY, almost entirely stock issued to IPG holders in the all-stock deal — real dilution already reflected in the current share count and multiples [Certain] (source: factsheet ST). Net debt is ~$6.7bn with $10.0bn gross debt and $3.3bn cash as of 2026-06-30, leverage comfortably inside the covenant [Certain] (source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/29989/000002998926000019/omc-20260630.htm).

## 6. Unusual items
Short interest is elevated at 9.6% of float and rose 2.6% in the latest month [Likely] (source: factsheet ST) — consistent with skepticism about integration/earnings quality rather than a company-specific red flag. Institutional ownership is reported at 109.5% of float (a data artifact of overlapping institutional filings, common for large-cap stocks) [Guessing]. Altman Z-score of 1.23 is in the "distress zone" for the generic model, but that model is poorly calibrated for asset-light, high-receivables agency businesses and should be read alongside the comfortable 2.4x covenant leverage above [Likely].
