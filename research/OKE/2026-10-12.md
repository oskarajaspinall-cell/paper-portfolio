# ONEOK, Inc. (OKE) — Core initiation — 2026-10-12
## 1. Business
ONEOK operates a ~60,000-mile US pipeline network across four segments — NGL, Refined Products & Crude, Gas Gathering & Processing, and Gas Pipelines — moving natural gas, NGLs, crude and refined products from basin to Gulf Coast [Likely] (source: https://www.oneok.com/en/about-us). Management says revenue is "primarily fee-based," limiting direct commodity-price exposure [Likely] (source: https://www.oneok.com/en/about-us).

## 2. Quality
Returns are respectable but eroding: ROIC fell from 9.8% (FY21) to 8.4% TTM, ROE from 24.9% to 16.3% [Certain] (source: stockanalysis.com ratios page). Margins improved (operating +1.6pp, gross +4.6pp over 5y) [Certain], but FCF conversion nearly halved (123%→79.5% TTM) [Certain] (source: stockanalysis.com cash-flow/income-statement). Net debt/EBITDA is 4.28x, high for midstream but stable [Certain]. Altman Z of 1.55 flags balance-sheet fragility; F-score 6/9 is adequate [Certain] (source: stockanalysis.com statistics page). The moat is real — pipelines are natural monopolies with high replacement cost and long contracts — but it isn't lifting returns, and growth is now acquisition-led rather than organic. The $4.425bn Brazos Midland acquisition (closed 10/6) is funded by a $9bn Apollo Class B minority investment plus ~$5bn debt paydown, targeting ~3.25x 2027 leverage and ~$80m year-1 synergies [Likely] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/oke/transcripts/741785-m-a-announcement/). Apollo's capital sits senior to common equity (capped at 7% IRR, 9 years), so integration risk stays with shareholders [Likely].

## 3. Valuation
At 89.80 USD, OKE trades on 15.5x P/E, 11.7x EV/EBITDA, 19.5x P/FCF — below its own 5y medians (17.1x/12.6x/17.2x) and well below the peer median (24.2x/14.2x/27.2x across WMB, KMI, ET, TRGP) [Certain] (source: stockanalysis.com ratios/statistics). The -18% to -57% peer discount likely reflects OKE's higher leverage and the Apollo structure's complexity, not business quality. Mechanical fair value (DCF 67%/EV-EBITDA 33%) is 104.43 USD base (+16.3%), bear 70.73 USD (-21.2%); the 343.10 USD bull case relies on extreme assumptions and should be treated with caution [Certain] (source: fact sheet fair-value section). The reverse DCF implies only 3.3% year-1 revenue growth fading to terminal — modest against management's guided ~20%/year Midland volume growth 2027-2031 [Likely] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/oke/transcripts/741785-m-a-announcement/). Base-case upside plus a below-peer multiple on a fee-based, larger asset base supports entry.

## 4. What would prove this wrong
- Net debt/EBITDA (ratios page) rises above 5.0x on a trailing basis, signalling the Apollo-funded deleveraging plan is not working
- FCF conversion (FCF/NI, cash-flow & income-statement pages) continues falling below 70%, indicating earnings quality is deteriorating faster than the fee-based model should allow
- ROIC (ratios page) falls below 7%, showing the acquisition-led growth strategy is destroying rather than creating returns
- Operating margin (income statement) falls below 15%, undermining the "primarily fee-based" insulation thesis

## 5. Capital allocation and cash conversion
Growth is now acquisition-led and financed via structured minority equity (Apollo) rather than retained cash flow: shares outstanding rose 4.6% YoY, partly funding growth/distributions [Certain] (source: stockanalysis.com statistics page). FCF margin TTM is 7.4%, down from a 5y high of 16.0% (FY23), and FCF conversion of 79.5% TTM shows cash generation lagging reported earnings [Certain] (source: stockanalysis.com cash-flow/income-statement pages). The Apollo structure is designed to let OKE deleverage (~$5bn debt retired) while still funding the Brazos deal, targeting 3.25x leverage by 2027 [Likely] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/oke/transcripts/741785-m-a-announcement/).

## 6. Unusual items
Short interest is low (4.34% of float) but rose 8.8% in the past month, worth monitoring but not yet material [Certain] (source: stockanalysis.com statistics page). Institutional ownership is high at 82.2%, insider ownership negligible at 0.18% [Certain] (source: stockanalysis.com statistics page). The Apollo Class B structure is non-standard: it is structurally senior to common equity at the operating level, which is a genuine (if capped) claim ahead of shareholders during the 7%-IRR period [Likely] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/oke/transcripts/741785-m-a-announcement/).
