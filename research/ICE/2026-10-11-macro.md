# Macro overlay: Intercontinental Exchange, Inc. (ICE) — 2026-10-11

## Assessment
Chain: rising long-term/real yields raise ICE's discount rate on a long-duration, 2.90x net-debt/EBITDA franchise **(inference)**, and higher mortgage rates soften loan-origination volume in Mortgage Technology [Likely]. Facts: 10y Treasury yield 5.22% (2026-10-08, +0.68pp/3m) and 10y real (TIPS) yield 2.87% (2026-10-08, +0.56pp/3m) — FRED (id=DGS10, id=DFII10) [Certain]. Breakeven inflation is flat (2.33%, -0.04pp/1m, id=T10YIE) [Certain], so the move is a real-rate/term-premium repricing, not inflation expectations [Likely]. As a policy-path proxy, the 2y yield (4.75%) sits 0.87pp above effective fed funds (3.88%, both 2026-10-08, id=DGS2/DFF), implying the market isn't pricing near-term cuts **(inference, proxy)**. ICE's own base-case DCF already uses today's 5.22% risk-free rate and still lands at $111.40 vs the $156.19 close (-28.7%; reverse DCF implies 14.6% year-1 revenue growth to justify the price) — the market is pricing durable double-digit growth that offsets the higher discount rate [Certain, source: factsheet]. ICE's own news flow (factsheet, 2026-10-05) notes ARM demand at a near four-year high "on rising mortgage rates" [Likely], consistent with softer conventional origination volume. **Bull**: yields plateau/retreat, discount rate eases, mortgage volumes stabilize — DCF fair value drifts up, narrowing the gap to price. **Bear**: yields extend higher, DCF fair value falls further below price, refinancing cost rises on the leverage, mortgage tech stays soft — multiple compression risk. **Tilt**: toward bear, moderate — the stock already prices high growth against a DCF built on today's rate; further rate rises widen that gap mechanically.
