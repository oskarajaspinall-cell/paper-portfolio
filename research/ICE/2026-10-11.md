# Intercontinental Exchange, Inc. (ICE) — Core initiation — 2026-10-11

## 1. Business
ICE operates financial-market infrastructure across three segments [Certain]: Exchanges (NYSE equities, ICE energy/financial futures and derivatives — transaction and clearing fees) [Certain], Fixed Income & Data Services (bond pricing, analytics, index/data subscriptions) [Certain], and Mortgage Technology (loan origination/servicing software — per-transaction and subscription fees) [Certain] (source: https://www.ice.com).

## 2. Quality
ROIC has risen to 8.6% TTM from 7.0% FY2021; ROCE is 10.3% TTM; operating margin is stable near 50-52%; FCF margin is 48.1% TTM with FCF conversion of 125.8% of net income [Certain] (source: factsheet). This points to a durable, capital-light franchise: exchange network effects, regulatory licenses, data lock-in and mortgage-tech switching costs make share hard to take away [Likely]. Net margin's apparent 5y decline (56.8%→29.6-33.4%) reflects a one-off FY2021 gain, not deterioration [Likely] (source: factsheet).

The offset is leverage: net debt/EBITDA is 2.90x TTM, down from a 4.51x FY2023 peak but still elevated for an exchange [Certain] (source: factsheet). ICE's website states it has agreed to acquire fixed-income peer MarketAxess (MKTX, also a valuation peer here) [Likely]; deal terms and leverage impact are undisclosed on any reachable allowlisted source, so dilution risk is unquantified (source: https://www.ice.com).

## 3. Valuation
ICE screens cheap against its own 5y range and peers (CME, NDAQ, CBOE, MKTX): P/E 22.1x is at the 15th percentile of its 5y range and 7% below peer median; EV/EBITDA 16.0x is below its 5y minimum and 7% below peers; P/FCF 17.3x is near its 5y low and 32% below peers; P/B 3.0x is 35% below peers [Certain] (source: factsheet). Only EV/Sales (10.1x) is richer than peers (+17%) [Certain].

This conflicts with the DCF: base fair value is $111.40 versus the $156.19 close (-28.7%); the reverse DCF needs 14.6% year-1 revenue growth fading to terminal to justify the price [Certain] (source: factsheet) — a high bar for a mature exchange. The gap likely reflects the DCF penalizing leverage more than the multiples do, or pricing in M&A-driven scale (the pending MarketAxess deal) the DCF excludes [Likely]. Net: cheap on peers/history, but no DCF margin of safety — a quality story, not deep value.

## 4. What would prove this wrong
- ROIC (ratios page) falls below 7.0% (the FY2021 level) for two consecutive fiscal years
- Net debt/EBITDA (ratios/balance sheet) rises back above 4.5x (the FY2023 peak) without a disclosed deleveraging plan — relevant if MarketAxess financing details confirm added debt
- Operating margin (income statement) falls below 47% (the FY2024 level) for two consecutive quarters
- FCF conversion (FCF/net income) falls below 100% on a sustained basis, signalling deteriorating cash quality

## 5. Capital allocation and cash conversion
FCF margin of 48.1% and FCF conversion of 125.8% of net income fund dividends, buybacks (shares outstanding down 0.95% YoY) and M&A [Certain] (source: factsheet). Net debt/EBITDA has fallen from a 4.51x FY2023 peak to 2.90x TTM, a credible deleveraging trend [Certain] (source: factsheet), but the announced MarketAxess acquisition [Likely] (source: https://www.ice.com) could reverse it depending on financing mix, which we could not confirm from an allowlisted source.

## 6. Unusual items
Short interest rose 29.7% versus the prior month but remains a modest 1.25% of float — not yet a distress signal [Certain] (source: factsheet). Institutional ownership of 97.0% is typical for a large-cap index name, not unusual [Certain] (source: factsheet). The announced MarketAxess acquisition changes ICE's own peer set (MKTX is both a valuation peer here and a disclosed target) and is a live catalyst to monitor for financing terms and leverage impact [Likely] (source: https://www.ice.com).
