# Regency Centers Corporation (REG) — Core initiation — 2026-10-12

## 1. Business
Regency Centers owns, operates and develops open-air, grocery-anchored shopping centers in high-income suburban US markets: ~485 centers, 59m+ sq ft, 85% grocery-anchored, average household income ~$160k around its properties [Certain] (source: https://www.regencycenters.com). It earns base rent, percentage/escalator rent and expense recoveries from tenants, plus development/redevelopment profit on a ~$680m in-process pipeline [Certain] (source: https://www.regencycenters.com).

## 2. Quality
Necessity-based grocery anchors in affluent trade areas drive durable, inflation-linked cash flow: leased occupancy ~97% and commenced occupancy 94.5% (record levels), cash rent spreads >10% (GAAP ~20%), and over 80% of new shop leases carry ≥3% annual escalators [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/reg/transcripts/658187-q2-2026/). The moat is the in-house national development platform — decades-tenured regional managing directors and >20% share of US shopping-center development despite a fragmented market — which smaller developers can't replicate and lets REG recapture weak anchors on "good land" [Likely] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/reg/transcripts/750844-bofa-ny-global-real-estate-conference-2026/). An A-rated balance sheet funds growth without equity issuance, a cost-of-capital edge over lower-rated peers [Likely] (same source). ROIC is flat at 5.4% TTM (+0.6pp/5y) — typical cap-rate-driven REIT returns, not premium — while ROE is rising (8.2% TTM, +1.7pp/5y) [Certain] (fact sheet). Net debt/EBITDA is 4.80x TTM, falling (-0.24x/5y) [Certain] (fact sheet).

## 3. Valuation
Current multiples sit roughly in line with peers (P/E 24.3x vs 23.7x peer median, +2%; EV/EBITDA 17.2x vs 17.0x, +1%; EV/Sales 10.9x vs 10.5x, +4%) but below REG's own 5-year range (EV/EBITDA, EV/Sales below their 5y minimum; P/E in the bottom 16% of its 5y range) [Certain] (fact sheet). The stock fell 9.3% over 3 months, underperforming SPY by 22.6pp over 6 months, even as management raised FY guidance (same-property NOI growth to mid-6%, core FFO/share growth >5%) [Certain]/[Likely] (fact sheet; https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/reg/transcripts/658187-q2-2026/) — more consistent with a sector-wide, rate-driven derating than company deterioration. Base fair value (P/FFO proxy, the owner's best method here) of $94.06 implies +30.6% upside to the $72.05 close; bear is -0.4%, bull +32.4% [Certain] (fact sheet). That asymmetry is the valuation case. FCF yield of 2.68% is unremarkable alone but typical for a REIT compounding via development spreads, not distributed yield [Likely].

## 4. What would prove this wrong
- Same-property NOI growth (earnings release) falls below 2% for two consecutive quarters, signaling the leasing/escalator engine has stalled.
- Net debt/EBITDA (ratios page) rises above 5.5x, the top of REG's own target leverage range, eroding its balance-sheet cost-of-capital edge.
- Leased/commenced occupancy (quarterly earnings release) falls back below 95%/92%, reversing the current record-occupancy trend.
- Development pipeline lease-up (investor presentation) falls below 70% leased, undermining the ~9%-yield, NAV-accretive development thesis.

## 5. Capital allocation and cash conversion
An A-rated balance sheet with near-full availability on a $1.5bn credit facility and ~$180-190m annual free cash flow funds the ~$400m/year development pipeline largely without new equity issuance [Likely] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/reg/transcripts/750844-bofa-ny-global-real-estate-conference-2026/). Net debt/EBITDA of 4.80x TTM sits at the low end of REG's 5.0x-5.5x target range and has fallen over five years (-0.24x) [Certain]/[Likely] (fact sheet; same source). Shares outstanding rose only 0.63% YoY, consistent with disciplined, mostly self-funded growth rather than dilutive raises [Certain] (fact sheet). Retained cash is reinvested into development at ~9% blended yields, accretive versus the portfolio's implied cap rate [Likely] (same source).

## 6. Unusual items
Short interest is 4.04% of float, up 8.5% month-on-month — not large enough to signal crowding but worth monitoring [Certain] (fact sheet). Institutional ownership is high (92.4%), typical for a REIT; insider ownership is low (0.40%) [Certain] (fact sheet). No governance flags; macro exposure is standard REIT rate-sensitivity, already reflected in the 12-month underperformance versus SPY. Otherwise: None.
