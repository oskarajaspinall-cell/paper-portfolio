# Amdocs Limited (DOX) — Core initiation — 2026-10-19

## 1. Business
Amdocs sells software (billing, customer management, network/OSS) and long-term managed services to telecom and media operators, running and modernizing their core IT/network operations under multi-year contracts, increasingly reframed around its "aOS" agentic AI platform [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/dox/). Recent deals (10-year VEON agreement, Telefónica Chile, prior Liberty Latin America) show the model: outcome-based, multi-year "run and modernize" contracts (source: https://www.tipranks.com/news/the-fly/veon-amdocs-enter-10-year-strategic-agreement-to-advance-digital-services-thefly-news).

## 2. Quality
Returns on capital have improved steadily: ROIC 13.9%→17.2% and ROCE 11.5%→16.6% FY21-25, with TTM at 15.3%/17.6%; gross margin rose 34.5%→38.3% and operating margin 14.0%→17.8% TTM [Certain] (fact sheet). Net and FCF margins have drifted down (net margin 16.1%→9.8% TTM, FCF margin 16.7%→13.7% TTM) and net debt/EBITDA has risen from near-zero to 0.85x TTM [Certain] (fact sheet). Switching costs in BSS/OSS are genuinely high — multi-year, deeply embedded systems are hard to rip out — but the customer base is extremely concentrated: AT&T was 25.9% of FY2025 revenue (24.5% FY2024), T-Mobile 19.9% (22.6% FY2024), and the top 10 customers together ~70% of revenue in both years [company-reported] (source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1062579/000119312525319187/dox-20250930.htm). AT&T and T-Mobile together are the largest single swing factor: the moat protects against competitors but not against either customer's own decision to insource, merge or consolidate vendors. This caps quality at good-not-great.

## 3. Valuation
At 14.2x P/E (5% into the 5-year range of 13.9x-20.3x, i.e. near the bottom) and 13% below peers' 16.4x median, the market is pricing DOX cheaply on earnings [Certain] (fact sheet). EV/EBITDA (6.9x), EV/Sales (1.5x) and P/FCF (9.8x) all sit below their own 5-year minimums and 21-33% below peer medians (CSGS, ACN, INFY, NICE) [Certain] (fact sheet). The reverse DCF implies the price assumes revenue shrinks ~4.4% in year one before fading to terminal growth [Likely] — a reasonable embedded fear given AT&T/T-Mobile concentration, but harder to square with the recently signed 10-year VEON deal and other renewals. Base fair value of $80.80 (+34.5% vs the $60.06 close) sits between a bear case of $63.75 (+6.1%, still modest downside protection) and a bull of $98.10 (+63.3%) [Likely] (fact sheet). FCF yield of 10.2% supports the buyback-funded capital return even if growth stays muted.

## 4. What would prove this wrong
- AT&T or T-Mobile discloses non-renewal, material scope reduction, or insourcing of its Amdocs relationship (next 20-F/10-K or press release) — each is ~20-26% of revenue.
- Operating margin (income statement page) falls back below 15%, reversing the FY21-25 expansion trend and signalling pricing pressure from concentrated customers.
- ROIC (ratios page) falls below 13%, giving back the five-year gains in capital efficiency.
- Net debt/EBITDA (ratios/balance sheet pages) rises above 1.5x, showing buybacks or weaker cash generation have materially levered the balance sheet.

## 5. Capital allocation and cash conversion
FCF conversion is strong and improving (FCF/NI 103.9%→140.8% TTM), and shares outstanding fell 3.52% YoY, consistent with ongoing buybacks [Certain] (fact sheet). Net debt/EBITDA has risen from -0.10x (FY21) to 0.85x TTM as capital returns have outpaced FCF growth [Certain] (fact sheet) — still a conservative balance sheet (Altman Z 4.33, Piotroski F 5/9) but a trend worth watching against the trigger above.

## 6. Unusual items
Customer concentration is the key non-fact-sheet risk: AT&T 25.9%/T-Mobile 19.9% of FY2025 revenue, top 10 customers ~70% [company-reported] (source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1062579/000119312525319187/dox-20250930.htm). The FY2025 20-F also expanded risk language on AI/GenAI tooling and on sanctions/geopolitical exposure (Russia-related trade restrictions; Israel-based operations amid a volatile post-ceasefire environment) [company-reported], neither yet a financial impact (source: same filing). Separately, Amdocs sold its non-core Vubiquity media-supply-chain unit to Premiere Digital (Oct 2026), consistent with management's stated focus on its core "agentic" aOS strategy — a small portfolio pruning move, not thesis-relevant on its own [Likely] (source: https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/premiere-digital-acquires-amdocs-vubiquity-creating-a-global-digital-distribution-leader-302902921.html).
