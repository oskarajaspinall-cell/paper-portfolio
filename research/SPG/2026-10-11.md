# Simon Property Group, Inc. (SPG) — Core initiation — 2026-10-11

## 1. Business
SPG owns and operates Class-A malls, Premium Outlets and mixed-use destinations in the US plus stakes in international joint ventures. Revenue is mainly minimum rent plus overage rent tied to tenant sales, with management fees from JVs and equity income from retail brand investments [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/spg/).

## 2. Quality
ROIC has risen from 7.4% (FY2021) to 9.3% TTM, and net debt/EBITDA (site definition) has fallen from 6.85x to 5.54x over the same period — both trends point to improving returns and a de-levering balance sheet [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/spg/financials/ratios/). The moat is scarcity: dominant, land-constrained Class-A mall/outlet real estate that is effectively irreplaceable, giving pricing power over tenants who need the foot traffic [Likely]. The Q2 2026 earnings call cited robust NOI and FFO growth, strong leasing demand and record sales trends, with 2026 FFO guidance raised [Likely] (source: https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/simon-reports-second-quarter-2026-results-and-increases-guidance-for-full-year-2026-real-estate-ffo-per-share-302847345.html). Piotroski F-Score is middling at 4/9, a mild caution flag on fundamental momentum [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/spg/statistics/). ROE of 120.5% TTM is inflated by REIT leverage and a thin equity base, not a clean quality signal [Certain].

## 3. Valuation
P/E of 14.1x sits near the bottom of its 5y range (9th percentile) and 42% below the peer median (24.3x) — but GAAP EPS for REITs is distorted by heavy depreciation, so this discount overstates cheapness [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/spg/financials/ratios/). EV/EBITDA (20.3x, 76th percentile of 5y range, +18% vs peers' 17.2x) and EV/Sales (15.0x, 85th percentile, +40% vs peers) are the more comparable REIT metrics and show the market paying a premium to both SPG's own history and peers for its NOI base [Certain]. P/B (14.7x vs peer median 2.1x) is not meaningful given depreciated historical-cost book values and is excluded from the read [Certain]. The fair-value model (P/FFO proxy, the owner's best method for this industry) gives a base case of $258.08 (+29.4% vs the $199.42 last close), bear $194.92 (-2.3%) and bull $295.31 (+48.1%) — asymmetric upside with limited downside in the model's own bear case [Likely] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/spg/).

## 4. What would prove this wrong
- ROIC (ratios page) falls back below 8%, reversing the five-year rising trend.
- Net debt/EBITDA, site definition (ratios page) rises back above 6.5x, undoing the de-leveraging.
- FCF yield (statistics page) drops materially below 4% while distributions keep growing, signalling cash conversion can no longer cover the payout.
- Piotroski F-Score (statistics page) falls below 4, confirming broader fundamental deterioration.

## 5. Capital allocation and cash conversion
FCF yield is 4.25% TTM [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/spg/statistics/). Shares outstanding are roughly flat YoY (-0.28%), consistent with buybacks offsetting modest dilution [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/spg/statistics/). The June 2026 10-Q shows the company funded both distributions and share repurchases from operating cash flow in H1 2026 while continuing to carry substantial secured and unsecured debt [Certain] (source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1063761/000110465926093360/spg-20260630x10q.htm). In September 2026 SPG issued additional senior notes, which (together with the falling net debt/EBITDA trend above) reads as proactive maturity management rather than distress [Likely] (source: https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/simon-property-group-sells-800-million-of-senior-notes-302874357.html).

## 6. Unusual items
Short interest is modest at 3.08% of float but rose 13% month-over-month, a mild increase in bearish positioning worth monitoring, not yet a red flag [Likely] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/spg/statistics/). Institutional ownership is very high at 95.9%, typical for a large-cap REIT and not itself a concern [Certain].
