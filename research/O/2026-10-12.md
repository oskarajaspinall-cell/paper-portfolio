# Realty Income Corporation (O) — Core initiation — 2026-10-12

## 1. Business
Realty Income is a triple-net-lease REIT: it buys freestanding, single-tenant commercial real estate (retail, industrial, gaming, and growing data-centre exposure) and leases it back on long contracts where tenants pay taxes, insurance and maintenance [Certain] (source: SEC 10-K, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/726728/000072672826000011/o-20251231.htm). Revenue is contractual rent with embedded escalators across 15,500+ properties in the US, UK and Europe; UK/Europe is now 19% of annualised base rent, up from 14% in 2024 [Certain] (same source).

## 2. Quality
Scale, low-cost capital access and diversification (top 20 clients only 35.8% of annualised base rent; 32.2% investment-grade) are Realty Income's moat versus smaller net-lease peers, who can't match its deal flow or underwriting breadth [Likely] (source: SEC 10-K). Net debt/EBITDA has fallen from 8.65x (FY21) to 5.74-5.85x TTM, a genuine five-year deleveraging trend [Certain] (fact sheet: RA). But returns on capital are structurally thin for the asset class: ROIC 3.6% TTM and ROE 3.2%, both roughly flat over five years, reflecting a low-margin, depreciation-heavy real-estate model rather than pricing power [Certain] (fact sheet: RA). Gross/operating/FCF margins are [data unavailable] on the fact sheet, so conversion of rent to distributable cash can't be verified directly here; the business is better judged on FFO than GAAP margins [Certain].

## 3. Valuation
At the 54.18 USD close, O trades at 39.6x P/E and 15.3x EV/EBITDA, both below its own five-year range minimum (45.8x and 15.8x) [Certain] (fact sheet: RA, ST). Versus peers (NNN, ADC, WPC, EPRT), EV/EBITDA (+2%) and EV/Sales (+1%) are in line, while P/E is 91% above the peer median — a GAAP-depreciation artefact typical of net-lease REITs with different asset ages, not evidence of overvaluation on EV-based metrics [Likely]. P/B of 1.3x is a 12% discount to the peer median 1.5x. The fact sheet's P/FFO-proxy fair value (the industry's best method) puts bear/base/bull at 59.59/60.82/63.44 USD, all above the close — +10% to +17% upside [Certain] (fact sheet). The price assumes the deleveraging and international-growth strategy stalls and/or the rate backdrop stays unfavourable indefinitely; the risk-free rate used (5.22% 10y Treasury) is itself elevated, which depresses the P/FFO output versus a lower-rate scenario [Likely].

## 4. What would prove this wrong
- Net debt/EBITDA (ratios page) rises back above 6.5x, reversing the five-year deleveraging trend from 8.65x to 5.74x.
- ROIC (ratios page) falls below 3.0%, below the five-year low of 2.8%.
- Investment-grade share of annualised base rent (SEC 10-K, Business section) falls below 25%, from 32.2% in FY2025.
- Short interest (statistics page) keeps rising past 7% of float with no stabilisation, beyond its current 4.34% (+8.0% month-on-month).

## 5. Capital allocation and cash conversion
Shares outstanding rose 4.53% YoY, typical REIT external-growth funding since ~90% of taxable income must be distributed [Certain] (fact sheet: ST). Realty Income also recycles capital: a September 2026 joint venture sold KKR a 49% stake in a stabilised European portfolio for €528m, funding growth with less new equity [Certain] (source: https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/realty-income-and-kkr-to-establish-euro-denominated-joint-venture-advancing-realty-incomes-private-capital-platform-302877296.html). It raised its monthly dividend to $0.2715/share, its 136th increase, 674th consecutive payment [Certain] (source: https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/136th-common-stock-monthly-dividend-increase-declared-by-realty-income-302872745.html). FCF yield is -3.87% per the fact sheet, but FCF margin/conversion are [data unavailable]; FFO is the standard REIT cash metric.

## 6. Unusual items
Short interest is 4.34% of float, up 8.0% month-on-month, alongside a -8.2% 12-month share-price decline and heavy underperformance versus SPY (-23.9pp over 12 months) [Certain] (fact sheet: ST, HI). This tracks a sector-wide rate-driven de-rating (headline: https://invezz.com/news/2026/10/06/heres-why-the-realty-income-stock-is-in-a-freefall-as-us-treasury-yields-jump/) rather than company-specific deterioration [Likely]. No governance red flags noted.
