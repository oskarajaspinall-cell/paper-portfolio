# Macro overlay: Equifax Inc. (EFX) — 2026-10-12
## Assessment
Chain: long-term/mortgage rates → US mortgage origination volume → EFX's USIS mortgage-linked credit-pull revenue; and 10y Treasury → CAPM discount rate → the fact sheet's own DCF fair value (risk-free 5.22%, beta 1.34) [Certain]. The note already flags mortgage volumes (~50% below historical) as "the single largest swing factor" for both revenue and the multiple [Likely].

Facts (FRED, 2026-10-08): 10y Treasury (DGS10) 5.22%, +68bp over 3m; 10y real yield (DFII10) 2.87%, +56bp over 3m; 2y Treasury (DGS2) 4.75% vs effective fed funds (DFF) 3.88%; breakevens (T10YIE) flat at 2.33%, -4bp 1m. Since breakevens barely moved, the back-up is real-rate, not inflation-expectation, driven.

Market pricing **(inference)**: the 2y sitting ~87bp above fed funds is a rough proxy that markets are not pricing near-term cuts — consistent with sticky core CPI (CPILFESL, +1.64 over 3m, lagging to 2026-08) [Likely]. That implies continued mortgage-rate "lock-in" and a higher discount rate than three months ago, both already partly reflected in the fact sheet's fair value (which uses today's 5.22% risk-free) but a further rise would compress it mechanically.

Bull: yields roll over, mortgage volumes inflect, discount rate falls → base/bull fair value (166.84/194.87) supported or exceeded.
Bear: yields stay elevated or rise further, mortgage recovery pushed out, DCF discount rate rises → fair value compresses below base case even as the stock looks statistically cheap.

Tilt: moderate toward bear — a diversified but still mortgage-sensitive bureau facing a real, not nominal, rate back-up with no cut signal priced.
