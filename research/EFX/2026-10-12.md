# Equifax Inc. (EFX) — Core initiation — 2026-10-12

## 1. Business
Equifax sells consumer and commercial credit data, identity/fraud tools and income-and-employment verification (The Work Number) to lenders, employers, government agencies and insurers [Certain]. Revenue is usage-based (per credit pull/verification) plus subscription analytics, split across U.S. mortgage-linked credit reporting, non-mortgage consumer lending, Workforce Solutions and international bureaus (Canada, UK, Latin America) [Likely].

## 2. Quality
Equifax is one of three national credit bureaus (with TransUnion and Experian), protected by regulatory scale, decades of proprietary data and lender-switching costs — a genuine oligopoly moat [Certain]. Workforce Solutions' Work Number holds 170 million active income/employment records, now the firm's third-largest vertical (source: https://investor.equifax.com) [Likely]. VantageScore 4.0 adoption is accelerating — nearly 2,000 mortgage lenders implementing it at $1 pricing through 2028 — supporting share versus FICO's ~$10 scores (source: https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/equifax-reports-accelerated-adoption-of-vantagescore-4-0-across-the-mortgage-industry-302902982.html) [Likely]. But returns have deteriorated: ROIC fell from 12.3% (FY2021) to 8.9% TTM, ROE from 22.0% to 14.3%, operating margin from 23.1% to 18.3% (fact sheet, ratios/income statement) [Certain], largely reflecting mortgage volumes still ~50% below historical levels plus debt-funded acquisitions (Barclays conference transcript, 2026-09-14, source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/efx/transcripts/736077-barclays-24th-annual-global-financial-services-conference/) [Likely]. FCF conversion improved sharply (19%→160% TTM) as the post-2021 capex cycle eases [Certain], a better current signal than reported margins.

## 3. Valuation
At last close 145.78, EFX trades at 25.6x P/E, 11.7x EV/EBITDA, 3.5x EV/Sales and 15.5x P/FCF — all below its own 5-year minimums (34.2x/16.8x/5.2x/23.4x respectively) [Certain]. Versus peers (TRU, FICO, DNB, VRSK), EFX is cheaper on EV/EBITDA (-16%) and EV/Sales (-41%), roughly in line on P/FCF (+2%), but above on P/E (+32%) and P/B (+105%, likely reflecting peers' thinner equity bases rather than EFX being overvalued) [Likely]. The fact sheet's blended fair value (DCF-weighted, base 166.84) implies 14.4% upside from last close; bull/bear are 194.87/64.22 [Guessing]. The market appears to be pricing a distant mortgage recovery and the recent margin compression, with limited credit for Workforce Solutions growth or the doubled ~$150m AI cost-reduction target (source: https://investor.equifax.com) [Likely]. Multiples compressed versus both history and most peers, alongside improving FCF conversion, support entry near current levels.

## 4. What would prove this wrong
- ROIC (ratios page) falls below 7%, a new multi-year low.
- Net debt/EBITDA (ratios page) rises back above 3.2x, reversing the FY2023-FY2025 deleveraging.
- FCF margin (cash-flow/income statement) falls back below 10%, reversing the recent conversion gains.
- Operating margin (income statement) falls below 17%, signaling losses beyond mortgage cyclicality.

## 5. Capital allocation and cash conversion
Equifax returned ~$1.2bn to shareholders in 2025 (dividends +20% YoY to $232.8m, plus buybacks; TTM buybacks ~$1.4bn per the Barclays conference transcript) while still funding bolt-on M&A, including the ~$740m Círculo de Crédito acquisition in Mexico [Likely] (source: https://investor.equifax.com). Net debt/EBITDA has fallen from a 3.46x peak (FY2023) to 2.71-2.75x (FY2025/TTM), showing deleveraging even as M&A continues [Certain]. FCF conversion of 160% TTM (fact sheet) funds both dividends and paydown without equity issuance [Certain], though continued acquisitiveness means leverage could re-widen if a large deal is struck.

## 6. Unusual items
Short interest is low and falling (4.47% of float, -33% month-on-month) [Certain]. Institutional ownership is reported above 100% of float (an overlapping-filings artifact), insider ownership is negligible at 0.34% [Certain]. No governance or accounting red flags identified; Altman Z-score 3.60 (safe zone) and Piotroski F-score 5 (mid-range) [Certain]. Mortgage-market sensitivity (volumes ~50% below historical levels) remains the single largest swing factor for both revenue growth and any multiple re-rating [Likely].
