# Macro overlay: L3Harris Technologies, Inc. (LHX) — 2026-10-12
## Assessment
Chain: real yields -> cost of equity (DCF discount rate) -> fair value, and -> refinancing cost on LHX's above-peer leverage [Likely] **(inference)**. Revenue is government/sole-source (defense budget, not a FRED variable); beta is low (0.51 [Certain], statistics page), so the ERP channel matters less than the rate-level channel, given net debt/EBITDA of 2.29x TTM (2.68x FY2025 [Certain], ratios page) and ~$1.8bn of debt maturing (note, Laguna transcript 2026-09-16) [Likely].

Facts (FRED, 2026-10-08/09): 10y real yield (DFII10) 2.87%, +0.56pp/3m; 10y nominal (DGS10) 5.22%, +0.68pp/3m; 10y breakeven (T10YIE) 2.33%, +0.09pp/3m only; HY spread (BAMLH0A0HYM2) 3.15%, +0.45pp/3m, still a low absolute level. The nominal rise is mostly real, not inflation-driven — a genuine discount-rate headwind [Certain]. Market pricing (proxy, **inference**): 2y yield (4.75%) sits 0.87pp above effective fed funds (3.88%), consistent with the market not pricing near-term cuts. The fact sheet's fair value already uses the spot 5.22% risk-free rate, so today's level is priced; the open question is direction from here.

**Macro bull**: real yields roll over -> discount rate eases -> fair-value range/multiple re-rate up on top of already-cheap EV/EBITDA and P/FCF. **Macro bear**: real yields grind higher -> discount rate rises further, refinancing the ~$1.8bn maturities gets costlier -> fair value compresses and interest expense pressures net margin even if operations improve. **Tilt**: small, toward bear — low beta dampens the ERP channel, but the real-yield move is unusually large (+56bp/3m) and leverage is above peers.
