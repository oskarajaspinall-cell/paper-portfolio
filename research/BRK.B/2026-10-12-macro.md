# Macro overlay: Berkshire Hathaway Inc. (BRK.B) — 2026-10-12
## Assessment
Two channels matter for BRK, cutting in opposite directions: short-term rates set the yield on its very large cash/T-bill hoard (investment income), while the 10y real yield sets the discount rate applied both to BRK's own multiple and, via GAAP mark-to-market, to its public-equity stakes (e.g. Apple) that flow through reported net income [Certain] (fact sheet: net income volatility from equity marks).

**Facts** (FRED, 2026-10-08/09): effective fed funds 3.88%, +0.25pp/1m, +0.26pp/3m; 10y Treasury 5.22%, +0.42/+0.68pp; 10y real yield (DFII10) 2.87%, +0.44/+0.56pp; 10y breakeven 2.33%, roughly flat (-0.04/+0.09pp); HY credit spread 3.15%, +0.48/+0.45pp; VIX 15.4, little changed. **Market pricing (proxy, inference)**: 2y yield (4.75%) sitting ~0.87pp above effective fed funds implies the market is pricing policy staying higher for longer, not imminent cuts [Likely] **(inference)**. With breakevens flat, the move is a real-rate, not inflation, repricing [Likely].

**Bull**: rates stay higher-for-longer with credit calm — BRK's cash pile keeps earning ~4%+ risk-free, supporting investment income; its own 1.5x P/B (83rd percentile of 5y range) holds.
**Bear**: either a renewed real-yield spike further compresses equity multiples market-wide, marking down BRK's equity stakes and squeezing its already-thin P/B cushion, or a dovish growth-scare pivot cuts short rates and the cash-yield tailwind.

**Tilt**: small, toward bear — the cash-yield benefit from elevated short rates is real but is offset by the multiple/mark-to-market risk from a thin valuation cushion (inference).
