# Berkshire Hathaway Inc. (BRK.B) — Core initiation — 2026-10-12

## 1. Business
A conglomerate holding wholly-owned operating subsidiaries plus a large public-equity portfolio [Certain]. Segments: insurance (GEICO, reinsurance — underwriting profit plus investment income on float), BNSF railroad, Berkshire Hathaway Energy (regulated utilities), and manufacturing/services/retail (incl. McLane, Pilot) [Certain] (source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1067983/000119312526083899/brka-20251231.htm). Reported net income is highly volatile because GAAP marks the equity portfolio to market through earnings [Certain].

## 2. Quality
Insurance float funded at a low/negative cost, BNSF's rail duopoly position, and GEICO's scale give durable advantages competitors can't easily replicate [Likely]. The balance sheet carries a large net-cash position (net debt/EBITDA −1.81x TTM, −2.15x FY2025) [Certain] (source: stockanalysis.com ratios page), giving optionality peers lack. But core profitability is drifting down: ROIC fell from 20.3% (FY2021) to 19.3% TTM (−3.3pp over 5y, including a −5.8% trough in FY2022 from equity losses), ROE fell to 9.8% (FY2025) from 18.8% (FY2021), and operating margin fell to 26.1% (FY2025) from 41.7% [Certain] (source: stockanalysis.com ratios/income-statement pages). Leadership transitioned to Greg Abel as CEO, confirmed in the FY2025 10-K and on the company site; Warren Buffett continues writing shareholder letters [Certain] (source: https://www.berkshirehathaway.com; 10-K as above). This is a long-telegraphed succession, but capital-allocation skill at this scale is not proven to transfer, and it is a key-person risk investors should track [Likely].

## 3. Valuation
P/E 12.9x sits at the 62nd percentile of its 5y range (7.4x–16.2x), near peer median 13.1x [Certain]. EV/EBITDA 6.7x is in line with peers (6.6x) [Certain]. P/B 1.5x is near the top of its own 5y range (83rd percentile) and 29% above peer median (1.1x) [Certain] — for a holding company whose book is mostly marked-to-market securities and insurance liabilities, P/B is the most informative multiple here, and shows little margin of safety. P/FCF (45.6x) is skewed by a depressed TTM FCF margin (6.3%, 5y range 3.1–9.5%); read with caution (source: stockanalysis.com ratios/statistics pages for all of the above). The fact sheet's modelled fair value (bear $378,764/base $728,393/bull $827,055) is unusable: it implies 73,000%+ upside versus the $515.62 close, almost exactly ~1,500x the real gap — consistent with a Class A/Class B share-count mismatch, not a signal. This note relies on the multiples comparison only.

## 4. What would prove this wrong
- ROIC (ratios page) falls below 12% for two consecutive fiscal years (vs 19.3% TTM, 17.0% FY2025) — core operating/underwriting returns eroding beyond cyclicality.
- Net debt/EBITDA (balance sheet/ratios page) rises above +1.0x (vs −1.81x TTM) — the net-cash fortress that funds opportunistic deployment is gone.
- FCF margin (cash flow/income statement) stays below 5% for two consecutive fiscal years (vs 6.3% TTM, 5y range 3.1–9.5%) — operating cash generation structurally weaker.

## 5. Capital allocation and cash conversion
FCF conversion (FCF/NI) is volatile because net income swings with unrealized equity marks (−96.2% in FY2022) but is 28.2% TTM and trending up (+8.3pp over 5y) [Certain] (source: stockanalysis.com ratios/cash-flow pages). Shares outstanding are flat YoY (−0.00%), implying minimal net buyback activity recently [Certain] (source: stockanalysis.com statistics page). Recent news shows management adding to a public-equity stake (Lennar) over several weeks, though at a reportedly slowing pace — directionally consistent with redeploying cash rather than only hoarding it [Likely] (source: https://www.barrons.com/articles/berkshire-buys-more-lennar-stock-owns-12-2c346680; numbers in that headline are not used as data).
