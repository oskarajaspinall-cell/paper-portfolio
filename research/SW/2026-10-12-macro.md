# Macro overlay: Smurfit Westrock Plc (SW) — 2026-10-12
## Assessment
Chain: rising real/nominal yields -> higher discount rate -> lower DCF fair value, and -> higher funding cost for an already-leveraged, debt-funded acquirer **(inference)**. Facts: US 10y real yield (DFII10) 2.87% on 2026-10-08, +0.56pp over 3m; US 10y Treasury (DGS10) 5.22% on 2026-10-08, +0.68pp/3m, of which real yield explains most of the move since 10y breakeven inflation (T10YIE) is flat at 2.33%, +0.09pp/3m [Certain] (https://fred.stlouisfed.org/graph/fredgraph.csv?id=DFII10, id=DGS10, id=T10YIE, 2026-10-08/09). High-yield credit spread (BAMLH0A0HYM2) 3.15%, +0.45pp/3m, still well below the portfolio's own 5.0% stress threshold [Certain] (https://fred.stlouisfed.org/graph/fredgraph.csv?id=BAMLH0A0HYM2, 2026-10-08). Market pricing (proxy): 2y yield (4.75%) sits above effective fed funds (3.88%), implying the market is not pricing near-term relief — **(inference)**. This matters specifically for SW because net debt/EBITDA is already 2.95x TTM (factsheet), Altman Z is 1.47 (distress zone), and management is layering ~$420m of further debt-funded M&A (CMPC Chile) on top [Certain/Likely, factsheet]. The fact sheet's own DCF already uses the current 5.22% risk-free rate, so further yield increases would cut the $49.15 base fair value and raise the cost of refinancing before deleveraging is complete; a reversal would do the opposite and support a re-rate toward the bull case. Fundamentals (merger integration, pricing/synergy timing) still dominate the thesis; macro shifts the odds, not the driver.
