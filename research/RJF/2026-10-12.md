# Raymond James Financial, Inc. (RJF) — Core initiation — 2026-10-12
## 1. Business
RJF is a diversified wealth manager and investment bank. Private Client Group (brokerage/advisory, $1.86tn AUA) earns fee and commission revenue from client assets; Capital Markets earns advisory/underwriting/trading fees; Asset Management earns fees on managed assets; RJ Bank earns net interest income on deposits and loans [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/rjf/, https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/rjf/transcripts/546287-q3-2026/).

## 2. Quality
ROIC is 12.2% TTM and ROE 18.4% TTM, both roughly flat over five years [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/rjf/financials/ratios/). Durable advantages: a scaled, largely independent-contractor advisor network that recruited advisors bringing $56bn of client assets and $393mn of trailing-12-month production in the first nine months of FY26 [Likely] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/rjf/transcripts/546287-q3-2026/), a recurring fee base tied to $1.86tn of client assets, and lower balance-sheet leverage than large banks, which limits risk through the cycle. Operating margin has held a stable 18.8-21.2% band over five years [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/rjf/financials/income-statement/). FCF margin/conversion swung sharply (67% to -32% to 9.5% TTM) [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/rjf/financials/cash-flow-statement/, https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/rjf/financials/income-statement/), but for a broker-dealer this reflects client cash/margin-balance timing rather than core profitability, so ROE/ROIC and margins are the more reliable quality signals. Piotroski F-score is a middling 4/9 [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/rjf/statistics/), worth monitoring but not a red flag given stable margins.

## 3. Valuation
At 13.8x P/E, RJF sits at 41% of its own 5y range (12.1x-16.2x) and 16% below the peer median of 16.5x (LPLA, SCHW, SF, MS) [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/rjf/financials/ratios/, peer statistics pages). P/B of 2.4x is mid-range on its own history (49%) and 27% below the peer median of 3.3x [Certain]. P/FCF of 21.1x looks stretched versus its own 5y range, but FCF is noisy here for the reason above, so P/E and P/B carry more weight. Base fair value is $171.36 (DCF 67%/P/E 33% blend), implying +8.2% upside; bear $143.55 (-9.4%), bull $201.16 (+27%) [Likely] (source: factsheet valuation table). The reverse DCF implies the price assumes only 2.8% year-1 revenue growth fading to terminal, against 16% TTM revenue growth [Likely]. The stock is -7.4% vs its 50-day MA and -4.1% over 12 months versus SPY +15.7% — a modest discount, not a deep-value setup.

## 4. What would prove this wrong
- Operating margin (income statement page) falls below 18% for two consecutive quarters — below its 5y low of 18.8%.
- Net new assets growth (quarterly results/10-Q) turns negative year-over-year, ending the AUA-compounding this thesis depends on (FY26 YTD was +119% y/y per the Q3 call).
- ROE (ratios page) falls below 15% for two consecutive quarters, versus a 5y range of 17.0-18.9%.
- Advisor recruiting reverses: trailing-12-month recruited client assets (disclosed quarterly) falls meaningfully below the $56bn pace cited for FY26 YTD.

## 5. Capital allocation and cash conversion
RJF returned ~$506mn to shareholders in Q3 FY26 (dividends plus buybacks) and ~$2bn over the trailing 12 months — about 86% of earnings [Likely] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/rjf/transcripts/546287-q3-2026/). Shares outstanding fell 3.82% year-over-year [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/rjf/statistics/), consistent with active buybacks. The Clark Capital acquisition (~$47bn of combined AUM/AUA) was funded without new debt disclosed on the call [Likely]. Reported FCF conversion is volatile due to client cash/margin-balance swings typical of a broker-dealer (source: cash-flow and income-statement pages), so capital-return capacity is better judged on earnings than on reported FCF.
