# Macro overlay: Dollar General Corporation (DG) — 2026-10-12
## Assessment
Two channels matter: (1) `rates -> discount rate -> DCF fair value` — the base fair value ($146.67) is built directly off the 10y Treasury (5.22% risk-free input), and DG carries above-peer leverage (net debt/EBITDA 3.96x TTM), making funding cost relevant; (2) `inflation -> low-income consumer spending power -> same-store sales`, a channel management itself flagged (Goldman conference transcript, 2026-09-15) [Certain].

Facts (FRED, 2026-10-08/09): 10y Treasury 5.22% (+0.68pp/3m), 10y real yield 2.87% (+0.56pp/3m), 2y yield 4.75% (+0.59pp/3m), fed funds 3.88% (+0.26pp/3m), breakeven 2.33% (+0.09pp/3m), HY spread 3.15% (+0.45pp/3m). Core CPI (Aug, lagging) +1.64 index pts/3m — a level, not a forward signal.

Market pricing **(inference)**: 2y yield ~0.87pp above fed funds proxies a market pricing a higher, not lower, policy path, not near-term cuts [Likely]. With flat-to-rising breakevens and widening HY spreads, that reads as a modest hawkish repricing vs three months ago — DG's low beta (0.23, floored to 0.8) dampens but doesn't remove the Rf-driven hit to cost of equity.

Bull: yields/spreads ease, discount-rate pressure fades, multiple drifts toward the $146-196 base/bull range; cooling CPI eases the low-income squeeze, supporting the Q2 FY2026 SSS inflection (+3.5%).
Bear: yields/HY spreads keep widening — fair value compresses mechanically, refinancing cost rises on the 3.96x leverage, and inflation squeezes the core shopper, undercutting the one-quarter turnaround.
Tilt: recent macro moves have been uniformly adverse for a levered, DCF-anchored, low-income-exposed retailer, tilting odds modestly toward bear versus fundamentals alone.
