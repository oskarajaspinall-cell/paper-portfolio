# Virtu Financial (VIRT) — Core initiation — 2026-11-15
## 1. Business
Virtu is a market maker: it earns the bid-ask spread and related income by trading as principal across equities and other asset classes globally, plus a smaller agency/technology brokerage business that earns commissions and fees. Nine-month revenue was $3,439.2 million, up 29.2%, driven mostly by trading income on higher volumes [company-reported] [Certain] (source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1592386/000159238626000034/virt-20260930.htm). Country revenue split: [data unavailable] in the fact sheet and not in the filing delta.

## 2. Quality
Profitability is high but cyclical. TTM operating margin is 42.4% and ROE 57.0%, but ROIC is only 10.5%, below its FY2021 level of 15.8% and with a 5-year falling trend; ROE is flattered by leverage [Likely] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/virt/financials/ratios/). Recent direction is up: ROIC rose from 8.9% to 10.5% and operating margin from 34.3% to 42.4% over 12 months [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/virt/financials/ratios/?p=trailing). FCF margin is 24.7% against 41.4% at its 2-year high, and FCF/NI fell from 240.5% in FY2021 to 140.3% TTM, so earnings convert to cash less readily than before [Certain]. The edge is scale and technology in electronic market making, but competitors can narrow it and returns swing with volatility and volumes (FY2023 operating margin 19.8%) [Likely]. Net debt/EBITDA: [data unavailable]. Piotroski F is 5, middling. Moat is moderate, not durable [Guessing].

## 3. Valuation
P/E is 8.7x, 31% up its own 5y range (median 8.0x) and far below the peer median of 28.2x (IBKR, CBOE, MKTX, NDAQ); peers are exchanges and brokers with steadier earnings, so the discount is partly deserved [Likely]. P/FCF of 10.5x and P/B of 2.6x are above their 5y maxima (5.7x, 2.1x), because FCF fell below earnings and ROE rose [Certain]. FCF yield is 9.54%. The price implies year-1 revenue shrinking 12.4% in the reverse DCF, which looks harsh against current growth [Likely]. Mechanical fair value is flagged unreliable (methods disagree 4.9x; base 124.06 vs 59.33 close), so weigh it lightly. EV/EBITDA: [data unavailable]. The price assumes today's peak-like earnings do not persist, which is reasonable for a volume-driven business [Likely]. The stock is 11.8% above its 200-day average, with 12-month change +64.8%: a business that is working. Source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/virt/statistics/

## 4. What would prove this wrong
- Operating margin (TTM, income statement page) falls below 30%.
- ROIC (TTM, ratios page) falls below 8%.
- FCF margin (TTM, cash flow and income statement pages) falls below 14%.
- Shares outstanding growth (statistics page, YoY) rises above 5%, i.e. earnings are diluted rather than returned.

## 5. Capital allocation and cash conversion
TTM FCF/NI is 140.3%, down from the FY2021-23 range of 240.5%-319.6%, so cash conversion is normalising. Debt: Term B-2 loan balance is $1,545.0 million after a $300.0 million incremental draw for general corporate purposes [company-reported] (source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1592386/000159238626000034/virt-20260930.htm). Net share count rose 1.30% YoY, so buybacks do not fully offset issuance [Certain]. New MD&A language adds risk from growth investments raising costs and from new asset classes (digital assets, event contracts) with regulatory uncertainty [Certain] (same 10-Q).
