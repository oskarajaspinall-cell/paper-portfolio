# Veralto Corporation (VLTO) — Core initiation — 2026-10-10

## 1. Business
Veralto (spun off from Danaher, 2023) sells instrumentation, software and consumables through two segments: Water Quality (treatment/testing analyzers, reagents — Hach) and Product Quality & Innovation (packaging traceability, marking/coding, color — Videojet, Pantone, Esko) [Certain] (source: https://www.veralto.com). ~61% of FY25's ~$5.5bn sales are recurring consumables/services/software on an installed instrument base — a razor-and-blade model [Certain] (source: SEC 10-K, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1967680/000196768026000011/vlto-20251231.htm).

## 2. Quality
Returns are strong: ROIC 26.7% FY25 (23.5% TTM, rising 5y, +1.1pp), gross margin 60.2% TTM (+2.2pp 5y) and FCF conversion 106% TTM (+10.1pp 5y) (ratios/income statement/cash-flow pages) [Certain]. No customer exceeds 10% of sales; the market is fragmented with no single scaled competitor across all product lines, limiting concentration risk but also pricing power versus a dominant incumbent [Likely] (SEC 10-K). The moat rests on the installed base (consumables lock-in) and trusted brands rather than patents, which management itself calls "not material individually" (10-K). Counter-signal: ROCE has fallen 5.2pp over 5 years to 20.0% TTM [Likely] (ratios page) even as ROIC held up, and net debt/EBITDA has risen from ~0.1x (FY21-22) to 1.06x TTM as bolt-on M&A is debt-funded (ratios/balance sheet) — a modestly more capital-intensive model, not yet a red flag.

## 3. Valuation
VLTO looks cheap versus its own 5-year range and peers (XYL, DHR, ROP, AME) on P/E (24.4x vs 5y range 24.1-30.2x, 4th percentile; peer median 30.4x, -20%), EV/EBITDA (18.1x, below its 5y minimum; -2% vs peers) and EV/Sales (4.4x vs peer median 6.4x, -31%) (ratios/statistics, peer statistics pages). P/B is an outlier at 7.6x, +192% vs peer median 2.6x — likely a thin post-spin-off equity base, a genuine conflict in the cross-sectional read [Guessing]. More decisive: the fact sheet's DCF/EV-EBITDA blend puts fair value at $64.92 (bear) / $76.92 (base) / $82.12 (bull) vs last close $96.93 — 15-33% above even the bull case. The reverse DCF shows the price implies 12.3% year-1 revenue growth fading to terminal, well above what a ROCE-declining (-5.2pp 5y) industrial has shown [Likely] (fair-value table). On every scenario modeled VLTO is overvalued; cheap relative multiples don't offset this.

## 4. What would prove this wrong
- ROIC (ratios page) falls below 20% on a sustained (2+ year) basis, showing the installed-base returns are eroding, not just normalizing from a FY25 high.
- Net debt/EBITDA (ratios/balance sheet pages) rises above 2.5x, showing M&A is being funded in a way that overwhelms the cash-generative model.
- Recurring-revenue mix (10-K / veralto.com) falls materially below ~55% of total sales, undermining the razor-and-blade moat this thesis depends on.
- FCF conversion (cash flow vs income statement) falls below 90% on a sustained basis, signalling deteriorating earnings quality.

## 5. Capital allocation and cash conversion
FCF conversion is strong (106% TTM, CF/IS) and funds a dividend plus a share-repurchase program (shares outstanding -0.40% YoY, ST) [Certain]. Management names acquisitions — "over 85 businesses over more than two decades" — as its dominant capital priority (10-K) [Certain], recently Cleanwater1 (~$452-465m after tax benefits) and Alfaa UV, partly funded by a $725m, 4.850% notes offering due 2032 (veralto.com) [Likely]. Net debt/EBITDA has risen from near-zero to 1.06x TTM (ratios/balance sheet) — still low absolutely, but the trend is up, and continued debt-funded M&A is the main capital-allocation risk to monitor.

## 6. Unusual items
Insider ownership is low (0.14% of shares; institutions hold 99.6%, ST), typical of a 2023 spin-off but limiting management's direct equity alignment [Likely]. Short interest is unremarkable at 2.73% of float, up 4.9% month-on-month (ST). Neither is material to the decision.
