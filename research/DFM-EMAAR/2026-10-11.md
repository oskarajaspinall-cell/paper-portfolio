# Emaar Properties PJSC (DFM:EMAAR) — Core initiation — 2026-10-11

## 1. Business
Emaar develops and sells master-planned residential/commercial communities in Dubai (Downtown Dubai, Dubai Hills, Dubai Creek Harbour) and select overseas markets, booking revenue on unit handovers. [Certain] A recurring leasing, hospitality and mall segment (Dubai Mall, hotels) supplements the cyclical development business with steadier cash flow. [Likely] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/quote/dfm/EMAAR/financials/income-statement/)

## 2. Quality
Returns have risen sharply and are now high: ROIC 7.3%→25.3% TTM, ROE 24.2% TTM, with gross/operating/net margins of 55.0%/45.2%/35.8% TTM. [Certain] The balance sheet is net cash (net debt/EBITDA -0.76x, from +2.09x in FY2021). [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/quote/dfm/EMAAR/financials/ratios/) The durable edge is a land bank in Dubai's prime, supply-constrained districts assembled over two decades at low cost, plus a recurring leasing/hospitality/mall income stream that funds new launches without leverage — a scale and location advantage smaller developers cannot replicate quickly. [Likely] The one caution: FCF margin fell from a 65-72% FY2022-24 peak to 46.9% TTM, and FCF conversion from >180% to 131% TTM — still strong, but the trend is down, consistent with the cycle normalizing rather than a structural break. [Likely] Peer comparability is limited (DFM:DIC is a diversified holding company; DFM:DEYAAR and DFM:UPP are much smaller), so "cheap vs. peers" claims below carry lower confidence than the absolute figures. [Guessing]

## 3. Valuation
At 5.2x P/E, 3.5x EV/EBITDA and 1.8x EV/Sales, EMAAR trades below its own 5-year range (6.0-10.5x, 5.3-8.7x, 2.4-3.4x) and below peer medians, with a TTM FCF yield near 25%. [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/quote/dfm/EMAAR/statistics/) P/B of 0.9x is the one multiple above peers (0.7x median), consistent with EMAAR's higher ROE. [Likely] The price assumes the Dubai cycle keeps normalizing: shares are down 16.6% over 12 months, -9.8% vs the 200-day average, trailing SPY by 32pp; the fact sheet's reverse-DCF reading implies the market is pricing roughly -29% revenue in year one, fading to terminal growth. [Likely] The base DCF fair value (AED52.82, +364% vs. last close) directionally supports "cheap" but isn't a reliable absolute anchor: NAV, the standard method for a land-bank developer, and SOTP are [data unavailable], and beta is floor-clamped to 0.8 against a raw 5y beta of 0.37, likely understating the discount rate. [Guessing]

## 4. What would prove this wrong
- Gross margin (income statement page) falls below 45% (TTM 55.0%, 5y low 41.6%) — pricing power eroding as regional supply catches up.
- Net debt/EBITDA (ratios page) rises above 1.0x — the net-cash cushion and capital discipline break down.
- ROIC (ratios page) falls below 12% — returns revert to pre-cycle (FY2021-22) levels.
- FCF margin (cash-flow/income-statement pages) falls below 30% — cash conversion deteriorates beyond the current moderation.

## 5. Capital allocation and cash conversion
The balance sheet is net cash (net debt/EBITDA -0.76x TTM), built from internally generated cash: FCF margin 46.9% TTM (down from a 65-72% FY2022-24 peak) and FCF conversion 131% of net income TTM (down from >180%), still comfortably funding land and construction spend without external debt. [Likely] Shares outstanding rose only 0.27% YoY — negligible dilution and no evidence of buybacks. [Certain] The fact sheet carries no dividend-payout or buyback-amount figure, so disclosed return-of-capital policy is [data unavailable]; the thesis rests on balance-sheet strength, not a stated payout commitment. (source: https://stockanalysis.com/quote/dfm/EMAAR/statistics/)
