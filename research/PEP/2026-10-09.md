# PepsiCo, Inc. (PEP) — Core initiation — 2026-10-09

## 1. Business
PepsiCo sells branded salty/sweet snacks (Frito-Lay, Quaker) and beverages (Pepsi, Gatorade, Mountain Dew) worldwide, mostly through its own direct-store-delivery network plus bottling/distribution partners. Snacks and beverages run as separate North American and international segments; international organic growth (8% in Q3 2026) now outruns the mature North American base (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/pep/transcripts/706870-q3-2026/) [Certain].

## 2. Quality
ROIC is 20.5% TTM, up from 18.1% FY2021 (+2.0pp); ROCE is 21.2% TTM, up from 17.9% (+2.2pp) (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/pep/financials/ratios/) [Certain]. Gross margin is up to 54.5% TTM from 53.4% FY2021, operating margin to 16.8% TTM from 14.9% (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/pep/financials/income-statement/) [Certain]. The moat is scale: Frito-Lay's DSD network and shelf-space entrenchment, plus a cola duopoly with Coca-Cola, are hard to replicate [Likely]. The weak spot is North America beverages: Q3 2026 soda volumes declined and management is "not satisfied with performance in the U.S.," citing cost inflation, unfavourable mix and a lost tariff benefit, which drove an EPS guidance cut despite a revenue beat (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/pep/transcripts/706870-q3-2026/) [Certain]. PepsiCo is raising prices on chips and soda; management says parts of the portfolio not producing growth "will not be part of the company" — a divestiture signal alongside margin discipline (source: https://www.marketwatch.com/story/pepsico-ceos-message-to-employees-if-youre-not-producing-growth-youre-out-13af0335) [Likely]. ROE is 52.2% TTM, dipping to 42.8% FY2025 from 53.7% FY2022 (ratios page) [Certain].

## 3. Valuation
At the $128.34 last close, PEP trades on 15.6x TTM P/E and 10.8x EV/EBITDA — 46% below the peer median (KO, MDLZ, KDP, MNST: 28.7x P/E, 20.0x EV/EBITDA) and below its 5-year P/E range (22.2x–31.2x). EV/Sales (2.2x vs 5.9x peer) and P/FCF (16.7x vs 25.8x peer) show the same gap; FCF yield is 6.0% (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/pep/statistics/, ratios page, peer statistics pages) [Certain]. The reverse DCF embedded in the price implies only 0.8% year-1 revenue growth fading to terminal growth — the market prices the North American slowdown as near-permanent, not cyclical (source: fact sheet fair-value section) [Certain]. Blended base fair value (DCF 67%/P/E 33%) is $165.21 (+28.7%); even the bear case of $99.67 is only -22.3% below price, an asymmetric setup if international growth offsets North America [Likely]. Some peer discount reflects the guidance cut and six-year low, so it is not a pure mispricing [Likely].

## 4. What would prove this wrong
- Organic revenue growth (earnings release/transcript) falls below 2% for two consecutive quarters, confirming a structural slowdown.
- Operating margin (income statement page) falls below 15% for a full fiscal year, versus 16.8% TTM, showing cost pressure is not transitory.
- Net debt/EBITDA (ratios page) rises above 3.0x, versus 2.05x TTM, showing leverage deterioration beyond self-funding capacity.
- FCF conversion (FCF/net income) falls below 70% for two consecutive fiscal years, versus 96.1% TTM, signaling an earnings-quality problem.

## 5. Capital allocation and cash conversion
FCF margin is 10.7% TTM (8.8% FY2021→8.2% FY2025, roughly flat) and FCF conversion (FCF/net income) is 96.1% TTM, up from 91.8% in FY2021, so earnings are substantially backed by cash (source: cash-flow and income-statement pages) [Certain]. Net debt/EBITDA has fallen to 2.05x TTM from 2.52x in FY2021 (source: ratios/balance-sheet pages) [Certain]. Shares outstanding fell only 0.26% year-over-year, a modest buyback pace relative to the dividend; exact buyback/payout figures are [data unavailable] on stockanalysis.com.

## 6. Unusual items
None material. Short interest is 1.90% of float, up 21.5% month-over-month, but the absolute level remains low (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/pep/statistics/) [Certain]. Institutional ownership is 80.2% and beta is 0.36, consistent with a low-volatility staple, not a distress signal.
