# Arch Capital Group Ltd. (ACGL) — Core initiation — 2026-10-07

## 1. Business
Bermuda-based specialty (re)insurer operating three segments: Insurance, Reinsurance and Mortgage. Underwrites property, casualty and specialty risk for premium income, invests float, and insures mortgage credit risk via MI/reinsurance. Profit = underwriting margin (combined ratio) plus net investment income on the float [Certain] (source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/947484/000094748426000124/acgl-20260630.htm).

## 2. Quality
Returns are strong and improving: ROIC rose from 12.3% (FY21) to 17.1% TTM, ROE from 16.3% to 19.9% TTM [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/acgl/financials/ratios/). Leverage is low and falling (net debt/EBITDA, site definition, 0.34x FY25 → 0.57x TTM) [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/acgl/financials/ratios/). Mortgage segment underwriting is a structurally high-margin, capital-light franchise with limited new entrants, and Reinsurance combined ratio improved to 76.6% YTD Q2-26 from 78.5% a year earlier, showing underwriting discipline [Likely] (source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/947484/000094748426000124/acgl-20260630.htm). The offsetting risk: the Insurance segment combined ratio deteriorated to 98.5% in Q2-26 from 93.4% a year earlier (97.5% YTD vs 93.1%) as property pricing softens amid rising competition [Certain] (source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/947484/000094748426000124/acgl-20260630.htm). Specialty (re)insurance has no durable moat beyond underwriting skill and capital scale — returns are cyclical and pricing-dependent, not protected by switching costs or network effects [Likely].

## 3. Valuation
The market is pricing ACGL as a low-growth, cyclically-peaking insurer. P/E of 7.4x sits at just the 11th percentile of its own 5-year range (6.3x–16.1x) and is 25% below the peer median of 9.8x (RNR, WRB, CB, EG) [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/acgl/statistics/). EV/EBITDA of 6.4x is below its own 5-year minimum (6.7x) and 39% below peers' 10.4x; P/FCF of 5.3x is 32% below peers' 7.8x [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/acgl/financials/ratios/). P/B of 1.4x is roughly in line with peers (-5%) despite ACGL's superior ROE, and FCF yield is 18.79% [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/acgl/statistics/). The discount looks wide given rising, above-peer returns; it likely reflects the market pricing in the Insurance-segment margin softening noted above rather than a structural flaw, which is the key monitorable.

## 4. What would prove this wrong
- ROE (ratios page) falls below 12% for two consecutive fiscal years (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/acgl/financials/ratios/)
- Net debt/EBITDA, site definition (ratios page) rises above 1.0x (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/acgl/financials/ratios/)
- FCF conversion, FCF/NI (cash flow & income statement) falls below 100% (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/acgl/financials/cash-flow-statement/)
- Insurance segment combined ratio (10-Q filings) exceeds 100% for two consecutive quarters (source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/947484/000094748426000124/acgl-20260630.htm)

## 5. Capital allocation and cash conversion
Cash conversion is strong: FCF/NI has averaged well above 100% over five years (129.0% TTM, though down from 156.9% in FY21) [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/acgl/financials/cash-flow-statement/). Shares outstanding fell 5.02% YoY, consistent with active buybacks [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/acgl/statistics/). Per the Q2-26 10-Q, the company repurchased $1.9bn of stock in H1 2026 and has $2.2bn remaining under a board authorization increased to $3.0bn on April 19, 2026 [Certain] (source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/947484/000094748426000124/acgl-20260630.htm). Combined with low, falling leverage, this signals a balance sheet with spare capacity and management confidence despite the Insurance-segment softness.

## 6. Unusual items
None beyond the Insurance-segment margin softening already discussed in Quality. Short interest is low (2.22% of float, falling) and institutional ownership is high (92.7%) [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/acgl/statistics/) — no governance or crowding concern.
