# Sanlam Limited (JSE:SLM) — Core initiation — 2026-10-18

## 1. Business
Sanlam is a pan-African financial-services group writing life, disability, income-protection, funeral, credit-life and health insurance, plus corporate insurance, financial planning and retirement/asset-management products, through five segments: Sanlam Life and Savings, Pan-Africa, Asia, Sanlam Investments and Santam (short-term insurance) (source: https://stockanalysis.com/quote/jse/SLM/) [Certain]. It earns premium income, investment returns on policyholder float, and asset-management fees across South Africa, the rest of Africa and Asia [Certain].

## 2. Quality
ROE rose to 19.9% TTM from 12.5% in FY2021, and ROIC to 61.3% TTM from 20.4% (ratios page) [Certain] — though ROIC for an insurer mixes policyholder float with shareholder capital, so the absolute level should be read cautiously [Likely]. FCF conversion is volatile (−39.4% to 131.7% of net income across FY2021-25) but TTM conversion is 134.5%, consistent with strong underlying cash generation [Certain]. Leverage looks modest on a conservative debt-less-cash basis (net debt/EBITDA 0.19x) but the site's own definition shows net cash (−0.17x) — a conflict flagged, not resolved [Certain]. The moat is moderate rather than structural: scale, Africa/Asia distribution reach, actuarial data and licensing create switching costs and entry barriers, but insurance is price-competitive and Sanlam competes directly with Old Mutual, Discovery and Momentum Metropolitan domestically [Likely]. Beta is low (0.51, statistics page), consistent with a defensive, asset-heavy financial [Certain].

## 3. Valuation
SLM trades on 9.5x P/E — 30% up its own 5y range (8.1x-12.9x), i.e. cheap vs its own history — but +19% above the 4-peer median of 8.0x [Certain]. P/B (1.6x) sits exactly at its own 5y median and is +11% above the peer median (1.4x) [Certain]. EV/EBITDA (1.4x) and P/FCF (7.0x, near the bottom of its 5y range and −69% below the 22.3x peer median) look very cheap, but EV/EBITDA is unreliable here given the net-debt sign conflict above, and P/FCF for an insurer is distorted by non-operating investment cash flows [Likely]. Net: the market prices SLM roughly in line with peers on earnings and book value, not at a standout discount; the P/FCF/EV/EBITDA cheapness should not be read as a free lunch [Likely]. No independent fair value is computable: stockanalysis.com's FX list carries no ZAR rate, so the fact sheet's P/B+ROE and P/E fair-value model both output [data unavailable] [Certain].

## 4. What would prove this wrong
- ROE (ratios page) falls below 15% for a full fiscal year, down from 19.9% TTM — profitability reverting toward its FY2021-22 level.
- FCF conversion (cash flow/income statement) turns and stays below −20% of net income for two consecutive fiscal years — cash generation no longer backing reported earnings.
- Shares outstanding (statistics page) rise more than 5% year-on-year — a dilutive capital raise (e.g. to fund the Santam buy-in) eroding per-share value.
- Net debt/EBITDA on the site's own definition (ratios page) moves from net cash to net debt above 1.0x — a material swing in balance-sheet leverage.

## 5. Capital allocation and cash conversion
Shares outstanding are flat year-on-year (statistics page), so no dilution or buyback is distorting per-share metrics [Certain]. FCF conversion exceeded 100% of net income in three of the last five fiscal years (cash flow/income statement), supporting dividend capacity and the disclosed plan to acquire the remaining minority stake in Santam for cash (company transcript, 2026-10-05) [Likely] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/quote/jse/SLM/transcripts/763836-m-a-announcement/). Management describes the deal as structurally simplifying with modest near-term EPS accretion; this is the company's own characterization, not independently verified [Guessing].

## 6. Unusual items
The Santam minority buy-in (announced 2026-10-05) is the main near-term capital-allocation event: a cash offer to acquire the remaining Santam shares and end its separate listing, concentrating existing short-term-insurance economics rather than adding new risk, per company disclosure [Likely] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/quote/jse/SLM/transcripts/763836-m-a-announcement/). Management flagged severe-weather claims and currency volatility as earnings headwinds in the Q1/Q2 2026 calls [Likely] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/quote/jse/SLM/transcripts/678442-q2-2026/). Institutional ownership is modest (27.1%) and insider ownership negligible (0.24%, statistics page); short-interest data is unavailable [Certain].
