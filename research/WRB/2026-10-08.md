# W. R. Berkley Corporation (WRB) — Core initiation — 2026-10-08

## 1. Business
WRB is a specialty property & casualty insurer and reinsurer operating roughly 60 decentralized niche underwriting units plus a reinsurance/monoline segment [Certain] (source: https://www.berkley.com/about-us). It earns premiums for underwriting risk and investment income on the float held against reserves, with profit split between underwriting margin and investment returns.

## 2. Quality
Returns are strong and improving: ROIC rose from 11.7% to a TTM 15.7% and ROE from 15.9% to a TTM 20.2% over FY2021-25 [Certain] (source: fact sheet, Ratios page). The balance sheet has deleveraged steadily (net debt/EBITDA, site definition, 1.23x→0.19x) [Certain] (source: fact sheet, Ratios page). FCF conversion remains very high (TTM 180% of net income) though it has drifted down from 207% in FY2021 [Certain] (source: fact sheet, Cash Flow/Income Statement). The durable edge is organizational: local underwriting/claims authority inside dozens of specialty units lets WRB price niche risk faster and more precisely than larger centralized peers, and the group keeps adding units (e.g., Berkley Meridian, formed 2026-08-18) to extend that niche footprint [Certain] (source: fact sheet News; https://www.berkley.com/about-us). Rising ROE alongside falling leverage looks like a genuine quality signal rather than one driven by reserve releases.

## 3. Valuation
At 14.3x TTM P/E (45th percentile of its own 5y range of 13.3x-15.7x) and 2.8x P/B (69th percentile, 5y range 2.3x-3.0x), WRB trades roughly mid-to-upper its own history and at a premium to the CB/TRV/MKL/RLI peer median (+34% P/E, +38% P/B) [Certain] (source: fact sheet, Valuation table). That premium is explained by superior ROE/ROIC versus the peer set rather than a stretched price. On cash-generation metrics the stock screens cheaper: P/FCF of 7.8x is 23% below the peer median and FCF yield is 12.8% [Certain] (source: fact sheet). The fact sheet's own-history/peer fair-value model puts base fair value at $73.89 (+6.0% vs the $69.73 last close), with bear/bull of $60.62/$77.06 [Likely] (source: fact sheet, Fair value; inform-only model). The price is consistent with a market that pays up for return on equity but is not paying for further multiple expansion.

## 4. What would prove this wrong
- ROE (Ratios page) falls below 15% for two consecutive fiscal years, below the FY2021-25 range (15.9%-22.1%)
- Net debt/EBITDA (site definition, Ratios page) rises back above 1.0x, reversing the FY2021-25 deleveraging trend
- FCF conversion (FCF/NI, Cash Flow & Income Statement) falls below 150%, extending the current downtrend past its FY2021-25 range
- ROIC (Ratios page) falls below 12%, back toward the FY2021 trough

## 5. Capital allocation and cash conversion
Shares outstanding fell 1.11% year-over-year, consistent with net buybacks funding part of capital return alongside the regular quarterly cash dividend [Certain] (source: fact sheet, Statistics; fact sheet News, dividend headline dated 2026-09-10 — amount not used, per house rules on headline figures). FCF conversion of 180% of net income (TTM) comfortably funds both the dividend and buybacks without added leverage, and net debt/EBITDA has fallen every year since FY2021 [Certain] (source: fact sheet, Cash Flow/Ratios).

## 6. Unusual items
Short interest is modest but rising (6.35% of float, +4.0% vs prior month) [Certain] (source: fact sheet, Other/News) — not large enough to be a standalone flag. Beta is low (0.27), consistent with an insurer whose earnings are driven more by underwriting cycle and float income than by broad market moves [Certain] (source: fact sheet, Other). No governance or accounting red flags identified in the fact sheet (Piotroski F-score 6; Altman Z unavailable).
