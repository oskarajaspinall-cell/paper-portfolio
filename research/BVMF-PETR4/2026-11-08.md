# Petróleo Brasileiro S.A. - Petrobras (BVMF:PETR4) — Core initiation — 2026-11-08
## 1. Business
Brazilian state-controlled integrated oil company: upstream (deepwater pre-salt) is the profit engine, with refining, fuels distribution and gas downstream (source: https://stockanalysis.com/quote/bvmf/PETR4/). Revenue is predominantly Brazilian and BRL-reporting; an export-country split is [data unavailable] in the fact sheet, and no primary source was fetched, so no foreign country is confirmed at ~10%+ [Likely]. Earnings are driven by Brent in USD against BRL costs and by government influence over domestic pricing and dividends [Likely].

## 2. Quality
Returns are high but not through-cycle stable. ROIC was 20.5% in FY2021, peaked at 33.5% in FY2022 and fell to 16.4% in FY2025; TTM is 18.7% (source: https://stockanalysis.com/quote/bvmf/PETR4/financials/ratios/) [Certain]. Over the last 12 months ROIC rose from 14.4% to 18.7% and operating margin from 30.5% to 36.1%, so the 5-year "falling" read is partly a peak-base effect (source: https://stockanalysis.com/quote/bvmf/PETR4/financials/ratios/?p=trailing) [Certain]. FCF margin moved the other way: 21.3% to 19.2%, versus 37.3% in FY2021, and FCF conversion is 79.0% of net income [Certain]. Net debt/EBITDA is 1.29x, moderate; Altman Z is 1.53 and Piotroski F is 8 (source: https://stockanalysis.com/quote/bvmf/PETR4/statistics/) [Certain].
Moat: low-cost pre-salt reserves and a quasi-monopoly refining position, but the state shareholder can steer pricing and capital allocation, which caps the durability of returns to minorities [Likely]. Cyclical commodity exposure means current margins are not a floor [Likely].

## 3. Valuation
At 45.30 BRL the stock trades on 4.4x P/E (5y range 1.8x–13.4x, median 3.7x) and 3.3x EV/EBITDA (median 3.0x), against peer medians of 13.0x and 4.4x, discounts of 66% and 26% (source: https://stockanalysis.com/quote/bvmf/PETR4/financials/ratios/ and peer statistics pages) [Certain]. P/FCF of 6.0x is above its own 5y maximum of 4.5x, yet 29% below peers; FCF yield is 16.79% (source: https://stockanalysis.com/quote/bvmf/PETR4/statistics/) [Certain]. The mechanical DCF base fair value is 88.94 BRL (bear 61.44), and the reverse DCF implies revenue shrinking 11.4% in year 1 [Certain]; DCF is a weaker guide than the unavailable NAV/SOTP and is sensitive to the 3.0% country premium [Likely]. The price assumes a sustained earnings decline or political value leakage; the peer discount is partly deserved by state-control risk [Likely].

## 4. What would prove this wrong
- ROIC (ratios page, TTM) falls below 12%.
- Operating margin (income statement, TTM) falls below 28%.
- Net debt/EBITDA (ratios page) rises above 2.2x.
- FCF margin (cash-flow page, TTM) falls below 12%.

## 5. Capital allocation and cash conversion
FCF conversion has fallen from 158.4% in FY2021 to 79.0% TTM, implying heavy investment spend (source: https://stockanalysis.com/quote/bvmf/PETR4/financials/cash-flow-statement/) [Likely]. Shares-change and payout detail are [data unavailable]. The 16.79% FCF yield leaves room for distributions, but as a state-controlled issuer the payout can be redirected to government priorities [Guessing]. Q3 production growth and a new Amapá-block discovery point to continued reinvestment (source: https://www.reuters.com/business/energy/petrobras-third-quarter-oil-gas-output-rises-12-year-earlier-2026-10-28/ and https://www.reuters.com/business/energy/brazils-petrobras-makes-third-oil-discovery-block-off-amapa-state-2026-10-29/) [Likely].
