# Petróleo Brasileiro S.A. - Petrobras (BVMF:PETR4) — Core initiation — 2026-11-15
## 1. Business
Brazil's state-controlled integrated oil company: deep-water pre-salt exploration and production is the profit engine, with refining, fuel distribution logistics, gas and biofuels downstream. Earnings follow oil prices and BRL fuel pricing policy. Financials are reported in USD, with the share quoted in BRL (source: https://stockanalysis.com/quote/bvmf/PETR4/). Home market is Brazil; no separate foreign-country revenue share was fetched, and I [Guessing] that export crude sales to Asia are material.

## 2. Quality
Returns are high but cyclical. TTM ROIC is 18.8% vs 17.4% in FY2025, and the 5-year trend is falling (peak 34.5% in FY2022). TTM ROIC is rising over the last 12 months (13.9% to 18.8%) but is still below its 2-year high of 21.0% (source: https://stockanalysis.com/quote/bvmf/PETR4/financials/ratios/?p=trailing) [Certain]. Operating margin recovered to 34.9% TTM from 29.5% a year earlier [Certain]. The weak spot is cash conversion: FCF margin is 19.0%, vs 37.3% in FY2021, and FCF/NI is 80.0% TTM, as capex absorbs the profit [Certain]. Net debt/EBITDA is 1.05x, moderate, Piotroski F 8. Moat is low-cost pre-salt reserves, a scale and access advantage that is real but tied to the oil price and state policy [Likely]. Q3 results showed record production and refinery output with raised guidance (source: https://www.reuters.com/business/energy/brazils-petrobras-posts-215-rise-third-quarter-net-profit-2026-11-10/) [Likely].

## 3. Valuation
The price assumes little. P/E is 4.2x (20% of its 5y range; peers 12.7x), EV/EBITDA 3.2x vs peers 6.2x, P/B 1.3x vs peers 2.2x, and FCF yield 17.73% (source: https://stockanalysis.com/quote/bvmf/PETR4/statistics/) [Certain]. P/FCF of 5.6x is above its 5y max because FCF margin has fallen, so the cheapness on earnings is partly a cash-flow story [Likely]. The mechanical DCF fair value is flagged unreliable (+271% from the price, SOTP/NAV data unavailable): weigh it lightly. The discount to peers largely reflects state-control, dividend and political risk, so it is not a free lunch [Likely]. Trend: the close is 1.4% above the 200-day average, 11.0% below the 50-day average, 12-month change +40.2%, RSI 34.4 after a pullback from the 59.79 high on 2026-10-23 [Certain].

## 4. What would prove this wrong
- ROIC (ratios page, TTM) falls below 12%.
- FCF margin (cash-flow statement, TTM) falls below 12%.
- Net debt / EBITDA (ratios page) rises above 2.0x.
- Shares change YoY (statistics page) shows dilution above 3%, which would signal a state-driven capital raise.

## 5. Capital allocation and cash conversion
Shares change is -0.02% YoY, so no dilution. Capex is rising with the raised investment forecast (source: https://www.marketbeat.com/instant-alerts/transcript-petroleo-brasileiro-sa-petrobras-q3-earnings-call-highlights-2026-11-11/) [Likely], which explains FCF conversion of 80.0% vs 158.4% in FY2021. Bolt-on deals are in play: a possible BP Bumerangue stake purchase (unconfirmed, source: https://www.reuters.com/business/energy/bp-talks-sell-stake-brazil-oil-discovery-petrobras-bloomberg-news-reports-2026-11-12/) [Guessing] and the Logum buy-out (source: https://www.reuters.com/business/energy/petrobras-buy-out-logum-partners-expand-biofuels-logistics-presence-2026-11-12/) [Likely]. The market is paying for growth capex, so returns on it matter more than payout.

## 6. Unusual items
- Governance: the state controls the company, and fuel pricing, dividends and investment can follow political aims (Brazil is also exploring Venezuela interest: https://www.reuters.com/business/energy/brazil-send-energy-minister-venezuela-amid-petrobras-interest-2026-11-11/) [Likely].
- Currency: the share is quoted in BRL and financials are in USD, so BRL moves affect a USD holder; the macro overlay should check Brazil country risk (BRL, Selic, politics) [Likely].
- Beta (5y) is -0.23, which is an unreliable measure of its true tie to oil [Likely].
- Short interest [data unavailable].
