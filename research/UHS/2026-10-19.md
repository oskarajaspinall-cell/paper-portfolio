# Universal Health Services (UHS) — Core initiation — 2026-10-19

## 1. Business
UHS owns and operates acute-care hospitals and the largest standalone behavioral-health hospital network in the US, earning fee-for-service revenue from Medicare, Medicaid and commercial payors [Certain]. It completed the acquisition of Talkspace, for approximately $835m in the aggregate [company-reported] (source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/352915/000119312526340278/uhs-20260630.htm), on 2026-08-17, adding virtual/outpatient behavioral care alongside its inpatient network [Certain].

## 2. Quality
Returns have been rising for five years: ROIC 10.6%→12.4% TTM, ROCE 12.6%→16.3%, ROE 15.7%→20.9% (ratios page) [Certain]. Gross and operating margins have also expanded (+3.9pp, +0.5pp). Moat sources look structural: certificate-of-need rules restrict new hospital supply in many UHS markets, and its scale in behavioral health, now extended into virtual care via Talkspace, is hard for smaller operators to replicate [Likely]. The business is payor-concentrated: UHS cut FY2026 guidance in July on Medicaid supplemental-payment changes (Reuters headline, 2026-07-27) [Certain], and newly-added 10-Q risk language on the One Big Beautiful Bill Act's Medicaid work requirements and provider-fee caps shows this risk is live (source: SEC 10-Q delta) [Likely]. A jury verdict against subsidiary Cumberland Hospital for Children and Adolescents is now disclosed as a specific litigation risk (same source) [Certain]. FCF conversion is volatile (2.8%→98.4%→55.4% TTM) but net debt/EBITDA has fallen to 1.90x [Certain].

## 3. Valuation
UHS trades at 7.5x P/E and 5.9x EV/EBITDA, both below its own 5-year minimum (9.2x/7.2x) and 27-32% below the HCA/CYH/THC/ACHC peer median (fact sheet valuation table) [Certain]. P/FCF (12.9x) and P/B (1.5x) are roughly in line with or below the 5-year range. The reverse DCF embedded in the current price implies only 3.7% revenue growth in year one fading to terminal growth — a conservative assumption given 5-year margin and ROIC trends are still improving [Likely]. Base fair value is $218.79 (DCF-weighted 67%, EV/EBITDA 33%), 18.2% above the last close of $185.10; bear case ($26.22) assumes the DCF turns deeply negative, which looks extreme against current profitability [Guessing]. The multiple gap looks like the market pricing in Medicaid/OBBBA headwinds the stock has already partly digested (12-month change -9.7% vs SPY +14.8%), leaving room if reimbursement risk doesn't worsen further [Likely].

## 4. What would prove this wrong
- ROIC (ratios page) falls below 9%, reversing the five-year improving trend and signalling returns are being regulated/competed away.
- Net debt/EBITDA, site definition (ratios page) rises above 3.0x, showing the debt-funded Talkspace deal and ongoing capex are eroding balance-sheet discipline.
- Operating margin (income statement) falls below 9% for two consecutive quarters, showing Medicaid/OBBBA reimbursement cuts are overwhelming cost control.
- FCF conversion, FCF/NI (cash flow & income statement) falls and stays below 40%, showing reported earnings are no longer converting to cash.

## 5. Capital allocation and cash conversion
Shares outstanding fell 6.69% YoY (fact sheet, statistics page), consistent with ongoing buybacks funded by free cash flow [Certain]. FCF margin is 4.7% TTM and FCF conversion 55.4% TTM, both below their FY2024 peaks (7.1%/98.4%) partly reflecting higher capex and the Talkspace deal, financed with additional borrowing under UHS's Credit Agreement rather than equity, for approximately $835m in the aggregate [company-reported] (source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/352915/000119312526340278/uhs-20260630.htm) [Certain]. Net debt/EBITDA of 1.90x TTM leaves room to absorb that acquisition without re-rating leverage risk materially, but the trend bears watching post-close [Likely].

## 6. Unusual items
Two items from the SEC 10-Q delta not yet fully reflected in reported numbers: (1) the DC Attorney General is reviewing UHS's restructuring of physician-group operations at its George Washington University Hospital/Cedar Hill District Hospitals under the Healthcare Equity Conversion Act, with a review expected to conclude by end-Q3 2026 — UHS has already assumed management/financial responsibility, so an adverse outcome could force an unwind [Likely] (source: SEC 10-Q delta, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/352915/000119312526340278/uhs-20260630.htm). (2) The filing dropped standalone risk language on state-by-state Medicaid revenue sensitivity in favor of more specific OBBBA work-requirement/provider-fee language, suggesting the generic Medicaid risk has become a specific, dated policy risk [Likely] (same source). Short interest is low (5.25% of float) and falling slightly; institutional ownership is high (91.0%) — neither is a flag.
