# Las Vegas Sands Corp. (LVS) — Core initiation — 2026-10-08

## 1. Business
LVS owns and operates integrated resorts (casino, hotel, retail, convention/MICE) in Macao (via Sands China) and Singapore (Marina Bay Sands). Revenue is gaming-led (mass and VIP tables, slots) with non-gaming (rooms, retail, F&B, meetings) supporting visitation and margin. It no longer owns Las Vegas assets, having sold them in 2022 [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/lvs/financials/income-statement/).

## 2. Quality
LVS operates under two of the most restricted gaming licenses in the world: one of only six Macao concessions, renewed to December 2032, and one of only two Singapore casino licenses (the GRA is barred from issuing a third until January 1, 2031) under a 30-year concession from August 2006 [Certain] (source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1300514/000130051426000013/lvs-20251231.htm). This statutory scarcity — not brand or scale — is the moat; new supply cannot enter either market before the mid-2030s.

Profitability has recovered post-pandemic: TTM ROIC 19.8%, ROCE 20.5% (both negative in 2021-22), gross margin 79.3%, operating margin 23.3%, FCF margin 19.7%, FCF conversion 156.5% [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/lvs/financials/ratios/). Net debt/EBITDA fell to 2.57x TTM from 34-39x in the pandemic years [Certain] (same source). Key risk: Macao/Singapore concentration, Macao volume and PRC policy exposure, and near-term disruption — Macao renovations take ~400-500 keys offline per quarter into 2027 [Likely] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/lvs/transcripts/653175-q2-2026/). Altman Z 2.75, Piotroski F 6 [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/lvs/statistics/).

## 3. Valuation
At 35.81 USD, LVS trades on 13.9x TTM P/E and 7.6x EV/EBITDA, each ~23% below the WYNN/MGM/MLCO peer median (18.0x / 9.9x) and below LVS's own 5-year multiple range (P/E 20.1-30.4x; EV/EBITDA 12.5-182.1x, the upper end distorted by pandemic losses) [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/lvs/financials/ratios/). TTM FCF yield is 11.65% [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/lvs/statistics/). On EV/Sales (2.6x vs 2.0x peer) and P/FCF (8.6x vs 5.0x peer) LVS is pricier than peers, so the discount is concentrated in earnings multiples. P/B of 39.9x vs a 3.0x peer median is not a useful signal here — equity is thin after years of buybacks and pandemic-era losses — and is excluded from the read-through. Net: the market is pricing a cautious view of Macao earnings recovery against a license/cash-flow profile that has already normalized [Likely].

## 4. What would prove this wrong
- ROIC (ratios page) falls back below 10% for two consecutive fiscal years, reversing the post-2022 recovery.
- Net debt/EBITDA (ratios page) rises back above 4x, signalling EBITDA deterioration or renewed leverage.
- Macao quarterly EBITDA fails to trend toward management's ~$700m/quarter target over several consecutive quarters, indicating structural share loss (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/lvs/transcripts/653175-q2-2026/).
- Macao concession (due Dec 2032) or Singapore license (duopoly protected to Jan 2031) is revoked, materially amended, or not renewed ahead of schedule.

## 5. Capital allocation and cash conversion
FCF conversion is 156.5% of net income TTM, funding both deleveraging (net debt/EBITDA to 2.57x) and heavy buybacks: $787m repurchased in Q2 2026 alone, 16.3% of shares retired over the last 11 quarters, against a $6bn authorization, plus a $0.30/share quarterly dividend [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/lvs/transcripts/653175-q2-2026/). Shares outstanding are down 6.01% YoY [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/lvs/statistics/). Macao maintenance capex runs ~$600m/year; the Marina Bay Sands expansion targets opening in early 2031, the next multi-year growth leg [Certain] (same source).

## 6. Unusual items
Short interest is 6.31% of float and rose 11.8% month-on-month; institutional ownership is 42.8%, insider 25.38% [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/lvs/statistics/). The stock is down 31.5% over 12 months and sits 17.1%/30.1% below its 50-/200-day moving averages with a 14-day RSI of 19 (deeply oversold) despite the fundamental improvement above — a sentiment/macro gap rather than an earnings issue, but worth monitoring given the rising short base [Likely] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/lvs/history/). Recent headlines are routine Sands China CSR/PR items with no earnings or regulatory content; none is price-sensitive [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/lvs/).
