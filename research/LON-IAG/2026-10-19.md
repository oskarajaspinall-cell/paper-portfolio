# International Consolidated Airlines Group (LON:IAG) — Core initiation — 2026-10-19

## 1. Business
IAG is the parent of British Airways, Iberia, Aer Lingus, Vueling and LEVEL, plus IAG Loyalty. It earns fares and ancillary revenue from premium long-haul (BA, Iberia) and short-haul/low-cost flying, plus cargo and loyalty-programme income, across hubs at Heathrow and Madrid [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/quote/lon/IAG/).

## 2. Quality
Airlines are structurally capital-intensive and cyclical, with limited pricing power against fuel and labour costs — not an obviously durable-moat business [Likely]. But IAG's returns have improved sharply post-COVID: ROIC rose from -24.6% (FY2021) to 27.5% TTM, ROCE from -13.9% to 19.0%, operating margin from -34.8% to 15.0%, and net debt/EBITDA fell from 4.74x (FY2022) to 0.74x TTM (source: https://stockanalysis.com/quote/lon/IAG/financials/ratios/). What protects these returns is scarce Heathrow slots and BA's premium long-haul brand, plus IAG Loyalty, not a cost advantage versus low-cost peers [Likely]. Piotroski F-score is only 4/9, a caveat on earnings-quality momentum despite the strong trend [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/quote/lon/IAG/statistics/). Management said the H1 2026 Middle East conflict hit capacity and fuel costs (Q2 profit down 16% y/y) while also boosting BA's premium long-haul from travellers avoiding the region — a cyclical/geopolitical swing, not a franchise change [Likely] (source: https://www.reuters.com/world/uk/british-airways-owner-iag-misses-second-quarter-profit-view-2026-07-31/, as listed in the fact sheet).

## 3. Valuation
The market prices IAG as a structurally cheap, no-growth cyclical. TTM P/E of 7.4x sits at the 32nd percentile of its 5y range (3.3x–16.0x), 42% below the peer median (12.7x) [Certain]. EV/EBITDA of 3.5x is below its own 5y minimum (4.4x) and 12% below peers; P/FCF of 5.4x is 55% below peers, with an 18.6% FCF yield [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/quote/lon/IAG/financials/ratios/ and peer pages in the fact sheet). P/B of 2.5x is a 106% premium to peers' 1.2x, but book value was negative for part of the 5y window, so this multiple is noisy [Likely]. The fact sheet's fair-value methods disagree: through-cycle DCF implies +407.9% upside (2,106.83 GBX) while EV/EBITDA implies only +7–12% (399.53–463.76 GBX) — suggesting the DCF overstates fair value here; EV/EBITDA is the more credible anchor [Guessing] (source: fact sheet fair-value table). Multiples price in no re-rating from depressed sentiment despite improved returns and leverage.

## 4. What would prove this wrong
- Operating margin (income statement page, https://stockanalysis.com/quote/lon/IAG/financials/income-statement/) falls below 10% (loses the post-COVID margin recovery).
- Net debt/EBITDA (ratios page, https://stockanalysis.com/quote/lon/IAG/financials/ratios/) rises back above 2.0x (reverses the deleveraging that underpins the buyback).
- FCF margin (cash-flow/income-statement pages) falls below 5% (cash generation no longer supports capital returns).
- ROIC (ratios page) falls below 15% (returns revert toward the industry's structurally poor norm).

## 5. Capital allocation and cash conversion
TTM FCF conversion is 128.6% of net income and FCF margin is 11.8%, funding both deleveraging (net debt/EBITDA to 0.74x) and buybacks: shares outstanding fell 8.59% y/y, consistent with the multi-year buyback programme referenced in recent news (source: https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/iag-launches-final-eu500-mln-share-buyback-program, as listed in the fact sheet; no euro figure is used here as it is not a stockanalysis.com or primary-source number) [Likely]. This combination — shrinking share count, falling leverage, strong cash conversion — is the clearest evidence of disciplined capital allocation [Certain].

## 6. Unusual items
Jet-fuel cost sensitivity: oil-price moves have driven short-term share weakness per recent headlines (source: https://invezz.com/news/2026/10/08/iag-share-price-heres-why-the-british-airways-parent-is-falling-today/) [Likely] — a real cost exposure, not a reason to avoid on its own given the margin cushion. Institutional ownership is modest at 24.2%, insider ownership negligible at 0.09% (source: https://stockanalysis.com/quote/lon/IAG/statistics/) [Certain]. Beta of 1.36 confirms above-market cyclicality [Certain]. Next earnings (Nov 6, 2026) is a near-term catalyst that could move the margin/leverage metrics above [Certain].
