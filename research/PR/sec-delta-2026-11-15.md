- **Added**: Crude oil prices have been highly volatile over the past year. NYMEX WTI prices declined through 2025, reaching a low of approximately $55.27 per barrel in December 2025, reflecting global supply growth, including increased OPEC+ production, and concerns regarding global economic growth, inflation, and international tariffs and trade policies. In early 2026, hostilities in the Middle East and resulting disruptions to key shipping routes through the Strait of Hormuz materially curtailed crude oil exports from the region, and NYMEX WTI prices reached a high of $112.95 per barrel on April 7, 2026. Oil prices subsequently declined during the second quarter of 2026 due to developments regarding the potential reopening of the Strait of Hormuz, but have since risen from second-quarter ...
- **Added**: Throughout 2025 and 2026, natural gas prices in the Permian Basin have been adversely impacted by pipeline capacity constraints out of the basin, pipeline maintenance, and higher production levels. These factors have led to lower or, during certain periods, negative regional gas prices being realized for natural gas sales at the Waha Hub in West Texas. Notably, Waha natural gas prices traded as low as negative $9.52 per MMBtu on April 16, 2026. More recently, Waha natural gas prices have returned to positive territory and averaged approximately $1.75 per MMBtu during the third quarter of 2026, driven primarily by additional pipeline capacity out of the Permian Basin.
- **Added**: Lower commodity prices and lower futures curves for oil and gas prices can result in impairments of our proved oil and gas properties or undeveloped acreage and may materially and adversely affect our operating cash flows, liquidity, financial condition, results of operations, future business and operations, and/or our ability to finance planned capital expenditures.
- **Added**: Due to the cyclical nature of the oil and gas industry, fluctuating demand for oilfield goods and services can put pressure on the pricing structure within our industry. The cost of oilfield goods and services are closely linked to commodity price trends, rising when prices increase and decreasing when prices fall. In addition, the U.S. experienced elevated inflation during 2025 and 2026 due to concerns, among other things, over international conflicts, tariffs, potential sanctions and higher energy prices as discussed above. Inflationary pressures such as these may also result in increases to the costs of our oilfield goods, services and personnel, which can in turn cause our capital expenditures and operating costs to rise.
- **Added**: On January 7, 2026, we completed a corporate reorganization pursuant to which we, among other things, reorganized under a new public holding company (the “Reorganization”). In connection with the Reorganization, the public holding company prior to the Reorganization became a wholly owned subsidiary of the new public holding company, which, following completion of the Reorganization, changed its name to “Permian Resources Corporation,” became the successor issuer of the prior public holding company and replaced the prior public holding company, with its shares of Class A Common Stock continuing to trade on the NYSE on an uninterrupted basis.

These acquisitions are part of our ongoing bolt-on and grassroots acquisition programs with the acquired acreage directly offset to our existing core position in the Delaware Basin allowing for seamless integration, extended lateral lengths and higher working interest in existing properties.
