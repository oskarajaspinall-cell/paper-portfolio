# Hess Midstream LP (HESM) — Core initiation — 2026-10-19

## 1. Business
Hess Midstream owns fee-based gathering, processing, storage and terminaling infrastructure in the Bakken shale (North Dakota) [Certain]. Its sole upstream customer is Chevron (via the Hess merger, closed July 2025), under long-term contracts with minimum-volume commitments [Certain]. Revenue is tariff/fee-driven, not commodity-price exposed [Likely].

## 2. Quality
Margins are high and stable: 77.5% gross, 62.1% operating, ROCE 24.7% TTM and rising (+2.1pp over 5y) (source: stockanalysis.com ratios page). FCF margin is high (51.9% TTM) but falling (-7.6pp over 5y) [Certain]. Net debt/EBITDA is a moderate 3.00x, rising (+0.22x over 5y); senior unsecured debt is rated BBB-/Ba1 [company-reported] (https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1789832/000119312526338055/hesm-20260630.htm).

The moat is narrow: contracted fees give revenue visibility, but there is a single customer and a single basin's production profile. On 2026-10-06 Chevron announced it will divest ownership interests in Hess Midstream and combine Bakken operations with its DJ Basin assets into an "independent multi-basin midstream company," citing extended contracts and a lower unit count as offsets [Likely] (source: https://www.reuters.com/business/energy/chevron-divest-ownership-interests-hess-midstream-dj-basin-crude-midstream-2026-10-06/, as listed in the fact sheet). Q2 2026 throughput fell 15% (oil terminaling) y/y on lower Chevron Bakken drilling [company-reported] (https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1789832/000119312526338055/hesm-20260630.htm) — returns durability hinges on Chevron's drilling pace and the new structure's terms, neither in HESM's control [Likely].

## 3. Valuation
The stock fell ~15.6% on 2026-10-07 on the restructuring news and is down 23.5% over three months (source: stockanalysis.com history page), closing at 30.66 vs a 52-week range of 29.85-41.44 and 19.9%/19.6% below its 50-/200-day averages; RSI is 22.1 (source: stockanalysis.com statistics page). P/E of 10.6x is below its 5y range (12.7x-20.0x) and 21% below the peer median (13.3x); EV/EBITDA (8.2x) and P/FCF (7.5x) sit above HESM's own 5y range but below peer medians (11.0x, 16.4x) (source: stockanalysis.com ratios/statistics pages). The reverse DCF implies the price assumes -19.3% revenue in year 1 — consistent with the market pricing the deal-related EBITDA step-down, not a confident "cheap" signal (source: fact sheet valuation section). The large base/bull fair-value gaps rest on pre-deal history and should be treated cautiously until post-restructuring terms are confirmed in a primary filing [Guessing].

## 4. What would prove this wrong
- ROCE (ratios page) falls below 20% (vs 24.7% TTM), signalling the new structure dilutes returns rather than protecting them.
- Net debt/EBITDA (ratios page) rises above 3.5x (vs 3.00x TTM) without a matching step-up in contracted EBITDA.
- Gross margin (income statement) falls below 74% (vs 77.5% TTM) as throughput declines (Q2 2026 oil terminaling -15% y/y) persist beyond the restructuring.
- FCF margin (cash flow/income statement) falls below 40% (vs 51.9% TTM, already on a falling trend).

## 5. Capital allocation and cash conversion
TTM FCF margin is 51.9% and FCF/NI conversion is 223% — still comfortably cash-generative despite a falling trend [Certain]. Shares outstanding rose 20.44% y/y (source: stockanalysis.com statistics page), which is dilutive for per-unit holders even as the partnership also funded a Class B unit repurchase and accelerated share repurchase (ASR) via revolver borrowings in H1 2026 [company-reported] (https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1789832/000119312526338055/hesm-20260630.htm) — buybacks funded with debt add leverage risk rather than reduce it.

## 6. Unusual items
Material: on 2026-10-06 Chevron announced a restructuring combining HESM's Bakken assets with Chevron's DJ Basin midstream assets into an independent multi-basin company, with HESM shares falling ~15.6% the next session on reduced near-term earnings expectations against longer contracts [Likely] (source: https://www.reuters.com/business/energy/chevron-divest-ownership-interests-hess-midstream-dj-basin-crude-midstream-2026-10-06/, as listed in the fact sheet). The company also issued revised FY26/FY27 guidance around the transaction, per third-party reporting, but the specific figures are not usable here as they appear only in headlines, not a primary filing [Guessing]. Short interest is 6.03% of float and rose 19.1% month-over-month; institutional ownership is high at 92.2% (source: stockanalysis.com statistics page).
