# Hess Midstream LP (HESM) — Core initiation — 2026-10-18
## 1. Business
Hess Midstream owns gathering, processing, storage and export infrastructure for crude oil, natural gas and produced water in North Dakota's Bakken shale, earning tariff and processing fees under mostly fixed-fee/cost-of-service contracts with Chevron (anchor customer since its 2025 acquisition of Hess) [Certain]. A pending transaction has Chevron divesting its entire LP/GP ownership while HESM acquires Chevron's DJ Basin assets, becoming an independent multi-basin platform [company-reported] (source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1789832/000119312526418053/d401552dex991.htm).

## 2. Quality
High margins and returns: gross margin 77.5%, operating margin 62.1%, ROCE 24.7% TTM and rising [Certain]. The moat is contractual: long-dated, largely fixed-fee agreements with a high-credit-quality anchor customer, backed by hard-to-replicate infrastructure [Likely]. The Oct 2026 Chevron transaction extends Bakken contract terms to 2045 (from 2033) and sets an 80% minimum revenue commitment [company-reported] (https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1789832/000119312526418053/d401552dex991.htm), lengthening and de-risking the moat. Offsetting this, 2027 adjusted EBITDA guidance of $850-950m [company-reported] (https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1789832/000119312526418053/d401552dex991.htm) sits well below the prior Bakken-only trajectory. Net debt/EBITDA is stable near 3.0x TTM [Certain]; senior unsecured debt is rated BBB-/Ba1 as of 2026-06-30 [company-reported] (https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1789832/000119312526338055/hesm-20260630.htm). FCF margin improved to 51.9% TTM but the 5-year trend is falling (-7.6pp) [Certain].

## 3. Valuation
Current EV/EBITDA of 8.2x is 174% above its own 5-year range (4.6x-6.6x) [Certain], yet 34% below the peer median of 12.4x (WES, MPLX, TRGP, EPD); P/E of 10.6x is 21% below the peer median [Certain]. The fact sheet's mechanical DCF-based fair value (base $106.97 vs the $30.66 close, +249%) is built on trailing financials that predate the Oct 6-7 transaction and excludes the 2027 EBITDA guidance [company-reported] (https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1789832/000119312526418053/d401552dex991.htm) or the ~40% cut in units outstanding from Chevron's unit cancellation [company-reported] (https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1789832/000119312526418053/d401552dex991.htm); treat this upside as stale, not a signal [Likely]. The reverse DCF already implies the price assumes a 19.3% revenue decline in year 1 [Certain]. The stock is 19.9% below its 50-day average and set a fresh 52-week low this week (29.85 on 2026-10-15) [Certain]; whether post-close per-unit economics justify re-rating toward peer multiples is the real question, not the stale fair value.

## 4. What would prove this wrong
- Net debt/EBITDA (ratios page) rises above 3.5x once the DJ Basin acquisition and the $200m cash payment to Chevron close.
- FY2027 adjusted EBITDA (company reporting) comes in below the $850m low end of guidance (8-K EX-99.1), showing the new contract/volume mix is underperforming.
- Gross margin (income statement) falls below 70%, indicating the DJ Basin or third-party volumes carry structurally worse economics than the legacy Bakken book.
- The transaction fails to close by year-end 2026 (per the 8-K timeline) or closes on materially worse terms than disclosed.

## 5. Capital allocation and cash conversion
FCF margin is 51.9% TTM [Certain], historically funding distributions and unit buybacks; the 10-Q discloses a Class B unit repurchase and ASR funded by revolver borrowings in Q2 2026 [company-reported] (https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1789832/000119312526338055/hesm-20260630.htm). Shares outstanding are +20.44% YoY per stockanalysis.com [Certain], a figure that predates the ~40% unit cancellation from the Chevron transaction [company-reported] (https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1789832/000119312526418053/d401552dex991.htm) and should reverse sharply once it closes. That deal is funded by a $200m cash payment to Chevron [company-reported] (https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1789832/000119312526418053/d401552dex991.htm) plus assumption of the DJ Basin assets, not new equity, limiting near-term dilution risk.

## 6. Unusual items
Chevron, HESM's controlling owner and primary customer, announced 2026-10-06 it will divest 100% of its GP and LP ownership (77.8m Class B units + 449k Class A shares, canceled) while HESM acquires Chevron's DJ Basin crude/gas assets (incl. a 20% stake in the Saddlehorn pipeline) for $200m cash, extends Bakken contracts to 2045 and sets an 80% minimum revenue commitment; expected to close by year-end 2026 [company-reported] (https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1789832/000119312526418053/d401552dex991.htm). The stock fell roughly 15.5% on the news and sits near its 52-week low; short interest rose 19.1% over the past month to 6.03% of float [Certain] (fact sheet). This transaction is the dominant swing factor for the thesis over the next two quarters [Likely].
