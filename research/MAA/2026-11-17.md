# Mid-America Apartment Communities (MAA) — Core initiation — 2026-11-17
## 1. Business
Apartment REIT: rents multifamily units in the Southeast, Southwest and Mid-Atlantic. [company-reported] 294 communities across 16 states at quarter-end, no foreign revenue (source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/912595/000119312526437813/maa-20260930.htm). Earnings are rent less property costs, funded by debt, so rent growth, occupancy and interest cost drive FFO. [Certain]

## 2. Quality
Mediocre returns, weakening. ROIC is 5.1% TTM against 5.5% a year earlier (-0.5pp) and a 2-year high of 6.1%; ROE is 6.9% and falling (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/maa/financials/ratios/). [Certain] Net debt/EBITDA is 4.74x TTM, up from 3.64x in FY2023: leverage is rising while returns fall. [Certain]
Moat is modest: scale, a Sunbelt footprint and an investment-grade balance sheet, but apartments are a competitive, supply-driven market. [Likely] Management says same-store revenue declined in Q3 on occupancy and flat effective rent, but renewals were strong, turnover improved and new deliveries are declining (source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/912595/000119312526437813/maa-20260930.htm). [company-reported] Earnings-call summary points to a delayed new-lease recovery and a narrowed, lower 2026 core FFO range, with October leasing better (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/maa/transcripts/719961-q3-2026/). [Likely]

## 3. Valuation
Current P/E is 35.3x, 31% of the way up its 5y range and +108% against peers (n=3), but P/E understates REIT earnings because of depreciation. EV/EBITDA is 15.9x, below its 5y minimum of 16.0x and 14% under peers; EV/Sales 8.8x is also below its 5y minimum. P/B is 2.6x, near the bottom of its range. FCF yield is 1.85% (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/maa/statistics/). [Certain]
Mechanical fair value (P/FFO proxy only; NAV unavailable) is 137.79 base, 126.02 bear and 145.73 bull, against a last close of 117.82: +16.9% to base. [Likely] The price assumes no rent recovery; cheap on enterprise multiples, but earnings are being cut and the stock sits 8.1% below its 200-day average, so it is cheap and not yet working. [Likely]

## 4. What would prove this wrong
- Net debt/EBITDA (ratios page) rises above 5.5x.
- ROIC TTM (trailing ratios page) falls below 4.5%.
- Same-store revenue growth (10-Q MD&A) is negative for two further consecutive quarters, or guidance for core FFO is cut again.
- Variable-rate debt is refinanced at costs that push core FFO per share below the 2026 range lower end in the next full-year guidance (company release).

## 5. Capital allocation and cash conversion
FCF margin and conversion are [data unavailable] on the fact sheet. Shares outstanding fell 0.50% YoY (buyback, small). [Certain] [company-reported] MAA redeemed its Series I preferred on 1 Oct, removing a higher-cost layer and modestly lifting FFO per share; it also holds $1.2 billion of variable-rate debt (commercial paper, term loan, revolver), exposing it to rates (source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/912595/000119312526437813/maa-20260930.htm).
