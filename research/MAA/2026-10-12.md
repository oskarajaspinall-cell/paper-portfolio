# Mid-America Apartment Communities (MAA) — Core initiation — 2026-10-12

## 1. Business
MAA is a REIT that owns, operates, acquires, develops and redevelops apartment communities, earning revenue mainly from residential rents [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/maa/). Portfolio: ~104,698 units across 16 states plus Washington, D.C., concentrated in Sunbelt (Southeast/Southwest) and Mid-Atlantic markets [Likely] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/maa/). Growth comes from same-store rent/occupancy, lease-up of a development pipeline, and selective acquisitions funded by equity, debt and retained cash flow [Likely].

## 2. Quality
Apartment demand is structurally durable (non-discretionary housing), and Sunbelt concentration has historically tracked faster population/job growth than coastal markets [Likely] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/maa/). But the moat is thin: apartments are a commodity asset class any well-capitalized landlord can build in the same submarkets, so returns compress when deliveries rise. ROIC is stuck at 5.2% TTM (only +1.2pp over five years) and ROE has fallen from 9.0% (FY2021) to 7.0% TTM [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/maa/financials/ratios/). Leverage is creeping up: net debt/EBITDA rose from 3.6x (FY2023) to 4.6x TTM [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/maa/financials/ratios/), cutting balance-sheet flexibility just as the stock has lagged SPY by 29.6pp over 12 months [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/maa/history/) — consistent with the market pricing a multi-year Sunbelt supply glut pressuring rents, not a company-specific problem. Piotroski F-score of 4 (mid-range) corroborates modest, not deteriorating, fundamentals [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/maa/statistics/).

## 3. Valuation
At 116.86, MAA trades on 15.8x EV/EBITDA, below its own 5-year minimum (16.0x) and 17% below the peer (AVB/EQR/ESS/UDR/CPT) median of 19.1x; EV/Sales (8.8x) and P/B (2.5x) sit at or below their 5-year floors and 23-24% below peer medians [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/maa/financials/ratios/). P/E (34.2x) looks rich mainly because REIT depreciation distorts earnings; it is still in the cheapest 26% of its own 5-year range. The fact sheet's base fair value (P/AFFO-proxy method) is 138.15, 18.2% above last close, with bear/bull of 126.35/146.12 (+8.1%/+25.0%) [Certain] (inform-only, never a buy signal; source: fact sheet). The price therefore assumes the leverage build and Sunbelt oversupply persist well beyond what the 5-year multiple history or peer group implies — a structurally worse residential-REIT environment than ESS/AVB currently command, not merely a cyclical dip.

## 4. What would prove this wrong
- Net debt/EBITDA (ratios page) rises above 5.0x (vs 4.62x TTM), showing leverage is not stabilizing.
- ROIC (ratios page) falls below 5.0% (vs 5.2% TTM) for two consecutive fiscal years, showing supply pressure is structural, not cyclical.
- ROE (ratios page) falls below 6.5% (vs 7.0% TTM).
- Piotroski F-score (statistics page) falls below 4, indicating broad fundamental deterioration.

## 5. Capital allocation and cash conversion
Shares outstanding fell 0.16% YoY [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/maa/statistics/) — modest buybacks, no material dilution. Gross/operating/net margin, FCF margin and FCF conversion are [data unavailable] on stockanalysis.com for this REIT, so cash conversion can't be verified directly; FCF yield is 1.48% [Certain] (source: fact sheet statistics page). MAA redeemed all outstanding 8.50% Series I preferred stock for cash in August 2026 [Certain] (source: https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/mid-america-apartment-communities-to-redeem-all-outstanding-shares-of-its-8-50-series-i-cumulative-redeemable-preferred-stock-302862372.html), retiring a high-cost capital layer — a modest positive given leverage is already rising.

## 6. Unusual items
Short interest rose 12.6% month-over-month to 4.26% of float [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/maa/statistics/) — not large in absolute terms, but worth monitoring given 12-month underperformance. Institutional ownership is high at 94.3% [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/maa/statistics/). Low beta (0.73) reflects the sector's bond-like volatility profile, not safety from supply-driven earnings risk. No governance or legal red flags found in allowlisted sources.
