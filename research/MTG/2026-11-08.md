# MGIC Investment Corp (MTG) — Core initiation — 2026-11-08
## 1. Business
MGIC writes private mortgage insurance on low-down-payment US home loans, sold to lenders and required by Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac (the GSEs). It earns premiums on insurance in force, plus investment income, and pays claims when insured borrowers default. [Certain] Revenue is essentially all US; no foreign country is near 10%. [Likely] (source: https://www.mgic.com, https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/mtg/)

## 2. Quality
Returns are good and steady: ROIC 12.9% TTM against 13.2% in FY2025 and a range of 12.1%-16.5% over five years; ROE 13.8% TTM (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/mtg/financials/ratios/). [Certain] FCF conversion of 102.3% TTM shows earnings turn into cash. Net debt/EBITDA is 0.71x, low. Piotroski F is 6 (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/mtg/statistics/). [Certain]

The moat is regulatory and capital-based, not pricing power: GSE-approved insurers must hold PMIERs capital, and a few approved peers compete on price. [Likely] Direction: TTM ROIC is down 0.6pp on a year earlier and below its 2-year high of 13.8% (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/mtg/financials/ratios/?p=trailing). That is mild drift, not a break. [Certain] Latest results showed continued earnings and favourable reserve development, with management expecting gradual credit normalisation (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/mtg/transcripts/719985-q3-2026/). [Likely]

## 3. Valuation
At 28.60 USD the stock trades at P/E 8.9x, above its 5y maximum of 8.9x and its 7.5x median, versus peers at 8.8x. EV/EBITDA is 6.7x (5y median 5.9x, peers 6.1x). P/B is 1.2x (5y median 1.1x, peers 1.0x). P/FCF is 8.0x against peers at 6.6x (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/mtg/statistics/). [Certain] FCF yield is 12.44%. The shares are priced at the top of their own history and at a premium to peers, so little cyclical normalisation is priced in. [Likely]

Mechanical fair value is 38.85 USD base (35.8% above the close), 32.06 bear and 39.79 bull, driven mainly by P/B plus sustainable ROE; the P/E leg is much higher than the market multiple. [Certain] That gap suggests the fair value leans on current ROE persisting; it is one estimate, not a signal. [Guessing]

## 4. What would prove this wrong
- TTM ROIC (ratios page, trailing view) falls below 10%.
- Net debt/EBITDA (site definition, ratios page) rises above 1.5x.
- FCF conversion (FCF/net income) falls below 70% on a TTM basis.
- The quarterly report states MGIC no longer meets PMIERs, or the GSEs cut PMIERs credit for its reinsurance (SEC filings page).

## 5. Capital allocation and cash conversion
Shares outstanding fell 11.51% year on year, so buybacks are large and are the main use of cash, alongside a quarterly dividend of 0.17 USD per share declared on 2026-10-29 (source: https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/mgic-investment-corporation-announces-quarterly-dividend-302924109.html). [Certain] Cash conversion is full (FCF/NI 102.3%), and leverage is low, so returns look funded from earnings, not debt. [Likely] Buybacks at the top of the 5y P/E range earn less than they did earlier. [Likely]

## 6. Unusual items
- Policy risk is the main one. The latest quarterly report adds language on GSE reform and an October 2026 Executive Order that could lead to a pilot to identify borrowers eligible to cancel mortgage insurance, which could shorten policy life and cut insurance in force; scope and timing are unspecified (source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/876437/000087643726000033/mtg-20260930.htm). [Likely]
- FHFA also reviews GSE pricing and credit-score changes (VantageScore 4.0 now available to all approved lenders), which may alter the business mix. [Likely]
- The bank capital proposal was revised in March 2026 and keeps the existing treatment of mortgage insurance, removing a prior threat (same filing). [Likely]
- The sector fell on FHFA Director posts in September (source: https://www.tipranks.com/news/the-fly/mgic-investment-nmi-holdings-essent-fall-after-pulte-posts-thefly-news). [Likely]
- Short interest is 6.50% of float, up 42.2% on the prior month. [Certain]
- Beta is 0.62, but the risk is housing credit and policy, not market beta. [Guessing]
