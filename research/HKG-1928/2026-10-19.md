# Sands China Ltd. (HKG:1928) — Core initiation — 2026-10-19
## 1. Business
Sands China owns and operates integrated resorts in Macau — casino gaming (VIP and mass-market) plus hotels, retail, dining and MICE — across the Cotai Strip and peninsula, including the Venetian, Parisian, Four Seasons, Londoner and Sands Macao. It holds one of Macau's six government-granted gaming concessions and is majority-owned by US-listed Las Vegas Sands. Revenue splits between gaming and non-gaming hotel/retail/F&B. [Certain]

## 2. Quality
Returns have recovered sharply post-pandemic: ROIC 18.3% TTM and ROCE 17.0% TTM, up from deeply negative in FY2021-22, with gross margin 79.2% and operating margin 16.2% TTM (source: ratios/income statement pages). [Certain] The moat is regulatory and capital-scale: Macau caps gaming concessions at six operators (Sands China, Galaxy, Wynn Macau, MGM China, SJM, Melco), and Cotai's integrated-resort format is capital-intensive to replicate, giving incumbents durable positioning. [Likely] Competitors can't easily take share without new concession capacity, but all six run similar-scale resorts, so returns are shared across the group, not Sands-specific — a good business, not clearly better than peers. [Likely] Leverage is improving: net debt/EBITDA fell to 2.75x TTM from 36.25x in FY2021 (source: ratios page), but Altman Z is 2.49 — the "grey zone," not safe — reflecting the balance sheet's capital intensity (source: statistics page). [Certain]

## 3. Valuation
All five multiples sit below their own 5-year range: P/E 13.5x vs a 5y min of 20.8x, EV/EBITDA 8.5x vs min 12.9x, P/FCF 6.9x vs min 11.3x (source: ratios/statistics pages) — the market prices the recovery well below what it has historically paid. [Certain] FCF yield is 14.4% TTM. [Certain] Versus peers (Galaxy, Wynn Macau, MGM China, SJM), it is cheaper on EV/EBITDA (-6%) and P/FCF (-8%) but pricier on P/E (+41%) and far pricier on P/B (+434%, peer median 1.7x) — the P/B gap reflects much higher ROE, not an undervalued balance sheet. [Likely] Base fair value (HKD 56.72) implies 394% upside; the bear case is negative (HKD -21.75) (source: fair-value table) — a DCF artifact of a modelled severe multi-year revenue contraction, not a real floor. [Guessing] Reverse DCF: the price implies -15.8% revenue growth in year one, fading to terminal growth — pessimistic given TTM margins are already recovered. [Likely]

## 4. What would prove this wrong
- ROIC (ratios page) falls back below 10%, erasing the post-pandemic recovery.
- Net debt/EBITDA (ratios page) rises back above 4x.
- Operating margin (income statement) falls below 12% for two consecutive half-years.
- FCF margin (cash flow/income statement) turns negative again.

## 5. Capital allocation and cash conversion
FCF conversion is 194% of net income TTM and FCF margin 21.8% (source: cash-flow/income statement pages), funding continued deleveraging (net debt/EBITDA down from 3.44x in FY2023 to 2.75x TTM) alongside ongoing Cotai capex such as the Londoner Grand, which Sands China and Marriott opened in Macau to add hotel/retail capacity (source: https://www.tipranks.com/news/the-fly/marriott-sands-china-to-open-londoner-grand-in-macao). [Likely] Dividend data is not shown on the fact sheet. [data unavailable] Conversion this high relative to net income is partly depreciation on the resort asset base rather than pure cash generation, so it should moderate as capex normalizes. [Guessing]

## 6. Unusual items
The Altman Z-score of 2.49 sits in the distress "grey zone" (not the safe zone above ~3), consistent with a capital-intensive, leveraged resort balance sheet rather than acute risk given the improving leverage trend. [Certain] News flow since mid-2025 is limited to routine filing announcements (H1/H2 earnings and annual reports) with nothing new that would change the thesis; the only operationally relevant item is the Londoner Grand opening (new non-gaming capacity), already reflected above. [Likely] No short-interest or insider-ownership data is available on the fact sheet. [data unavailable]
