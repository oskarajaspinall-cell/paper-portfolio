# CME Group Inc. (CME) — Core initiation — 2026-10-11

## 1. Business
CME Group operates the world's largest derivatives exchange group, listing futures and options across interest rates, equity indices, FX, energy, agriculture and metals, plus cash markets (BrokerTec, EBS) [Certain] (source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1156375/000115637526000009/cme-20251231.htm). Revenue comes mainly from per-contract clearing/transaction fees, market data sales (~53% non-US), and licensing of benchmark indices such as the exclusive S&P 500 futures license and Term SOFR administration [Certain] (same source).

## 2. Quality
ROIC rose 7.1%→12.2% and ROE 9.8%→15.8% over FY2021-TTM, with operating margin 65.7%, net margin 63.4%, FCF margin 62.3%, 98.3% FCF/NI conversion and net debt/EBITDA only 0.33x [Certain] (source: factsheet). The moat is structural: as central counterparty, CME is a systemically important financial market utility, and 85% of its volume comes from members whose trading-right/fee protections raise switching costs; it also holds long-dated exclusive index licenses (Nasdaq to 2039, Russell to 2037, S&P 500 futures exclusivity) locking flagship contracts away from rivals [Certain] (source: 10-K). Deep, self-reinforcing liquidity (record interest-rate volumes for three straight years, per the 10-K) makes it hard for entrants to pull open interest away once established [Likely]. Recent disclosures point to continued record Q3 volumes and international growth, supporting demand [Likely] (source: PRNewswire link in Sources). Risk: crypto and sports-linked launches are small and unproven next to core volume [Guessing].

## 3. Valuation
P/E (23.8x) sits near the bottom of its 5y range (12th percentile) and in line with the peer median (23.7x, ICE/CBOE/NDAQ) — not rich on earnings. But EV/EBITDA (21.5x), EV/Sales (15.2x) and P/FCF (23.9x) carry 34-49% premiums to peers, and P/B (3.8x) is above its own 5y maximum, though still below peers [Likely] (source: factsheet). The fact sheet's DCF/FCFE blend puts base fair value at 206.10 (-26.5% vs the 280.56 close), with even the bull case (236.73) below the market price; the gap narrows sharply on the P/E method alone (base 283.49) [Certain] (source: factsheet). The reverse DCF implies the market prices roughly 13.3% year-one revenue growth fading to terminal by year ten — a high bar for a decade [Likely] (source: factsheet). Net: a very good business priced for durable above-trend growth, with little margin of safety on the model the fact sheet weights most.

## 5. Capital allocation and cash conversion
FCF conversion is 98.3% of net income on a TTM basis and net debt/EBITDA is only 0.33x, so growth and any dividend are funded internally with spare capacity [Certain] (source: factsheet). Specific dividend/buyback payout figures are not shown on the fact sheet or in the 10-K section reviewed, so the payout ratio itself is [data unavailable]; cash generation is judged here on conversion and leverage alone.

## 6. Unusual items
The Altman Z-score of 0.53 (statistics page) reads as distress, but the model is built for asset-heavy industrials; CME is a capital-light clearinghouse with ~100% gross margin and minimal fixed assets, so this score is not a meaningful distress signal here [Likely]. Short interest is low (1.64% of float, up 3.1% month-on-month) and institutional ownership is high (91.0%) — neither is material to the thesis [Certain] (source: factsheet).
