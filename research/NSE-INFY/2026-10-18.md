# Infosys Limited (NSE:INFY) — Core initiation — 2026-10-18
## 1. Business
Infosys sells IT services and consulting (application development/maintenance, infrastructure, BPO, digital/AI transformation) to large global enterprises, mostly billed by time-and-materials or fixed-price contracts under multi-year relationships [Certain]. Revenue is concentrated in US/Europe clients delivered substantially via an India-based offshore workforce [Likely].

## 2. Quality
Returns are strong and improving: ROIC 41.7% TTM, up from 36.7% in FY2022; ROCE 38.4% TTM, up from 33.8% (source: ratios page) [Certain]. This reflects a durable moat in switching costs (multi-year enterprise contracts) and a labour-cost advantage versus onshore-delivery peers, not a cyclical spike [Likely]. Gross margin has fallen from 32.6% (FY2022) to 29.7% TTM and operating margin from 23.0% to 20.4% (source: income statement) — a structural erosion worth monitoring, likely wage inflation and pricing pressure [Likely]. Text-only risk: the US suspended new/pending PERM (green card) applications for eight IT firms including Infosys in October 2026; Infosys stated "no material impact" [Likely] (source: https://www.ndtv.com/world-news/infosys-on-trumps-green-card-crackdown-perm-suspension-no-material-impact-12169008#publisher=newsstand). A visa-dependent delivery-model risk worth tracking, though management's statement and peers' low filing volumes suggest limited near-term exposure [Guessing].

## 3. Valuation
INFY trades at 15.2x P/E and 10.2x EV/EBITDA, both below its own 5-year range minimum (16.3x, 11.8x) and roughly in line with peers (P/E peer median 15.8x, EV/EBITDA peer median 10.0x; peers NSE:TCS, NSE:WIPRO, NSE:HCLTECH, CTSH) [Certain]. P/FCF (13.1x) is below its 5y minimum (14.5x) and 15% cheaper than peers, while P/B (5.1x) sits above the peer median (3.6x), reflecting high ROE rather than book-value cheapness [Certain]. The reverse DCF embedded in the price implies just -0.9% revenue growth in year 1 fading to 2.5% terminal growth — a pessimistic assumption [Likely]. The price has fallen 21.2% over 12 months and sits 6.1% below its 200-day average, consistent with a genuine growth-outlook de-rating rather than a temporary dislocation [Likely]. Base fair value of INR 1,514.40 implies +30.4% upside to the last close of INR 1,161.20 (DCF 67%/EV-EBITDA 33% blend) [Certain], inform-only.

## 4. What would prove this wrong
- Operating margin (income statement page) falls below 18% on a TTM basis, confirming margin erosion has become structural rather than cyclical.
- ROIC (ratios page) drops below 30% TTM, indicating competitive pressure has eroded the pricing/cost advantage.
- Net new large-deal bookings or revenue guidance (next earnings, Oct 23, 2026) show a sustained growth slowdown beyond the already-priced-in near-zero growth.
- Evidence the PERM/green-card suspension materially raises onshore delivery costs or client attrition over two or more quarters.

## 5. Capital allocation and cash conversion
FCF conversion (FCF/NI) is strong at 114.5% TTM, up from 103.1% in FY2022, and FCF margin is 18.7% TTM (source: cash flow/income statement) [Certain]. Net debt/EBITDA is -0.49x (net cash position), giving balance-sheet flexibility for buybacks/dividends without leverage risk [Certain]. Shares outstanding fell 1.37% year-over-year, consistent with net shareholder-friendly capital return (source: statistics page) [Likely].

## 6. Unusual items
Altman Z-score 9.44 and Piotroski F-score 5 indicate no distress signal [Certain]. Institutional ownership is 66.9% with insiders at 2.56% (source: statistics/news section); beta is unusually low at 0.12, likely reflecting the stock's INR-denominated, defensive earnings profile versus USD benchmarks rather than genuine low volatility [Guessing].
