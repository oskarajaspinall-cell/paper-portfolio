# OneMain Holdings (OMF) — Core initiation — 2026-11-09
## 1. Business
Non-prime US consumer lender: it earns the spread between interest on personal loans (branch network plus digital) and its secured and unsecured funding, plus credit card, servicing and insurance revenue. Profit is net interest income less credit losses and operating costs. Funding is heavily securitised: $13.6bn of receivables were pledged as collateral at 30 Sept 2026 [company-reported] (source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1584207/000158420726000041/omf-20260930.htm). US-only; no foreign revenue concentration [Likely].

## 2. Quality
- ROIC is not meaningful for a lender; ROE is the better gauge: 22.7% TTM vs 16.0% in FY2024 and 40.2% in FY2021, a falling 5-year trend (-16.5pp) [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/omf/financials/ratios/). ROIC TTM 2.9% is flat on a year earlier (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/omf/financials/ratios/?p=trailing).
- Returns are well above a cost of equity but cyclical, and fell as credit costs rose from 2021. [Likely]
- Moat: underwriting data, a branch network and securitisation market access in non-prime credit; not a durable pricing advantage, since larger banks and fintechs can lend into the same segment. [Likely]
- Piotroski F is 4 (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/omf/statistics/). Margins, cash flow and leverage ratios are [data unavailable] for this industry.

## 3. Valuation
P/E is 8.5x, at 51% of its 5y range (4.7x–12.2x, median 9.2x) and in line with peers (8.7x median, -2%). P/B is 1.9x, 55% of its 5y range and +64% above the 1.2x peer median (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/omf/financials/ratios/). The premium to peers rests on the higher ROE, but it leaves no valuation cushion. [Likely]

Mechanical fair value is P/E-based only (FCFE and DCF failed): base 60.48 USD, +7.8% against the 56.09 close; bear 30.68 (-45.3%), bull 66.75 (+19.0%). The skew is poor: modest upside against a large loss if earnings multiples compress with credit. [Likely] FCF yield of 50.03% is a lender cash-flow artefact (loan flows), not a signal; ignore it. [Certain]

The price is 13.7% lower over 3 months and trails SPY by 16.4pp over 12 months, so some credit-cost worry is priced. [Certain]

## 4. What would prove this wrong
- ROE (ratios page) falls below 15% on a TTM basis (22.7% now).
- Provision for finance receivable losses (income statement) grows more than 30% YoY for two consecutive quarters (the 10-Q shows +26% in Q3 on receivables growth and higher net charge-offs).
- Piotroski F (statistics page) falls below 3 (4 now).
- Shares change YoY (statistics page) turns positive (-2.49% now), signalling dilution to fund capital.

## 5. Capital allocation and cash conversion
Capital return is the thesis. Q3 dividend was $1.05 per share and YTD repurchases were $173m, against $70m a year earlier [company-reported] (source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1584207/000158420726000041/omf-20260930.htm). Share count is down 2.49% YoY (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/omf/statistics/). Leverage is high: long-term debt was $24,074m against equity of $3,391m [company-reported] (source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1584207/000158420726000041/omf-20260930.htm). Growth is funded by debt; interest expense rose 8% in Q3 on higher average debt and cost of funds [company-reported] (source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1584207/000158420726000041/omf-20260930.htm). Cash conversion is [data unavailable] on stockanalysis.com.
