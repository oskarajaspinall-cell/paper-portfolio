# LATAM Airlines Group (SNSE:LTM) — Core initiation — 2026-11-09
## 1. Business
Latin America's largest airline group: passenger revenue (domestic Brazil, Chile and other Spanish-speaking markets, plus international) and a cargo arm. [Certain] Revenue and costs are in USD; the shares quote in CLP. Geographic revenue shares are [data unavailable]: the company's own site is not on the allowlist. Brazil is plausibly ~10%+ of revenue, with local-currency exposure [Likely]. The macro overlay should check Brazil and Chile. (source: https://stockanalysis.com/quote/snse/LTM/)

## 2. Quality
Returns are high for an airline. ROIC is 25.4% TTM against 32.1% in FY2025 and -23.8% in FY2021 (source: https://stockanalysis.com/quote/snse/LTM/financials/ratios/). Operating margin is 13.7% and FCF margin 13.0%, with FCF conversion of 132.4% of net income. [Certain]
Direction is a concern. ROIC, ROCE and operating margin are all falling over the last 12 months, from 30.1% to 25.4% for ROIC and from 15.5% to 13.7% for operating margin (source: https://stockanalysis.com/quote/snse/LTM/financials/ratios/?p=trailing). They sit 3.5-5.3pp below their 2-year highs. [Certain] The Q3 headlines point to a fuel-cost spike, partly offset by higher unit revenue, and a trimmed earnings outlook (source: https://www.reuters.com/business/latam-airlines-trims-earnings-outlook-fuel-costs-bite-2026-11-03/). [Likely]
Moat is moderate. Dominant hubs and slots in a consolidated region help, but airlines are cyclical and fuel-exposed. Returns look near a cycle peak. [Likely]

## 3. Valuation
P/E is 8.7x against a 5y median of 9.6x, at the 69th percentile of its range, and below the peer median of 10.3x (DAL, UAL, CPA). EV/EBITDA is 4.9x, below the 5y minimum of 5.0x and 39% under peers at 8.0x. P/FCF is 6.4x against a 5y median of 4.7x. FCF yield is 15.62%. [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/quote/snse/LTM/statistics/)
P/B of 6.2x is far above the peer 2.1x, but a depleted post-restructuring equity base distorts it. [Likely]
The mechanical fair value is flagged unreliable: the two methods disagree 10.4x and the base case sits +268% from the price. Weigh it lightly. [Certain]
The price assumes earnings fade from peak: cheap on cash flow, but not on P/E versus its own history. Earnings-quality risk is fuel, not leverage. [Likely]
