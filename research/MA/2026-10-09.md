# Mastercard Incorporated (MA) — Core initiation — 2026-10-09

## 1. Business
Mastercard runs a four-party card network (issuer–acquirer–merchant–cardholder), earning fees per transaction and on payment volume for switching, processing and cross-border settlement, plus value-added services (fraud scoring, data/analytics, loyalty, Agent Pay). It carries no credit or interest-rate risk — issuers and acquirers bear that — so revenue scales with global payment volume, not a loan book. [Certain]

## 2. Quality
Mastercard switched 175.5bn transactions in FY2025 (source: 10-K) [Certain]. Network/scale economics are rare and rising: ROIC 93.8% and ROCE 64.3% TTM, both up for five straight years (73.2%→93.8%, 41.9%→64.3%) [Certain]. Operating margin 59.9%, net margin 46.3%, FCF margin 47.6%, FCF conversion 102.7% TTM [Certain] (fact sheet: Ratios/Income statement/Cash flow). The four-party model creates structural switching costs for issuers, acquirers and merchants that Visa shares but new entrants cannot easily replicate [Likely]. Durability risks are real: the 10-K discloses active US, UK, Netherlands and Portugal merchant-interchange litigation dating to 2011, and names interchange-fee regulation/surcharging as its top forward-looking risk [Certain] (source: 10-K). A WSJ headline (2026-10-08) reports a US interchange-fee bill has "gained Trump as an ally," raising odds of federal action [Likely] (source: https://www.wsj.com/finance/regulation/the-credit-card-bill-that-banks-fear-most-has-gained-trump-as-an-ally-013551b3). The filing also flags real-time account-to-account rails as a structural disintermediation threat [Certain].

## 3. Valuation
P/E (31.6x), EV/EBITDA (23.3x) and EV/Sales (14.7x) all sit below their own 5-year minimums (-30%/-28%/-16%); P/FCF (30.1x) is near the 5y median (+3%) [Certain]. P/B (89.9x, +159% vs 5y max) is distorted by book value shrunk by buybacks and is not a reliable signal here [Likely]. Versus peers (V, AXP, PYPL, GPN), MA trades at large EV/EBITDA and EV/Sales premiums, but AXP's bank-model balance sheet and PYPL's thinner margins make the comparison imperfect [Likely]; the premium broadly matches MA's far higher ROIC and margins. Fair value (FCFE-DCF/DCF blend): bear $304.10, base $360.87, bull $466.75, all below last close $574.76 (-47%/-37%/-19%) [Certain]. The reverse-DCF implies ~20% year-1 revenue growth fading to terminal; a fixed 2.5% terminal-growth DCF structurally caps fair value for a high-ROIC, low-capital compounder, so the gap flags margin of safety rather than disqualifying the name [Likely].

## 5. Capital allocation and cash conversion
FY2025 returned $17.6bn to shareholders ($14.5bn buybacks + $2.8bn dividends) [Certain] (source: 10-K) against a 47.6% TTM FCF margin and >100% FCF conversion [Certain] (fact sheet, cash-flow/income statement). Shares outstanding fell -2.35% YoY [Certain] (fact sheet, statistics page). Net debt/EBITDA of 0.59x [Certain] leaves headroom to keep repurchasing through any near-term litigation settlement or regulatory fine.
