# Emaar Development PJSC (DFM:EMAARDEV) — Core initiation — 2026-10-11

## 1. Business
Dubai build-to-sell developer: develops and sells residential and commercial units, plus development-management services; also does construction, facilities management and design implementation [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/quote/dfm/EMAARDEV/). Revenue is recognised as units are sold and built out, so results track off-plan launch and construction volumes rather than a recurring rental book.

## 2. Quality
Returns are exceptional and still rising: TTM ROCE 18.3% (+3.8pp over 5y), ROE 40.8% (+9.0pp), net margin 42.5% (+20.4pp), gross margin 56.5% (+20.0pp) [Certain] (fact sheet, Ratios/Income statement pages). FCF conversion has stayed well above 100% of net income every year (TTM 129.4%), because buyers fund construction through progress payments before costs are incurred — a structurally asset-light model with a net-cash balance sheet (net debt/EBITDA −3.0x) [Certain]. The risk to durability: ROIC jumped +268pp over five years to the low hundreds of percent by FY2025, a level so far above history it reads as a cyclical peak, not a sustainable run-rate [Likely]. Returns depend on Dubai launch/absorption velocity and the group's land pipeline and brand, not a moat that is obviously insulated from a citywide downcycle — unlike Abu Dhabi-diversified peer Aldar, this is single-city, single-sector exposure [Likely].

## 3. Valuation
Every earnings-based multiple sits well below its own 5-year range and peers: P/E 3.9x is below the 5y minimum (4.3–7.2x), 27% under the peer median (5.4x); EV/EBITDA 0.6x is 82% below peers (3.5x); EV/Sales 0.3x is 84% below peers (2.1x); FCF yield is 33%. Yet P/B (1.2x) is 42% *above* the peer median (0.8x) — the market is not marking down book assets, it is discounting the earnings stream, consistent with pricing a cyclical reversion from the margin/ROIC peak [Likely]. The stock sits near its 52-week low (12.05 vs last close 12.82), below both the 50- and 200-day averages, underperforming SPY by 24pp over 12 months on a low beta (0.45) — a laggard, not a crowded trade. The fact sheet's DCF fair value (base AED 86.98) extrapolates the ROIC spike to an implausible upside; the multiple-vs-peer read above is more reliable [Guessing].

## 4. What would prove this wrong
- Net margin (income statement) falls below 30% for two consecutive quarters — faster normalisation than priced.
- Net debt/EBITDA (ratios page) turns positive (loses net-cash status) — funding model deteriorates.
- ROCE (ratios page) falls below 10% on a full-year basis — returns compress toward typical developer levels.
- FCF margin (cash flow/income statement) turns negative for a full fiscal year — presales/collections stall.

## 5. Capital allocation and cash conversion
FCF conversion has exceeded 100% of net income in every one of the last five years (FY2025 185.9%, TTM 129.4%) [Certain], because the build-to-sell model collects cash from buyers ahead of construction spend. The balance sheet is net cash (net debt/EBITDA −3.0x), giving flexibility to self-fund new launches without dilutive capital raises [Certain]. Shares outstanding are flat (−0.25% YoY), so there is no buyback or stock-based dilution distorting returns [Certain]; the fact sheet shows no dividend data, so payout policy is [data unavailable].

## 6. Unusual items
Institutional ownership is only 6.3% of shares [Certain] (fact sheet, Statistics page), consistent with a small free float typical of UAE developers with large founder/sovereign-linked stakes — a liquidity and governance consideration; short-interest data is unavailable so crowding cannot be assessed. Single-city (Dubai), single-sector (residential/commercial development) concentration is a macro risk this peer set (Aldar aside) shares. Altman Z 3.30 (safe zone) and Piotroski F 6 show no distress signal; no earnings-type price reaction in the last 10 sessions.
