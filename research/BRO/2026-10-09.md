# Brown & Brown, Inc. (BRO) — Core initiation — 2026-10-09
## 1. Business
Brown & Brown is a U.S. insurance brokerage with two segments: Retail (59% of 2025 commissions/fees, $3.39bn) sells P&C, employee-benefits and personal insurance plus advisory fees to commercial, public-entity and individual clients; Specialty Distribution (41%, $2.38bn) comprises MGU programs (Arrowhead), wholesale brokerage (Bridge Specialty) and specialty/captive services. Revenue is commissions, fees and contingent (profit-sharing) commissions; it takes no underwriting risk on brokered business [Certain] (source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/79282/000119312526046984/bro-20251231.htm).

## 2. Quality
Returns are compressing: ROIC fell from 12.0% (FY21) to 8.6% (FY25, TTM 9.5%); ROCE from 12.0% to 7.4% (TTM 10.8%); ROE from 14.8% to 11.2% (TTM 10.0%) [Certain] (source: fact sheet). Margins have held up — operating margin flat near 28-36%, FCF margin ~22-25% [Certain]. The moat case rests on scale (714 locations, ~22,400 employees), diversified revenue lines and a decentralized, ~20% employee-owned culture that reduces producer turnover [Likely] (source: 10-K), but AJG, MMC and AON run the same roll-up playbook, limiting true differentiation [Likely]. Net debt/EBITDA climbed from 1.52x to 3.45x (FY21-25) funding the RSC/Accession deal, partly financed by 17.1% YoY share dilution [Certain] (source: fact sheet), diluting per-share quality. The 10-K also flags Accession integration, captive-tax and litigation risk [Likely] (source: 10-K). Durable fee income, but the debt/dilution step-up tempers the "wonderful business" case near-term.

## 3. Valuation
The price assumes a de-rating versus both own history and peers, but not a growth collapse. P/E 20.1x sits 32% below its 5y range and roughly at the peer median (19.8x, +1%) [Certain]. EV/EBITDA 9.8x is 24% below the peer median (13.0x) and far below its own 5y range (17.2x-20.9x) [Certain] — reflecting the EBITDA step-up from Accession plus higher net debt, not a genuine re-rating [Likely]. P/FCF (14.8x vs peer 17.9x, -17%) and P/B (1.7x vs peer 4.5x, -63%) are both cheaper than peers [Certain]. The fact sheet's own-history fair value (inform-only) puts base case $72.46 (+13.7% vs last close $63.75) and the bear case $67.05 (+5.2%) above the last close [Likely] (source: fact sheet). Net: the market is pricing in slower growth and leverage risk post-Accession, not a value trap — though the cheap EV/EBITDA and P/B partly reflect the debt-funded scale-up rather than pure mispricing [Likely].

## 4. What would prove this wrong
- ROIC (ratios page) falls below 8% for two consecutive fiscal years, confirming returns keep compressing post-Accession.
- Net debt/EBITDA (ratios page, site definition) rises above 4.0x, signalling the M&A roll-up is outrunning cash generation.
- Organic revenue growth (income statement / earnings releases) turns negative for two consecutive quarters outside the 2026 H1 dip.
- Altman Z-score (statistics page) falls below 1.8, moving from the current grey zone into distress territory.

## 5. Capital allocation and cash conversion
FCF conversion is strong (120% of net income TTM, rising) with an FCF margin of ~22-24% [Certain] (source: fact sheet). But capital has gone overwhelmingly into M&A (43 acquisitions in 2025) rather than buybacks: shares outstanding rose 17.1% YoY and net debt/EBITDA rose to 3.45x at FY25-end to fund the RSC/Accession deal [Certain] (source: fact sheet; 10-K). Dividend and buyback programs exist per the 10-K but amounts are not disclosed on the fact sheet [data unavailable]. Debt-plus-equity funding of a large deal dilutes near-term per-share cash conversion even though consolidated FCF conversion looks healthy [Likely].

## 6. Unusual items
Altman Z-score is 1.80, in the "grey zone" (<1.8 distress, >3 safe) [Certain] (source: fact sheet) — a flag given the recent debt step-up, though Piotroski F-score of 7/9 is solid [Certain]. Short interest is 4.78% of float, down 3.2% m/m; institutional ownership is 86.4% [Certain] (source: fact sheet). The 10-K discloses pending litigation against subsidiary Oxford Risk Management Group over a 2024 captive-policy restructuring and an IRS risk around Accession's Section 831(b) captive-advisory business [Likely] (source: 10-K). Recent headlines (Marcus & Millichap partner expansion, new EB hire, a workplace award) are immaterial to the decision [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/bro/).
