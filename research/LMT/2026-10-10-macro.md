# Macro overlay: Lockheed Martin Corporation (LMT) — 2026-10-10

## Assessment
LMT's bull case rests on a valuation gap (DCF base fair value $612.84 vs close $509.59, built on a 10y Treasury risk-free rate of 5.22%) [Certain], not on a cyclical earnings call — revenue is multi-year, sole-source government contract backed, and beta is 0.10 [Certain]. The relevant chain is **real yields → DCF discount rate → fair value/multiple**, not earnings: LMT's book is overwhelmingly USD-denominated and insulated from FX translation or credit-spread funding stress [Likely] **(inference)**.

Facts (FRED, 2026-10-08/09): the 10y Treasury yield is 5.22%, +0.68pp over 3 months; the 10y real yield (TIPS) is 2.87%, +0.56pp over 3 months; 10y breakevens are flat at 2.33% (-0.04pp 1m); the 2y yield is 4.75% vs effective fed funds 3.88% [Certain]. Market pricing (proxy, **inference**): a 2y yield ~0.9pp above fed funds, with breakevens flat, implies the market already expects no near-term cuts, attributing the yield rise to real-rate/term-premium repricing, not inflation fear. VIX (15.41, falling) and HY spreads (3.15%, +0.45pp) show no risk-off trigger for a safe-haven bid.

Since the current 5.22% risk-free rate is already the model's input, the risk is incremental: a further rise mechanically shrinks the stated fair-value premium (bear) even with fundamentals unchanged; a reversal (Fed cuts as growth/inflation cool) widens it and supports re-rating (bull). The move is already largely reflected and the market's own forward pricing shows no near-term reversal, so no fresh surprise — small bear tilt on duration sensitivity of the valuation case only.
