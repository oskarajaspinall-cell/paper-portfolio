# Macro overlay: SBM Offshore N.V. (AMS:SBMO) — 2026-10-19

## Assessment
Chain: real yields & HY credit spreads → cost of capital (DCF discount rate; project-finance debt cost) → fair value multiple and marginal FPSO-project returns for a still-leveraged lessor (net debt/EBITDA 3.26x TTM, down from 8.28x FY2021, fact sheet). Facts [Certain]: US 10y real yield (DFII10) 2.78% on 2026-10-15, +0.43pp over 3m (https://fred.stlouisfed.org/graph/fredgraph.csv?id=DFII10); US HY spread (BAMLH0A0HYM2) 3.23% on 2026-10-15, +0.52pp over 3m (https://fred.stlouisfed.org/graph/fredgraph.csv?id=BAMLH0A0HYM2); effective fed funds (DFF) 3.88% on 2026-10-15, +0.25pp over 1m (https://fred.stlouisfed.org/graph/fredgraph.csv?id=DFF). Market pricing (proxy, **(inference)**): 2y Treasury (DGS2, 4.77%, 2026-10-15) sits ~0.89pp above fed funds, consistent with the market still pricing further tightening, not cuts — a policy-path proxy, not a forecast. Real yields and credit spreads rising together signal a genuine cost-of-capital tightening, which hits a leveraged, long-duration lessor more than a typical industrial mid-deleveraging. Revenue is mostly locked into 15-20y fixed contracts, insulating near-term earnings, so the channel runs through the valuation multiple and future project-financing terms (e.g. the $465m FSO Chalchi financing, closed June 2026), not current cash flow. **Bull**: yields/spreads ease → lower discount rate, cheaper refinancing → multiple re-rates toward peer EV/EBITDA. **Bear**: both keep rising → discount rate and refinancing costs up on new awards → the EV/EBITDA premium (+30% vs peers) widens, capping upside despite cheap equity multiples. **Tilt**: small, toward bear — deleveraging, returns and record backlog still dominate the thesis; macro only caps re-rating pace.
