# International Business Machines Corporation (IBM) — Core initiation — 2026-10-10

## 1. Business
IBM earns revenue from three segments: Software (~45% of revenue — hybrid cloud/Red Hat, Data, Automation, Transaction Processing), Consulting (~30% — systems integration and advisory), and Infrastructure (the rest — zSystems mainframes, distributed infrastructure, storage) [Certain]. Software and Consulting are subscription/contract-like and recurring; Infrastructure is cyclical, swinging with the multi-year zSystems product-launch cycle (source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/51143/000005114326000077/ibm-20260722xex991.htm).

## 2. Quality
Margins have expanded steadily over five years: gross margin 58.1% TTM (+3.3pp), operating margin 18.5% (+9.0pp), net margin 15.5% (+5.7pp); ROIC has risen to 14.0% TTM from 7.8% in FY2021 and ROCE to 11.0% from 5.7% [Certain] (source: fact sheet, Ratios/Income Statement pages). FCF margin is a healthy 20.0% TTM and FCF still exceeds net income, though conversion has fallen 72.7pp over five years as net income normalizes [Likely]. The durable edge is switching costs in mainframe software/Red Hat and multi-year consulting contracts, but net debt/EBITDA of 3.47x TTM is high for the earnings quality, and Infrastructure is genuinely cyclical: Q2 2026 IBM Z revenue fell 42% y/y even as Distributed Infrastructure rose 37% and Software grew 5% [Certain] (source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/51143/000005114326000077/ibm-20260722xex991.htm). That single-segment shortfall, not a broad execution failure, drove the "miss" narrative in the headlines [Likely].

## 3. Valuation
At 227.13 USD, IBM trades on 20.2x P/E — the bottom of its own 5-year range (19.9x-77.7x) and 19% below the peer median of 25.0x; EV/EBITDA (16.5x) and P/FCF (15.5x) are also below peers (-5% and -39%) [Certain] (source: fact sheet). Only P/B (6.2x) sits above peers (+30%), reflecting thin book equity rather than overvaluation. However, the fact sheet's own DCF/EV-EBITDA blend puts base fair value at 205.24, 9.6% below the last close; only the bull case (241.17, +6.2%) clears the current price [Certain]. So the stock looks cheap against peers and its own trading history, but not against its own cash-flow-based fair value: the market is already paying for continued Software/Consulting growth while Infrastructure normalizes [Likely]. The reverse DCF implies ~5.7% year-1 revenue growth, close to management's raised FY2026 guidance, leaving little room for further re-rating without a genuine beat.

## 4. What would prove this wrong
- Operating margin (income statement page) falls below 17% (vs 18.5% TTM)
- Net debt/EBITDA (ratios page) rises above 4.0x (vs 3.47x TTM), reversing five years of deleveraging
- Software segment revenue growth (quarterly release) falls below 3% y/y for two consecutive quarters
- ROIC (ratios page) falls back below 11% (vs 14.0% TTM), reversing five years of improvement

## 5. Capital allocation and cash conversion
Shares outstanding rose 1.26% y/y [Certain] (source: fact sheet, statistics page) — IBM is a net issuer, not a repurchaser, consistent with funding the Red Hat-era debt load and dividend. Net debt/EBITDA of 3.47x is still above a typical investment-grade tech peer [Likely]. FCF margin of 20.0% TTM and FCF yield of 6.44% comfortably support the dividend, but FCF conversion's 72.7pp five-year decline shows net income growing faster than cash generation, worth monitoring [Likely] (source: fact sheet, cash flow/income statement pages).

## 6. Unusual items
A securities-law "investigation" press release (Hagens Berman, 2026-10-01) cites an "IBM Z shortfall" and a "$68 billion" market-cap move; this corroborates the real 42% Q2 IBM Z revenue decline but is a plaintiff-firm solicitation, not a confirmed claim or regulatory action, and the dollar figure in the headline is not a stockanalysis.com number [Guessing] (source: https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/international-business-machines-corporation-ibm-68-billion-wipeout-amid-z-shortfall-triggers-investigation---hbss-302896343.html). Short interest is low (2.39% of float, +1.8% m/m) and institutional ownership is high (66.2%), so there is no crowding risk [Certain] (source: fact sheet, statistics page).
