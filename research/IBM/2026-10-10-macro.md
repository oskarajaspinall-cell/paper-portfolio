# Macro overlay: International Business Machines Corporation (IBM) — 2026-10-10
## Assessment
Chain: real yields -> cost of equity (DCF discount rate) -> IBM's fair-value range; secondarily, dollar strength -> FX translation -> reported earnings (IBM derives roughly half of revenue outside the US **(inference)**, from segment mix).

Facts (FRED, 2026-10-08/09): the 10y real yield (DFII10) is 2.87%, +0.56pp over 3m; the 10y Treasury (DGS10) is 5.22%, +0.68pp over 3m — the rate the fact sheet's own DCF already uses, so today's base fair value (205.24, -9.6% vs the 227.13 close) is current-dated, not stale [Certain] (https://fred.stlouisfed.org/graph/fredgraph.csv?id=DGS10). Breakeven inflation (T10YIE) is roughly flat (2.33%, -0.04pp 1m), so the real-yield climb looks term-premium/growth-driven, not an inflation surprise [Likely]. The 2y yield (DGS2, 4.75%) sits 87bp above effective fed funds (DFF, 3.88%) — a proxy, not a forecast, for the market pricing further tightening rather than cuts **(inference)** [Likely]. The dollar (DTWEXBGS) rose ~2.4% in the past month to 121.4 (2026-10-02) [Certain], a modest translation headwind into the Oct 21 print.

Bull: real yields plateau or retreat, the discount rate eases, and base fair value migrates back toward/above price with no change to IBM's numbers — reinforcing the "cheap vs peers/history" case.
Bear: the real-yield climb extends, pushing base fair value (205) further below price and raising refinancing cost on elevated leverage (3.47x net debt/EBITDA); dollar strength adds translation drag.

Tilt: moderate toward bear — a +0.56pp/3m real-yield move is large and mechanically embedded in the valuation model, on top of the company-specific Z-segment miss already driving the de-rating.
