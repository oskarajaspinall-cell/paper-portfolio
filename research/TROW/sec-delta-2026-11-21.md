- **Added**: Global equity markets generated mixed returns during the third quarter of 2026 as investors balanced resilient corporate earnings against renewed escalation in the U.S.-Iran conflict, uneven labor market data, and persistent inflation pressures. Higher energy prices and a tighter monetary policy backdrop contributed to divergent performance across regions, market capitalizations, investment styles, and sectors.
- **Added**: U.S. equity markets were mixed, with large-cap shares outperforming small- and mid-cap shares and value stocks outperforming growth stocks across market capitalizations. Within the S&P 500 Index, energy advanced by double-digit gains and information technology, health care, and communication services advanced, while utilities recorded double-digit losses and industrials and business services, real estate, consumer discretionary, materials, and consumer staples declined.
- **Added**: International equity markets were mixed during the third quarter as developed European markets posted gains in July and August before declining in September, while developed Asian markets increased. Japanese equities were supported by semiconductor and other artificial intelligence-related shares, as well as a weaker yen following the Bank of Japan’s July policy meeting where it held its policy rate at 1.00%. Emerging market equities were roughly flat, as strength in semiconductor-related companies in South Korea and Taiwan was offset by pressure on energy-importing markets and weakness in China related to soft domestic demand and property-sector data.
- **Added**: Global fixed income markets generated negative returns during the third quarter of 2026 as government bond yields moved higher and several major central banks shifted toward tighter monetary policy. Persistent inflation pressures, elevated energy costs, geopolitical uncertainty, and concerns about government debt supply weighed on bond prices across developed and emerging markets.
- **Added**: The Federal Reserve maintained the federal funds target range at 3.50% to 3.75% in July, with three policymakers dissenting in favor of an immediate increase. In September, the Federal Reserve unanimously raised the target range to 3.75% to 4.00%, its first increase in more than three years. Updated projections indicated that most policymakers expected another increase before year-end.
- **Added**: U.S. fixed income markets recorded negative returns as Treasury yields increased across the curve, with the Federal Reserve’s September rate increase, stronger economic data, and firm inflation readings leading investors to price a higher policy-rate path. Within the investment-grade bond universe, mortgage-backed securities declined the most, followed by corporate bonds and U.S. Treasuries, while non-agency commercial mortgage-backed securities and asset-backed securities also decreased.
- **Added**: International and emerging markets also declined during the quarter. Developed market sovereign yields rose as tighter monetary policy and higher energy costs sustained inflation concerns, while weakness in several major currencies against the U.S. dollar reduced U.S. dollar-based returns. Emerging market hard currency debt underperformed local currency debt as rising U.S. Treasury yields pressured dollar-denominated sovereign bonds, while the U.S. dollar strengthened in September, weighing on currencies in several oil-importing economies.
