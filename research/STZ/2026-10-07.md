# Constellation Brands (STZ) — Core initiation — 2026-10-07

## 1. Business
Constellation imports and sells high-end beer in the US (Modelo Especial, Corona, Pacifico) — roughly the bulk of profit — plus a smaller, lower-growth Wine & Spirits portfolio (premium/luxury wine brands, plus newer RTD spirits like Mi CAMPO and the newly acquired SpikedAde) [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/stz/transcripts/702832-q2-2027/). Beer is exclusively brewed in Mexico and sold in the US under license, giving it scale/distribution advantages over smaller imported-beer competitors [Likely].

## 2. Quality
TTM ROIC 12.9% and ROCE 15.9%, both up from near-zero in FY2022-23, with operating margin a high 33.0% and FCF margin 20.0% (FCF conversion 95.6% of net income) (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/stz/financials/ratios/, https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/stz/financials/income-statement/). Gross and operating margins have drifted down over five years (-1.8pp, -2.1pp) [Certain], consistent with heavier marketing spend management flagged is "a return to healthy levels" post-Q2 2027 (~10-11% of net sales in H2) (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/stz/transcripts/702832-q2-2027/) [Likely]. The moat is brand/distribution scale in imported beer — Modelo and Corona are top-selling US beer brands with marketing budgets peers can't match — but management itself calls out industry-wide softening beer demand [Likely] (source: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/07/constellation-brands-earnings-beer-demand.html), and Wine & Spirits remains a drag with no clear strategic resolution disclosed [Guessing]. Net debt/EBITDA 2.96x is manageable but not low (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/stz/financials/ratios/).

## 3. Valuation
At 10.4x P/E and 8.8x EV/EBITDA, STZ trades below its own 5-year minimum on every multiple (P/E -58%, EV/EBITDA -49%, EV/Sales -57%, P/FCF -30%, P/B -66%) and below the peer median on P/E (-53%) and roughly in line on EV/EBITDA (-5%) and P/FCF (+1%) (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/stz/financials/ratios/). FCF yield is 9.2% (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/stz/statistics/). The price assumes either a structural re-rating of US beer growth lower, or embeds the Wine & Spirits drag and net-debt load more heavily than peers' balance sheets [Likely]. The gap to peers on P/E specifically may partly reflect the small peer sample (n=2) rather than a clean read [Guessing]. Given reaffirmed FY27 EPS guidance of $11.20-$11.90 and possible upside to the high end on September trends (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/stz/transcripts/702832-q2-2027/), the current multiple looks to undervalue a still-growing, high-margin beer franchise.

## 4. What would prove this wrong
- Operating margin (income statement page) falls below 30% for two consecutive quarters, signalling pricing/mix deterioration beyond the current 5y trend.
- Net debt/EBITDA (ratios page) rises above 3.5x, indicating balance-sheet deterioration beyond the current comfortable range.
- FCF margin (cash flow/income statement pages) falls below 15%, indicating cash conversion breaking down.
- ROIC (ratios page) falls below 8%, indicating returns compressing toward the cost of capital.

## 5. Capital allocation and cash conversion
FCF conversion is strong (95.6% TTM, 106.4% in FY2026) (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/stz/financials/cash-flow-statement/). Management returned $530m to shareholders via buybacks year-to-date with $2.5bn of authorization remaining through FY2028, while still funding the Veracruz brewery expansion (~85% complete, due online FY2028, adding ~$75m annual depreciation) (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/stz/transcripts/702832-q2-2027/) [Certain]. Shares outstanding fell 3.32% YoY, consistent with the buyback (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/stz/statistics/).

## 6. Unusual items
Short interest is 5.40% of float and rose 15.2% in the past month (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/stz/statistics/) — a modest bearish positioning build worth monitoring but not yet large [Likely]. Institutional ownership is high at 88.3% (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/stz/statistics/). Beta of 0.43 is unusually low for a stock with this price volatility (-19.1% vs 200-day MA), likely reflecting a long low-beta history pre-dating the recent de-rating [Guessing].
