- **Added**: Our financial performance in the third quarter of 2026 reflected strong year-over-year earnings growth. Net earnings applicable to common shareholders increased to $262 million, or $1.78 per diluted share, from $221 million, or $1.48 per diluted share, in the third quarter of 2025. The increase was primarily driven by improved credit performance. Customer deposit growth exceeded loan growth year-over-year, enabling the reduction of higher-cost wholesale funding.
- **Added**: Compared with the preceding quarter, net earnings decreased from $452 million, or $3.05 per diluted share, as second quarter results included $252 million of pre-tax net gains, primarily from sale of Visa Class B-1 shares and unrealized gains on Small Business Investment Company (“SBIC”) investments, net of associated success fee accruals.
- **Added**: •Net interest income increased $12 million, or 2%, in the third quarter of 2026, compared with the prior year period, primarily reflecting increased earning assets. Net interest margin declined to 3.23% from 3.28%, as the impact of lower interest rates on earning asset yields more than offset the benefit of lower funding costs.
- **Added**: ◦Average interest-earning assets increased $2.6 billion, or 3%, reflecting growth in average money market investments and loans and leases, partially offset by lower average investment securities. The increase in money market investments was driven by excess liquidity as deposit growth exceeded loan growth.
- **Added**: ◦Average interest-bearing liabilities decreased $141 million, or less than 1%, as lower average borrowed funds were largely offset by growth in interest-bearing deposits and long-term debt. Average noninterest-bearing deposits increased $1.3 billion, or 5%, primarily reflecting the migration of a consumer interest-bearing product into a new noninterest-bearing offering.
- **Added**: •The provision for credit losses was an $8 million benefit, compared with a $49 million provision expense in the prior year period, which reflected credit losses on revolving credit facilities extended to two related commercial borrowers that financed commercial mortgage originations and purchases.
- **Added**: •Customer-related noninterest income increased $29 million, or 18%, reflecting broad-based growth across nearly all revenue streams. Capital markets fees and income increased primarily due to changes in net credit valuation adjustment (“CVA”), while wealth management fees increased due to growth in assets under management and personal trust fees, and retail and business banking fees increased primarily due to higher overdraft service fees.
- **Added**: •Noncustomer-related noninterest income decreased $21 million, or 81%, primarily due to lower net securities gains. Compared with the second quarter of 2026, noncustomer-related noninterest income declined $273 million, primarily reflecting the aforementioned pre-tax net gains recognized in the preceding quarter.
- **Added**: •Noninterest expense increased $26 million, or 5%, primarily reflecting higher salaries and employee benefits, professional and legal services, technology-related costs, and marketing and business development expenses. The increase also included costs associated with the acquisition and integration of the Basis Multifamily Finance agency lending platform. These increases were partially offset by lower deposit insurance and regulatory expense. The efficiency ratio was 61.9%, compared with 59.6% in the prior year quarter, and improved from 62.2% in the preceding quarter.
- **Added**: ◦Net loan and lease charge-offs totaled $3 million, or 0.02% of average loans and leases annualized, compared with $56 million, or 0.37%, in the prior year quarter, including $50 million of charge-offs related to the credit losses discussed above.
- **Added**: ◦Credit quality improved compared with the prior year period. Nonperforming assets declined to $315 million, or 0.50% of total loans and leases and other real estate owned, from $324 million, or 0.54%. Classified loans declined to $2.1 billion, or 3.40% of total loans and leases, from $2.4 billion, or 4.00%, in the prior year period, primarily reflecting payoffs of classified CRE exposures.
