# Marsh & McLennan Companies, Inc. (MRSH) — Core initiation — 2026-10-09

## 1. Business
MRSH operates two fee/commission businesses: Risk and Insurance Services (~64% of revenue — Marsh insurance broking and Guy Carpenter reinsurance broking, paid via commissions, fees and contingent payments) and Consulting (~36% — Mercer health/wealth/career advisory and Oliver Wyman management consulting, paid via client fees and asset-based fees) (source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/62709/000006270926000022/mrsh-20251231.htm) [Certain].

## 2. Quality
A capital-light, fee-based franchise: TTM ROIC 14.5% and ROCE 17.9% (5y ROCE trend rising +1.3pp), operating margin 24.5% TTM (up from 24.0% FY2021), and FCF conversion 119.8% of net income, up from ~99% in FY2021-2022 (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/mrsh/financials/ratios/) [Certain]. Insurance/reinsurance broking is effectively an oligopoly (Marsh, Aon, WTW, Gallagher) where scale, carrier relationships and licensing requirements raise switching costs and entry barriers; the 10-K cites 130-country reach and ~95,000 colleagues, though it discloses no hard switching-cost data [Likely] (source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/62709/000006270926000022/mrsh-20251231.htm). The quality blemish is leverage: net debt/EBITDA rose from 2.07x (FY2021) to 2.67x TTM, partly funding bolt-on M&A, including the Accel Holdings acquisition completed 2026-10-02 [Certain] (source: https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20261002937423/en/Marsh-Completes-Acquisition-of-Accel-Holdings/, as listed on stockanalysis.com). ROE drifted down from 31.0% to 25.9% TTM, consistent with a more levered balance sheet rather than weaker unit economics [Likely].

## 3. Valuation
MRSH trades at the bottom of its own 5-year multiple range — P/E 21.6x vs a 5y range of 21.8x-27.9x (-4%, below the 5y min), EV/EBITDA 13.5x vs 14.0x-18.1x (-13%), EV/Sales 3.8x vs 4.1x-5.0x (-34%) and P/FCF 17.7x vs 18.2x-28.2x (-5%) — yet still at a premium to the peer median (AON, WTW, AJG, BRO) on P/E (+13%), EV/EBITDA (+12%) and P/FCF (+3%), with a discount only on EV/Sales (-11%) (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/mrsh/financials/ratios/) [Certain]. P/B is +84% above peers but is less comparable given differing buyback/leverage histories. The fact sheet's own-history fair value (P/E-led, EV/EBITDA second) gives base $211.93 (+20.0% vs the $176.67 close), bear $179.24 (+1.5%) and bull $221.09 (+25.1%) [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/mrsh/, fair value table derived from income statement, balance sheet, cash flow and ratios pages). The price assumes no multiple re-rating toward MRSH's own 5-year average despite rising margins and FCF conversion.

## 4. What would prove this wrong
- ROIC (ratios page) falls below 13% (vs 14.5% TTM), signalling the oligopoly's returns advantage is eroding.
- Net debt/EBITDA (ratios page) rises above 3.0x (vs 2.67x TTM), showing M&A-funded leverage overwhelming the balance sheet.
- FCF conversion, FCF/net income (cash-flow + income statement) falls below 90% (vs 119.8% TTM), indicating cash earnings quality is deteriorating.
- Operating margin (income statement) falls below 23% (vs 24.5% TTM / 26.5% FY2024 peak), indicating cost or pricing discipline is breaking down.

## 5. Capital allocation and cash conversion
FCF margin is rising (17.1% TTM vs 15.7% FY2021) and FCF conversion is strong at 119.8% of net income, a capital-light model needing little reinvestment [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/mrsh/financials/cash-flow-statement/). Shares outstanding fell -1.31% YoY, implying buybacks alongside the quarterly $0.990/share dividend declared 2026-09-16 [Certain] (source: https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260916638460/en/Marsh-Declares-Quarterly-Cash-Dividend/, as listed on stockanalysis.com). The firm is also funding bolt-on M&A (Accel Holdings, closed 2026-10-02), pushing net debt/EBITDA from 2.07x to 2.67x over five years — returning cash and acquiring at once, sustainable only if ROIC stays above the cost of debt [Likely].

## 6. Unusual items
None material. Short interest is low (1.23% of float, down 20.5% over the past month) and institutional ownership is high (94.3%), both consistent with a stable, widely-owned large-cap; Altman Z of 2.82 sits in the "grey zone" but the model is poorly suited to a fee-based services business with minimal fixed assets [Guessing].
