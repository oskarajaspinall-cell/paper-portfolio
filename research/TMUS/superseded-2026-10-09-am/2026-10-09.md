# T-Mobile US, Inc. (TMUS) — Core initiation — 2026-10-09

## 1. Business
TMUS is the second-largest US wireless carrier, monetizing a subscriber base through recurring postpaid/prepaid service plans (T-Mobile, Metro, Mint MVNO) plus equipment financing, fixed wireless access (home broadband) and growing enterprise/fiber businesses [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/tmus/).

## 2. Quality
Returns on capital have risen every year for five years: ROIC 5.4%→8.9% TTM (+3.2pp FY trend), ROCE 5.5%→10.7% TTM (+4.5pp), FCF margin to 20.0% TTM (+18.4pp) and FCF conversion (FCF/NI) to 174% TTM [Certain] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/tmus/financials/ratios/). Net debt/EBITDA has fallen to 3.42x from a 4.08x FY2022 peak [Certain]. The market already pays up for this versus incumbents VZ/T: TMUS trades at an EV/EBITDA and P/FCF premium to the 3-peer median (+24%/+12%) [Certain] (source: fact sheet valuation table). The moat rests on network scale and mid-band spectrum depth from the Sprint merger, which VZ/T have struggled to match on capacity. New variable: an Oct 8, 2026 headline reports a SpaceX spectrum-license deal hit AT&T/Verizon/T-Mobile shares [Likely] (source: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/08/spacex-spectrum-license-att-verizon-tmobile.html, as listed in the fact sheet). T-Mobile's CFO separately dismissed satellite-to-device competition as a threat [Guessing] (source: https://www.businessinsider.com/starlink-elon-musk-strategy-cellphones-no-threat-t-mobile-cfo-2026-9, as listed in the fact sheet). Monitor, not yet a fundamental change.

## 3. Valuation
TMUS trades at a premium to peers on every multiple (P/E +119%, EV/EBITDA +24%, EV/Sales +31%, P/FCF +12%, P/B +111% vs the VZ/T/CHTR median) [Certain], reflecting better returns on capital. Against its own history it looks cheap: P/E 18.0x is 6% below its 5-year minimum, EV/EBITDA 8.8x is 29% below minimum, P/FCF 10.0x is 3% below minimum; P/B (57th percentile) and EV/Sales (12th percentile) sit mid-range [Certain] (source: fact sheet valuation table). FCF yield is 10.0% [Certain]. DCF fair value (best method) is $275.91 base / $290.55 bull vs last close $171.31 (+61%/+70%); EV/EBITDA (2nd method) gives $220.17 base [Certain] (source: fact sheet fair value table). The bear case ($38.93, -77%) rests on a reverse-DCF signal of -6.1% year-1 revenue growth fading to terminal — unsupported by current margin/FCF trends, so weight it cautiously [Likely]. Net: priced for a slowdown the quality metrics don't yet show.

## 4. What would prove this wrong
- ROIC (ratios page) reverses its 5-year rising trend, falling back toward the FY2021 level of 5.4%
- FCF margin (cash flow/income statement) drops below ~15%, giving back over a quarter of the current 20.0% TTM level
- Net debt/EBITDA (ratios page) resumes rising back above its FY2022 peak of 4.08x
- Operating margin (income statement) contracts below 18% (from 22.1% TTM) as new entrants force pricing concessions

## 5. Capital allocation and cash conversion
H1 2026: $7.1bn of buybacks (34.75m shares), cutting shares outstanding -3.93% YoY, plus a dividend raised 15% on Sept 24, 2026 [Certain] (source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1283699/000128369926000101/tmus-20260630.htm; https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260924651626/en/T-Mobile-Announces-a-15-Quarterly-Dividend-Increase/, as listed in the fact sheet). Total debt ~$84.6bn against a net debt/EBITDA of 3.42x that is still falling [Certain]. Capital is also being redeployed into fiber JVs (i3 Broadband, ~$700m for 50%, closing H2 2026; GoNetspeed/Greenlight, ~$2.0bn for 50%, closing H1 2027) [Certain] (source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1283699/000128369926000101/tmus-20260630.htm), extending the moat into broadband while FCF conversion (174% TTM) funds both buybacks and growth.

## 6. Unusual items
Altman Z-score of 1.80 sits in the distress zone of the classic model, but the model is a poor fit for a stable, high-FCF-conversion telecom with contracted recurring revenue; Piotroski F is a respectable 5/9 [Likely] (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/tmus/statistics/). Beta of 0.32 is unusually low for a ~3.4x levered carrier [Certain] (source: same). Short interest is low (3.72% of float, +5.7% month-on-month) and not a crowding risk [Certain]. None of these are decision-relevant on their own.
