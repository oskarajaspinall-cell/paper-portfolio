# Macro overlay: T-Mobile US, Inc. (TMUS) — 2026-10-09
## Assessment
TMUS's subscriber revenue is largely macro-insensitive (recurring, contracted) [Likely], but its 3.42x net debt/EBITDA leverage and its own DCF, built on the risk-free rate, make the discount rate the relevant channel, not earnings: real yields/fed policy -> cost of debt (refinancing) and equity discount rate -> DCF fair value and bond-proxy dividend/multiple appeal.

Facts: 10y real yield (DFII10) 2.92% (2026-10-07), up 0.61pp/3m; 10y nominal (DGS10) 5.28%, up 0.72pp; effective fed funds (DFF) 3.88%, up 0.26pp — a rise, not a cut (fred.stlouisfed.org, series DFII10/DGS10/DFF, 2026-10-07). Market pricing (proxy, **inference**): the 2y yield (4.77%, DGS2) sits 0.89pp above fed funds, implying the market prices further tightening, not cuts. Breakevens (T10YIE 2.35%) are up only 0.12pp/3m, so this reads as a term-premium/policy-path repricing more than an inflation scare. The fact sheet's fair value already uses today's 5.28% risk-free rate, so the current level is priced in; a *further* rise is the unpriced risk.

Bull: real yields roll over -> discount rate falls -> DCF fair value (base $275.91, +61% vs close) re-rates toward it, bond-proxy demand returns. Bear: core CPI stays sticky and real yields push further above 3% -> discount rate and refinancing cost on 3.42x leverage both rise, compressing fair value, adding to the existing -24.8% 12-month drawdown. Tilt: moderate toward bear — leverage and bond-proxy status leave TMUS unusually exposed to a continued hawkish repricing, reinforcing (not creating) the sell-off already underway on sector-specific spectrum news.
