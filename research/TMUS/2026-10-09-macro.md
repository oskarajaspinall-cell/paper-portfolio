# Macro overlay: T-Mobile US, Inc. (TMUS) — 2026-10-09
## Assessment
TMUS is a levered (net debt/EBITDA 3.42x, ~$84.6bn debt), high-FCF, dividend/buyback compounder whose fair value uses a CAPM discount rate anchored to the 10y Treasury [Certain] (research/TMUS/factsheet-2026-10-09.md). Rates have moved materially, not just drifted: 10y real yield (DFII10) 2.92% (2026-10-07, FRED), up +0.61pp/3m, +0.49pp/1m; nominal 10y (DGS10) 5.28%, up +0.72pp/3m; fed funds (DFF) 3.88%, up +0.26pp/3m; HY credit spread (BAMLH0A0HYM2) 3.09%, up +0.39pp/3m (https://fred.stlouisfed.org/graph/fredgraph.csv?id=..., dates above) [Certain].

Chain: real yields -> CAPM discount rate -> DCF/EV-EBITDA fair value and multiple; secondarily, rates/spreads -> refinancing cost on existing leverage -> margin at the edge.

Market pricing (proxy, **inference**): the 2y yield (4.77%) sits ~0.9pp above fed funds, implying the market still prices further tightening, not cuts, over two years; stable breakevens (T10YIE 2.35%, -0.02pp/1m) mean this is a real-rate/term-premium move, not an inflation surprise [Likely]. Base fair value ($275.91) already uses today's 5.28% risk-free rate, so further rate moves, not the current level, are what shift the multiple from here.

**Bull**: yields stabilize/retreat -> discount rate eases -> the fair-value gap (+61% base) closes faster via re-rating. **Bear**: yields extend their rise -> discount rate climbs further -> fair value compresses and the re-rating stalls; wider spreads nudge up refinancing cost on debt rollover. **Tilt**: toward bear, moderate — the 3-month rate move already helps explain 5-year-low multiples despite rising ROIC/FCF, and curve pricing implies more tightening ahead.
