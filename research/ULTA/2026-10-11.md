# Ulta Beauty, Inc. (ULTA) — Core initiation — 2026-10-11

## 1. Business
Ulta operates 1,500+ specialty beauty stores (plus 84 Space NK stores in the UK/Ireland) selling 600+ prestige and mass cosmetics, skincare, haircare and fragrance brands, with in-store salons, e-commerce and a loyalty program (Ultamate Rewards, ~47mn members, ~95% of US sales) driving repeat traffic; top-10 brand partners are ~51% of sales [Certain] (source: SEC 10-K, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1403568/000110465926035243/ulta-20260131x10k.htm).

## 2. Quality
The loyalty base and omnichannel integration (omnichannel members spend ~3x store-only shoppers, per the 10-K) are a real moat versus mass or pure online rivals [Certain]. But returns are trending down: ROIC fell from 34.0-40.8% in FY2022-24 to 25.4% TTM, ROCE from 40.5-44.4% to 34.5% TTM, operating margin from 16.1% (FY2023) to 12.5% TTM (fact sheet, ratios/income statement). Management attributes this to a "more promotional" environment and guided gross margin only "roughly flat" for FY2026 [Likely] (source: Q2 FY2027 transcript, https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/ulta/transcripts/684477-q2-2027/). The 10-K flags mass merchandisers, pure-play e-commerce and salon chains as rivals with "greater financial, marketing, and other resources" [Certain]. FCF conversion is strong (95.0% TTM) and net debt/EBITDA low (1.19x) — the balance sheet is fine; the issue is margin erosion as competition intensifies. Still a good business, but the moat is being tested, not strengthening.

## 3. Valuation
P/E of 20.8x sits mid-range of Ulta's own 5-year history (54th percentile) and looks cheap versus the peer median (59.0x, -65%), but on EV/EBITDA (+15%), EV/Sales (+9%), P/FCF (+45%) and P/B (+68%) ULTA trades at a premium to the peer group — a more consistent read given the P/E peer median is likely skewed by a depressed-earnings peer. More tellingly, the last close of 570.37 is 22.4% ABOVE the fact sheet's own base-case DCF fair value of 442.43, and only 5.8% below the bull case (603.46) — the market is pricing close to the best plausible outcome. The reverse DCF embedded in the price implies 13.9% revenue growth in year 1 fading to terminal, versus the ~8.9% growth and 9-12% H2 EPS growth guidance management reported last quarter [Certain] (fact sheet; transcript). There is little margin of safety if growth or margin trends disappoint.

## 4. What would prove this wrong
- ROIC (ratios page) falls below 20% (TTM 25.4%, already down 5.8pp over 5 years)
- Operating margin (income statement) falls below 11% (TTM 12.5%, down from 16.1% peak)
- FCF margin (cash flow/income statement) falls below 7% (TTM 8.9%)
- Net debt/EBITDA (ratios page) rises above 2.0x (TTM 1.19x)

## 5. Capital allocation and cash conversion
FCF conversion is strong at 95.0% TTM (FCF margin 8.9%) and net debt/EBITDA is modest at 1.19x, leaving room for both buybacks and store growth. Shares outstanding fell 4.22% YoY (statistics page) [Certain]. Management raised FY2026 buyback guidance to $1.8bn, partly funded via the revolver, with $1bn of authorization remaining [Likely] (source: Q2 FY2027 transcript). The 10-K targets long-term expansion to 1,800+ freestanding US stores (from 1,500+ today) at ~$2.4mn per new store, funded from internally generated cash [Certain] (source: 10-K).

## 6. Unusual items
Short interest is 5.74% of float, up 9.0% month-over-month — a modest increase, not a distress signal [Certain] (fact sheet, statistics page). No governance, accounting or concentration red flags beyond the vendor-concentration point already noted in Quality (top 10 vendors ~51% of sales). Otherwise none.
