# ConocoPhillips (COP) — Core initiation — 2026-11-16
## 1. Business
Independent oil and gas explorer and producer. Revenue comes from selling crude, bitumen, natural gas and LNG, with Lower 48 shale the largest segment (1,517 MBOED of 2,302 total in Q3 2026 [company-reported], https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1163165/000116316526000045/cop-20260930.htm). Earnings follow realised oil prices. Norway plus Teesside were about five percent of 2025 production and Qatar about four percent [company-reported] (same filing). No foreign country is shown at ~10%+. [Likely]

## 2. Quality
Good but cyclical, and the moat is cost position and scale, not pricing power. [Likely]
- ROIC fell from 31.3% (FY2022) to 9.6% (FY2025), then recovered to 14.6% TTM. Operating margin is 25.9% TTM versus 28.0% in FY2021 (source: https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/cop/financials/ratios/). [Certain]
- The latest 12 months improved (ROIC +3.2pp, FCF margin 11.6% to 16.1%). The filing attributes this to higher realised prices from Middle East supply disruption, not to structural change. [Certain] That improvement is therefore price-led and reverses if oil falls. [Likely]
- Net debt/EBITDA is 0.45x, Piotroski F 6 and Altman Z 3.28, so the balance sheet is strong. [Certain]
- Management targets over $1bn of run-rate cost and margin gains by year-end 2026 [company-reported] (filing above). [Likely]

## 3. Valuation
The price assumes elevated oil prices persist. [Likely]
- P/E 15.0x and EV/EBITDA 6.1x are both above the 5y maximum (14.5x and 6.0x), and EV/Sales is above its range too. [Certain]
- Against peers it is -6% on EV/EBITDA but +14% on P/E and +28% on P/FCF. [Certain]
- FCF yield is 6.61%. The price is 15.4% above the 200-day average, with a 12-month gain of +59.7%. [Certain]
- Mechanical fair value (EV/EBITDA only, because the reserve NAV is [data unavailable]) is 123.89 base and 130.02 bull, against a 142.28 close. The price is 8.6% above even the bull case. [Certain]
- Read: a good business that is working, but not cheap on its own history. Upside needs sustained high crude. [Likely]

## 4. What would prove this wrong
- ROIC (ratios page, TTM) falls below 10%.
- FCF margin (rolling 12-month cash flow and income statement pages) falls below 11%.
- Net debt/EBITDA (statistics page) rises above 1.5x.
- Operating margin (income statement page, TTM) falls below 21%.

## 5. Capital allocation and cash conversion
Q3 operating cash flow was $7.2bn and shareholder returns $3.4bn, of which $2.4bn were buybacks and $1.0bn the dividend; the dividend was raised 11% [company-reported] (https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1163165/000116316526000045/cop-20260930.htm). [Certain] The share count fell 2.43% YoY. FCF conversion is 96.2% TTM, but its 5y trend is down 53.8pp. The company met its disposition target and agreed to buy a stake in a Kirkuk, Iraq joint venture from BP, expected to close by end-2026 [company-reported] (https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1163165/000116316526000045/cop-20260930.htm). [Certain] Buying in Iraq adds geopolitical exposure. [Likely]

## 6. Unusual items

- Portfolio changes: a possible sale of Norway and Teesside after an unsolicited offer; the company says it keeps the assets if the price is not met [company-reported] (same filing). [Certain]
- Q3 beat and dividend increase (source: https://www.reuters.com/business/energy/conocophillips-beats-profit-estimates-boosts-quarterly-dividend-2026-11-05/). [Likely]
